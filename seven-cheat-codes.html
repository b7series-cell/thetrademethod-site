Why most traders fail

The two biggest reasons are human emotions and losing too often or too big. You can have a few good green days, and one big red day takes the whole week's profit. In the video he says he lost over $30,000 in his first years and nearly quit. The seven rules below are what he says turned it around, and they're meant to be learned in order, because each one builds on the last.

Cheat code 1: a strategy with a proven edge

You can work on psychology and mindset all you want. You won't be profitable until you have a strategy that works. These are the three scalping setups he trades every day, and none of them is complicated.

Three setups, all retests

1

The three setups

  • Pre-market high and low. Mark the high and low from 4:00 to 9:30 a.m. Eastern. Wait for a break, don't take the breakout itself, and enter on the retest: old support turned resistance (or the other way round), with a weak or strong candle at the level.
  • The one candle rule. In an uptrend, down-close candles should support price. Mark the last down-close candle. When price comes back to it and holds, buyers step in: long, looking for 2R or the high of the day. In a downtrend, the last up-close candles do the same job from above.
  • The 5-minute opening range. Mark the high and low of the first five minutes after the open. Wait for a break, then the retest, and enter on strong price action for the continuation.

All three have the same shape: a level, a break, and an entry on the retest instead of the break. That's what gives you a small stop and a bigger target.

Simplicity is what pays

Whatever you trade can work: ICT, smart money concepts, supply and demand, price action. The thing that sinks beginners is making it complicated. He says the only times he saw results were when he simplified.

the trapChasing the break

The breakout candle is where a lot of retail traders get faked out. You'd be buying the top of a move with a wide stop.

the edgeWaiting for the retest

The pullback into the level is the low-risk, high-reward entry. If it never comes back, there's no trade.

Cheat code 2: master market structure

Being a scalper or a day trader doesn't mean you can ignore the trend. You're looking for quick moves and to be done early, but you still need to know what condition the market is in. And it only moves three ways.

Up, down or sideways

Test yourself: which trade is higher probability?

Both are the same setup, the one candle rule. In each, the down-close candle is marked (gold), price broke above it, and now it's coming back for the retest. Pick one, then watch what happened.

2

Same setup, different structure

  • Uptrend: a low, then higher lows. Every low is higher than the last. Look for price to continue up.
  • Downtrend: a high, then lower highs. Look for continuation down, and yes, you can make money with the stock going down.
  • Range: equal highs and equal lows, no clear direction. He trades the trends and avoids the range, because that's where traders using these setups blow their accounts.

You can trade the same strategy, but without understanding how the stock is moving, you'll keep losing with it.

Cheat code 3: combine time frames to enter

Even when scalping, he combines time frames for the entry. The higher time frame gives the direction. The lower time frame is just the place to get the best risk to reward. Here's his live example, redrawn on made-up bars.

Daily for direction, 1-minute for the entry

3

The order he does it in

  • Daily first. Read the structure: here, a high and a higher low, then a break below that higher low and lower highs. The stock is bearish.
  • Then the plan on the 1-minute. Let the first five minutes play out and mark the 5-minute high and low. Because the daily is bearish, only the short side counts.
  • Use whichever setup shows up. No retest of the 5-minute low? Then the next setup: in a downtrend, the last up-close candles should hold price for a move down.
  • Enter on the retest. Weak price action at those candles is the short. The stop is a break back above the candle you entered on. The target is the next whole number.

The only reason to be confident in a trade like this is that the time frames line up. If the higher time frame is bearish, the lower time frame is just where you get the best entry.

Cheat code 4: 90% of scalps should work right away

If you've done the first three steps correctly, most of your trades should be working within minutes of the entry. So five minutes in, you already know: it's working, or the thesis is wrong and you get out.

Five minutes tells you

4

Don't sit in consolidation

  • A loser: a break and retest of the pre-market high. Within five minutes he knew the thesis was wrong and got out, a $1,650 loss in the video. Staying would have meant sitting in it for at least another hour for it to not work out.
  • A winner: a previous-day low retest. Sellers stepped in, he entered on weak price action, and within a couple of minutes it was back at the low of the day, then gave a 5-minute low retest for more. About a $12,000 trade.

This works for scalps because the setups are built on a level and a quick reaction. A trade that needs an hour to start working usually isn't the trade you planned.

The five-minute check

workingMoving your way, fast

Price leaves the level right away. Hold it and manage it to the target.

not workingSitting at the level

Chop, no follow-through. The thesis is wrong. Take the small loss and move on.

Cheat code 5: start small and scale up slowly

He says he was taking the exact same trades four years ago for about $100 a day as he takes now for about $10,000 a day. The setups didn't change. The size did, and the size only went up because the experience did.

Same trade, any size

The chart doesn't care how many shares you have. Pick how much you're willing to lose and the shares follow. Start with the code 3 example.

The money comes later

  • In the beginning, the job is controlling your emotions and perfecting your execution. If that's right, the money comes in the long run.
  • Trading isn't a get-rich-quick system. Raise the size slowly, over a long period, as the experience builds.
  • He shows a $78,000 week in the video and says it only happened because he started small and increased from there. He also has a separate video on starting with as little as $10.

Small size makes the first four codes cheap to learn. A mistake at $10 of risk is a lesson. The same mistake at full size is a red week.

Cheat code 6: only trade your A+ conditions

Out of a month of trading days, he says maybe 10 to 15 (and that's high) are A+ conditions. The rest are B+ or B− days. It's fine not to trade every day: on the good days the trades are easier to execute, easier to hold and easier to follow. On choppy days you keep losing.

The choppy day vs the trend day

6

Your edge is built for certain conditions

  • On the choppy day, the 5-minute high retest kept coming back, so it kept getting taken: −$500, −$1,200, then +$600.
  • That last win feels like you did the right thing by taking it. You didn't. The day is still −$1,100.
  • On the trend day, the same trader takes two trades, the pre-market high retest (+$700) and the one candle rule at 2R (+$400), and wins both.

Unlike what most people think, it's better not to trade every day and to trade the good, consistent days.

How to tell the days apart

B day · sit outEvery break rejects

Tries at the high fail, tries at the low fail, and price keeps coming back to the same level. A lot of range.

A+ day · tradeMove, retest, move

A clear trend: a move, a retest, another move. The retests are the setups.

Read the daily

  • Higher lows: bullish, look for longs. Lower highs, or a broken higher low: bearish, look for shorts.

Mark your levels

  • The pre-market high and low (4:00 to 9:30 a.m. Eastern).
  • The 5-minute high and low, once the first five minutes are done.

4

Enter on the retest

Watch the clock, then stop

Questions

What scalping setups does he trade?

Three, all retests: the premarket high and low from 4:00 to 9:30 a.m. Eastern, the one candle rule (the last down-close candle in an uptrend), and the 5-minute opening range.

Why enter on the retest instead of the break?

The breakout candle is where many traders get faked out, buying the top with a wide stop. The pullback into the level gives a small stop and a bigger target.

How fast should a scalp work?

Within about 5 minutes. If it doesn't, get out.

Why do most traders fail?

Emotions, and losing too often or too big. One big red day can take a week of profit.

Pick one code you're breaking.

Look at your last ten losing trades. How many were against the daily trend, in a range, on a choppy day, chased on the break instead of the retest, or held long after five minutes of nothing? Most traders find the same one or two codes behind most of the losses. Fix that one first.

  • Trade a setup with a proven edge, and keep it simple.
  • Trade with the structure: higher lows or lower highs, not a range.
  • Daily for the direction, 1-minute for the entry.
  • Enter on the retest, not the break.
  • Not working in five minutes? Get out.
  • Small size first. Scale up with experience.
  • A+ days only. Practice every day.
