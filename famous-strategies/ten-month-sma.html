10-Month Moving Average

The worst stretches for stocks have tended to happen when prices were already below their long-term trend. Check once a month: if the price closed above its 10-month average, stay in. If it closed below, step aside into T-bills until it's back above. One check a month.

What is the 10-month moving average strategy?

Mebane Faber's 2007 timing rule: on the last trading day of each month, if the price closed above its 10-month simple moving average, own the asset for the next month. If it closed below, sell and hold 90-day T-bills.

The whole rule

Faber wanted a rule simple enough to follow without judgment. It uses one number per month and one comparison, and the same rule works on any asset that has a price.

Above or below

1

The rule

  • On the last trading day of each month, compare the closing price with the average of the last ten month-end closes.
  • If the close is above the average, own the asset for the next month. If it's below, sell and hold 90-day T-bills.
  • Do nothing else until the next month-end. For several assets, run the rule on each one separately.

It's trend following at its simplest: stay with the market while it trends up, and step aside when a decline has gone on long enough to drag the price under a year of trend.

What it's for

The rule never sells the top or buys the bottom. Its job is to stay out of the long middle of a bear market, which is where most of the damage happens.

A bear market, timed

2

Late out, late in

  • It exits after the decline has started, so it always takes part of the loss.
  • It returns after the rebound has started, so it always misses part of the gain.
  • The payoff comes when the decline is long and deep: missing the middle of it is worth more than the two late switches cost.

That's why the return ends up close to holding while the drawdown is much smaller. The rule trades a little return in good times for a lot of protection in bad ones.

Equity-like returns with bond-like volatility. Faber's own summary of the five-asset version: about the same return as the buy-and-hold mix, with drawdowns closer to a bond fund's.

The price of it

No timing rule is free. This one pays in sideways markets, where the price keeps crossing its average and every cross is a trade.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Wait

  • Don't check during the month.
  • Only the month-end close counts.

Fewer checks, fewer false signals.

2

Check

  • On the last trading day, compare the close with its 10-month simple moving average.
  • Use the same rule on every asset you hold.

One comparison per asset.

3

Hold

  • Closed above: own it for the next month.
  • Already own it? Do nothing.

Stay with the trend.

4

Cash

  • Closed below: sell, and hold 90-day T-bills.
  • Buy back only after a month-end close above.

Step aside for the long declines.

5

Repeat

  • Five assets: equal weights, each with its own signal.
  • Rebalance monthly.

Some assets are always in; the mix is rarely all cash.

We tested it Smaller drops, trails holding

Cut the drops, and the gains with them

On SPY alone it made about half of buy and hold. Across five assets it halved the worst drop but trailed holding them.

  • +9.5%a year, SPY timed
  • +17.6%SPY held
  • +6.8%a year, 5 assets timed
  • +10.0%5 assets held
  • Worst drop across the five assets: −3.6% timed against −7.7% held.
  • Timing rules earn their keep in long bear markets, and these five years didn't have one.

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It plots the 10-month average on any chart, shades invested and cash months, marks each switch, and shows the signal for five ETFs that stand in for Faber's five assets. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • 10-Month SMA Timing

    alerts

    On the chart: the 10-month simple moving average, green shading while the last month-end close was above it and red while it was below, and an IN or CASH label at each switch. The signal uses only completed months, so it never changes in the middle of a month.

    A table with how far the chart's last monthly close is above or below its average, and the same for five ETFs: SPY, EFA, IEF, VNQ and DBC for US stocks, foreign stocks, bonds, real estate and commodities, with the share of the five-asset mix that would be invested.

    Faber tested the indexes themselves, with history back to 1901 and 1973. The ETFs are a stand-in you can trade, and every symbol is a setting.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.
  3. Any timeframe works. A daily or weekly chart of SPY shows the switches clearly.
  4. To get alerted, create an alert on the chart and pick 10M SMA: buy or 10M SMA: to cash.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// 10-Month Moving Average Timing — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/ten-month-sma.html
//
// Mebane Faber, A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation
// (2007, updated 2013). Once a month, at the month-end close:
//
//   Close above its 10-month simple moving average  ->  hold it
//   Close below it                                  ->  move to cash (T-bills)
//
// Each asset is timed on its own. The five-asset version spreads the
// money equally over US stocks, foreign stocks, bonds, real estate and
// commodities, each with its own signal (the table on the right).
//
// Works on any timeframe: the signal is always the monthly one.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("10-Month SMA Timing", shorttitle = "10M SMA", overlay = true)

len   = input.int(10, "Moving average length (months)", minval = 2)
show5 = input.bool(true, "Show the five-asset table")
grp = "Five assets"
s1 = input.symbol("AMEX:SPY", "US stocks", group = grp)
s2 = input.symbol("AMEX:EFA", "Foreign stocks", group = grp)
s3 = input.symbol("NASDAQ:IEF", "Bonds (10-year Treasuries)", group = grp)
s4 = input.symbol("AMEX:VNQ", "Real estate", group = grp)
s5 = input.symbol("AMEX:DBC", "Commodities", group = grp)

// the last completed month only: no look-ahead, no mid-month flips
mClose = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "M", close[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
mSma   = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "M", ta.sma(close, len)[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
liveSma = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "M", ta.sma(close, len), lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_off)

invested = mClose > mSma
buySig  = invested and not invested[1] and not na(mSma[1])
sellSig = not invested and invested[1] and not na(mSma[1])

plot(timeframe.ismonthly ? ta.sma(close, len) : liveSma, "10-month SMA", color = color.new(#2997ff, 0), linewidth = 2)
bgcolor(na(mSma) ? na : invested ? color.new(#30d158, 92) : color.new(#ff453a, 92), title = "Invested / cash")
plotshape(buySig, "Buy: month closed above", shape.labelup, location.belowbar, color.new(#30d158, 10), text = "IN", textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)
plotshape(sellSig, "Sell: month closed below", shape.labeldown, location.abovebar, color.new(#ff453a, 10), text = "CASH", textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)

alertcondition(buySig, "10M SMA: buy", "{{ticker}}: the month closed above its 10-month average. Hold it.")
alertcondition(sellSig, "10M SMA: to cash", "{{ticker}}: the month closed below its 10-month average. Move to cash.")

sig(sym) =>
    c = request.security(sym, "M", close[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    m = request.security(sym, "M", ta.sma(close, len)[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    [c, m]
[c1, m1] = sig(s1)
[c2, m2] = sig(s2)
[c3, m3] = sig(s3)
[c4, m4] = sig(s4)
[c5, m5] = sig(s5)

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 3, 8, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
cellRow(int r, string name, float c, float m) =>
    ok = not na(c) and not na(m)
    up = ok and c > m
    table.cell(tb, 0, r, name, text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, r, ok ? str.tostring((c / m - 1) * 100, "+#.0;-#.0") + "%" : "n/a", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, r, not ok ? "?" : up ? "hold" : "cash", text_color = not ok ? color.new(#f4b942, 0) : up ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : color.new(#ff453a, 0), text_size = size.small)
    ok and up ? 1 : 0
if barstate.islast
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "Last month's close", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, "vs 10M SMA", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 0, "", text_size = size.small)
    cellRow(1, syminfo.ticker + " (this chart)", mClose, mSma)
    if show5
        n = cellRow(2, "US stocks", c1, m1) + cellRow(3, "Foreign stocks", c2, m2) + cellRow(4, "Bonds", c3, m3) + cellRow(5, "Real estate", c4, m4) + cellRow(6, "Commodities", c5, m5)
        table.cell(tb, 0, 7, "Five-asset mix", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
        table.cell(tb, 1, 7, str.tostring(n * 20) + "% invested", text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
        table.cell(tb, 2, 7, str.tostring(100 - n * 20) + "% cash", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)

Questions

Why does it help?

The worst stretches for stocks have tended to happen when prices were already below their long-term trend, so stepping aside then cuts the deepest drops.

What did Faber's test show?

On the S&P 500 from 1901 to 2012, 10.2% a year timed against 9.3% held, with the worst drawdown cut from 83.5% to 50.3%, before costs and taxes.

What is the downside?

Whipsaws and lag. It sells after a decline has started and buys back after a rebound, so it trails in choppy markets.

Does the 10-month moving average work?

It cut the drops and the gains with them in our test: +9.5% a year on SPY timed against +17.6% held, over five years with no long bear market.

Try it on paper first.

Put a reminder on the last trading day of each month. Write down SPY's close, its 10-month average and what the rule says to hold. Do it for a year, and count how many switches it made and what each one cost.

  • Check only at month-end.
  • Write the signal down before the next month starts.
  • Count the switches that reversed within two months.
