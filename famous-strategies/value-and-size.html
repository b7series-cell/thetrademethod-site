Value and Size

Do cheap stocks and small stocks earn more? Fama and French found that two easy numbers, market value and book-to-market, lined up with average returns far better than beta did. Tilt toward small and cheap, rebalance once a year, and wait. Two numbers, not one.

What is the value and size effect?

Fama and French's 1992 paper found two easy numbers lined up with stock returns far better than beta: size (small stocks earned more) and book-to-market (cheap stocks earned more). The strategy tilts toward small and cheap stocks and rebalances once a year.

The paper that sank beta

For decades the textbook answer was that a stock's beta, how much it moves with the market, sets its expected return. Fama and French tested it on almost three decades of US stocks, and it didn't hold up.

Flat, not rising

1

What the paper found

  • From 1963 to 1990, the relation between beta and average return was flat, even when beta was the only thing tested.
  • Two simple numbers did line up with returns: size (smaller stocks earned more) and book-to-market (stocks priced cheaply against their book value earned more).
  • Together, size and book-to-market absorbed the effects of leverage and earnings-to-price that earlier studies had found.

It didn't say risk doesn't matter. It said beta, measured the usual way, wasn't the risk the market was paying for.

From the abstract: "Two easily measured variables, size and book-to-market equity, combine to capture the cross-sectional variation in average stock returns associated with market β, size, leverage, book-to-market equity, and earnings-price ratios."

The two sorts

The method is simple enough to copy: rank every stock on one number, split them into ten groups, and see what each group earned over the next year.

Small beat big, cheap beat expensive

2

How the portfolios were built

  • Each June, measure size as market value at the end of June, and book-to-market from the previous fiscal year's book equity and December's market value.
  • Use NYSE stocks to set the cut-offs, so the many tiny NASDAQ stocks don't fill every group, then hold each group from July to the next June.
  • Leave out financial companies, whose high leverage means something different from an industrial company's.

The six-month gap between the fiscal year end and July makes sure the accounting numbers were public before the portfolio is formed.

What "value" means here

Book-to-market is the paper's measure of cheap.

high book-to-marketValue

The market prices the company low against the equity on its books. Often out-of-favor, slow-growing or troubled companies.

low book-to-marketGrowth

The market pays many times book value, expecting growth the accounts don't show yet.

Six boxes and two factors

The 1993 follow-up turned the two effects into factors anyone can measure, and they became the standard yardstick for judging funds and new strategies.

SMB and HML

market

The market

Stocks minus T-bills. What every stock portfolio earns for owning stocks at all.

SMB

Small minus big

The extra return of small stocks over big ones, with value and growth balanced out.

HML

High minus low

The extra return of high book-to-market (value) over low (growth), with size balanced out.

how it's used

The yardstick

If a fund's returns are explained by these three, its manager added nothing you couldn't buy cheaply.

After the paper

Like most famous anomalies, both effects got weaker once they were famous. How much weaker is still argued about, by the same two authors among others.

Smaller, and noisy

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Measure

  • Size: price times shares outstanding.
  • Book-to-market: book equity divided by market value.

Two numbers from any annual report.

2

Sort

  • Small or big, split at the median (the paper uses NYSE stocks for the cut-off).
  • Growth, neutral or value, split at the 30th and 70th percentiles.

Six boxes, as in the 1993 paper.

3

Tilt

  • Put more money toward small and value, or hold a fund that does.
  • Small value is where both effects stack.

It's a tilt, not a stock pick: you own lots of names.

4

Rebalance

  • Re-sort once a year. The papers form portfolios each June.
  • Trade as little as possible in between.

The premiums are small. Turnover costs can eat them.

5

Wait

  • Value has had long stretches of losing to growth, including much of the 2010s.
  • Decide in advance how long you'll give it.

The premium, if it's there, shows up over decades, not months.

We tested it Value positive, size flat

Value worked, small caps didn't

Cheap stocks beat expensive ones by about 6% a year over the five years. Small stocks barely beat big ones.

  • +6.2%a year, value minus growth
  • +1.6%a year, small minus big

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It reads TradingView's fundamental data to compute book-to-market and market value for the chart's stock and up to 11 others, sorts the list into the paper's six boxes, and shows which box the chart's stock is in. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • Value and Size Box

    alerts

    A pane under your chart with the stock's book-to-market (gold) against your list's value line (its 70th percentile, green) and growth line (its 30th percentile, red). The background turns green while the stock sits in the small-value box.

    A table shows the six boxes, small and big against growth, neutral and value, with how many of your stocks fall in each and a dot on the chart's stock, plus its book-to-market and market value.

    It sorts the list you give it, not the whole market, so "small" means small within your list. Book value is the latest quarter. Leave banks and insurers out, as the paper did.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Value and Size Box — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/value-and-size.html
//
// Fama and French (1992, 1993): two easy numbers, size (market value)
// and book-to-market (book value of equity / market value), captured
// most of the differences in average stock returns. Small stocks and
// high book-to-market ("value") stocks earned more on average.
//
// A chart can't sort the whole market, so this sorts a list you give it
// (the chart's stock plus up to 11 others) the way the three-factor model
// does: small or big (split at the median size), and growth, neutral or
// value (split at the 30th and 70th percentiles of book-to-market). It
// shows which of the six boxes the chart's stock lands in.
// Book value is the latest quarter; marks only, it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Value and Size Box", shorttitle = "Value/Size", overlay = false)

grpL = "The list to sort"
s1  = input.symbol("NYSE:XOM", "1", group = grpL)
s2  = input.symbol("NASDAQ:AAPL", "2", group = grpL)
s3  = input.symbol("NYSE:F", "3", group = grpL)
s4  = input.symbol("NASDAQ:NVDA", "4", group = grpL)
s5  = input.symbol("NYSE:PFE", "5", group = grpL)
s6  = input.symbol("NASDAQ:COST", "6", group = grpL)
s7  = input.symbol("NYSE:VZ", "7", group = grpL)
s8  = input.symbol("NYSE:NKE", "8", group = grpL)
s9  = input.symbol("NYSE:MOS", "9", group = grpL)
s10 = input.symbol("NASDAQ:ETSY", "10", group = grpL)
s11 = input.symbol("NYSE:CMC", "11", group = grpL)
showTbl = input.bool(true, "Show the table")

bm(string sym) =>
    bvps = request.financial(sym, "BOOK_VALUE_PER_SHARE", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    sh   = request.financial(sym, "TOTAL_SHARES_OUTSTANDING", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    px   = request.security(sym, "D", close, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    b = not na(bvps) and px > 0 ? bvps / px : na
    m = not na(sh) and not na(px) ? sh * px : na
    [b, m]

syms = array.from(syminfo.tickerid, s1, s2, s3, s4, s5, s6, s7, s8, s9, s10, s11)
n = array.size(syms)
bms  = array.new<float>(n, na)
caps = array.new<float>(n, na)
for i = 0 to n - 1
    [b, m] = bm(array.get(syms, i))
    array.set(bms, i, b)
    array.set(caps, i, m)

// breakpoints across the list (ignoring missing data)
pctl(array<float> a, float p) =>
    c = array.new<float>()
    for v in a
        if not na(v)
            array.push(c, v)
    array.size(c) > 0 ? array.percentile_linear_interpolation(c, p) : na

capMed = pctl(caps, 50)
bm30 = pctl(bms, 30)
bm70 = pctl(bms, 70)

sizeOf(float m) => na(m) or na(capMed) ? "n/a" : m <= capMed ? "Small" : "Big"
styleOf(float b) => na(b) or na(bm30) ? "n/a" : b >= bm70 ? "Value" : b <= bm30 ? "Growth" : "Neutral"

myBM  = array.get(bms, 0)
myCap = array.get(caps, 0)
mySize  = sizeOf(myCap)
myStyle = styleOf(myBM)
isSV = mySize == "Small" and myStyle == "Value"

plot(myBM, "Book-to-market", color = #f4b942, linewidth = 2, style = plot.style_stepline)
plot(bm70, "Value line (70th pct of list)", color = color.new(#30d158, 30), linewidth = 1)
plot(bm30, "Growth line (30th pct of list)", color = color.new(#ff453a, 30), linewidth = 1)
bgcolor(isSV ? color.new(#30d158, 90) : na, title = "Small value")
alertcondition(isSV and not isSV[1], "Moved into small value", "{{ticker}} is now in the small-value box of your list")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 4, 4, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if showTbl and barstate.islast
    lc = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    vc = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    hl = color.new(#5b4a1f, 0)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, syminfo.ticker, text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, "Growth", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 0, "Neutral", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 3, 0, "Value", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    rows = array.from("Small", "Big")
    cols = array.from("Growth", "Neutral", "Value")
    for r = 0 to 1
        table.cell(tb, 0, r + 1, array.get(rows, r), text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
        for c = 0 to 2
            cnt = 0
            for i = 0 to n - 1
                if sizeOf(array.get(caps, i)) == array.get(rows, r) and styleOf(array.get(bms, i)) == array.get(cols, c)
                    cnt += 1
            me = mySize == array.get(rows, r) and myStyle == array.get(cols, c)
            table.cell(tb, c + 1, r + 1, (me ? "● " : "") + str.tostring(cnt), text_color = vc, bgcolor = me ? hl : color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "B/M", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, na(myBM) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(myBM, "#.00"), text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 3, "Size", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 3, 3, na(myCap) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(myCap / 1e9, "#.0") + "B", text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)

Questions

What is book-to-market?

A company's book value of equity divided by its market value. A high number means a stock is priced cheaply against its book value.

How big was the value premium?

From 1963 to 1990, value beat growth by 1.53% a month. After 1991 the value premium shrank to about 0.11% a month.

Did the paper say risk doesn't matter?

No. It said beta, measured the usual way, wasn't the risk the market was paying for.

Does value and size investing work?

Value did, size didn't, in our test: cheap stocks beat expensive ones by about 6.2% a year, and small stocks beat big ones by only 1.6%.

Try it on paper first.

Pick 30 stocks across sizes and sectors, with no banks. Sort them into the six boxes with the indicator, and write down the small-value names. Check them against the S&P 500 next June, and the June after.

  • Fix the list and the sort date before you start.
  • Compare against a plain index fund, not a hand-picked benchmark.
  • Write down how long you'll give it before deciding.
