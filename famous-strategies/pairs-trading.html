Pairs Trading

Two stocks have moved together for a year. When one suddenly runs ahead of the other, does the gap close again? Short the one that ran, buy the one that lagged, and close both when their prices cross. Bet on the gap, not the market.

What is pairs trading?

Pairs trading bets on the gap between two stocks that have moved together. In Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst's 2006 paper, each stock is paired with the one whose price line stayed closest to its own over 12 months. When the gap reaches two standard deviations, you short the leader and buy the laggard, and close both when their prices cross.

Finding the pairs

The paper doesn't pick pairs by industry or story. It lets twelve months of prices decide: every stock is matched with the one whose price line stayed closest to its own.

The closest partner

1

The rule

  • Formation: take twelve months of daily total returns for every stock and start each line at $1.
  • Match each stock with the partner whose line has the smallest sum of squared gaps from its own. Rank all pairs by that distance and keep the closest 5 or 20.
  • Trading: for the next six months, open a pair when the gap reaches two standard deviations (of the gap in the formation year), and close it when the prices cross. Anything still open after six months is closed.

A new formation and trading cycle starts every month, so at any time six overlapping portfolios are running. The paper reports their average.

Distance, not correlation

Two common ways to pick pairs, and the one the paper used.

the paperSmallest distance

The sum of squared gaps between normalized prices. Simple, and it picks stocks that actually traded at the same level.

later researchCointegration

A statistical test that the spread itself tends to revert. More complex, and the subject of a lot of later work.

Opening and closing a trade

Once the pair is fixed, there's only one number to watch: how far apart the two prices are, compared with how far apart they usually were.

Open at 2σ, close at the cross

2

What can happen to an open pair

  • The gap closes and the prices cross: close both legs for a profit. This is the trade the rule is built for.
  • The gap never closes within the six months: the position is closed at the end, at a gain or a loss.
  • One stock is delisted, merged or acquired: the paper closed the pair at the last price or delisting return. These are the trades that hurt.

The rule never cuts a loser because the gap widened. Wider gaps are, by the rule's logic, better entries. That's the strategy's main risk.

Wait a day. The paper's base case trades the day after the gap hits two standard deviations, not on the same close. Signals on the same close are partly bid-ask bounce: the price you see isn't one you could have traded.

What the paper found

Forty years of US stocks, a new cycle every month, and every rule fixed in advance. The returns were steady, and most of them survived the authors' own attempts to explain them away.

The paper's returns

returns

Up to ~11% a year

Above the risk-free rate, for the base case that waits a day before trading.

risk

Close to market neutral

Market betas were near zero, and the returns weren't explained by the usual risk factors.

costs

About 1.6% a round trip

The paper's estimate per pair. Profits stayed significant after it, but it's a big share of the edge.

decline

Smaller after the 1980s

Returns fell in the later part of the sample as more hedge funds traded the same idea.

Market neutral is not risk free

Being long one stock and short its twin takes the market out of the trade. It leaves the risk that the two stop being twins.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Form

  • Twelve months of daily total returns for every stock you can trade.
  • Start every line at $1.

Now a $20 stock and a $200 stock can be compared.

2

Match

  • For each stock, the partner with the smallest sum of squared gaps.
  • Rank all pairs and keep the closest 5 or 20.

The price history picks the pairs, not a story.

3

Watch

  • For the next six months, track the gap between the two lines.
  • Measure it in the standard deviations it had during the formation year.

One number per pair.

4

Open

  • At two standard deviations, short the one that ran and buy the one that lagged, a dollar of each.
  • The paper trades the day after the signal.

You're betting on the gap, not the market.

5

Close

  • When the prices cross, close both legs.
  • If they haven't crossed when the six months end, close anyway.

Then the next month's pairs take over.

We tested it No edge

Lost a little on each pair trade

Entering at a z-score of 2 and exiting on the return to the mean lost about a fifth of a percent per trade after costs.

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). Put it on one stock and give it the partner: it tracks the gap between the two as a z-score, marks when it reaches two standard deviations and when it comes back, and alerts on both. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • Pairs Trading Spread

    alerts

    A pane under your chart with the pair's z-score: the log of this stock's price divided by the partner's, compared with its average over the formation window (252 days by default), in standard deviations. Lines at +2 and −2 are the open levels.

    At +2 a red marker says short this stock and buy the partner; at −2 a green one says the reverse. The trade stays shaded until the z-score crosses back through 0 (a white dot) or the maximum holding time runs out (an orange cross). A table shows the partner, their correlation, the z-score and the position.

    It's the common practical version, a rolling z-score of the price ratio, not the paper's fixed 12-month formation and 6-month trading periods. Every number is a setting: the partner, the window, the open and close levels, and the maximum hold.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.
  3. Open a daily chart of one stock, then in the indicator's settings pick its partner (it ships with KO, for a PEP chart).
  4. To get alerted, create an alert on the chart and pick Open: short A, buy B, Open: buy A, short B or Close the pair.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Pairs Trading Spread — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/pairs-trading.html
//
// Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst (2006): find two stocks whose prices
// have moved together, and when they split apart by two standard
// deviations, buy the cheap one and short the rich one. Close when the
// prices cross again.
//
//   1. Pair       The chart's symbol (A) against a partner you choose (B).
//   2. Spread     The log of A's price divided by B's price.
//   3. Z-score    How far the spread is from its average over the
//                 formation window, in standard deviations.
//   4. Signals    Opens at +/-2 (short the rich leg, buy the cheap one),
//                 closes when the z-score crosses back through 0, or
//                 after the maximum holding time.
//
// This is the common practical version (a rolling z-score of the price
// ratio), not the paper's fixed formation / trading periods. Use a daily
// chart. Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Pairs Trading Spread", shorttitle = "Pairs Z", overlay = false)

// ---------------- inputs ----------------
symB    = input.symbol("NYSE:KO", "Partner stock (B)")
lenF    = input.int(252, "Formation window (bars)", minval = 20, maxval = 1000, tooltip = "252 trading days is about the paper's 12-month formation period.")
zOpen   = input.float(2.0, "Open at |z| of", minval = 0.5, step = 0.1)
zClose  = input.float(0.0, "Close when z crosses", step = 0.1, tooltip = "0 = the spread is back at its average: the prices have crossed.")
maxHold = input.int(126, "Maximum holding (bars)", minval = 5, tooltip = "126 trading days is about the paper's 6-month trading period.")
showTbl = input.bool(true, "Show the table")

// ---------------- spread ----------------
cB = request.security(symB, timeframe.period, close, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
spread = math.log(close) - math.log(cB)
mu = ta.sma(spread, lenF)
sd = ta.stdev(spread, lenF)
z  = sd > 0 ? (spread - mu) / sd : na
corr = ta.correlation(close, cB, lenF)

// ---------------- position ----------------
var int pos = 0          // 1 = long A / short B, -1 = short A / long B
var int openBar = na
openShort = pos == 0 and z >= zOpen
openLong  = pos == 0 and z <= -zOpen
if openShort
    pos := -1
    openBar := bar_index
else if openLong
    pos := 1
    openBar := bar_index
held = pos != 0 ? bar_index - openBar : 0
converged = pos == -1 and z <= zClose and not openShort or pos == 1 and z >= -zClose and not openLong
timedOut  = pos != 0 and held >= maxHold and not converged
closeNow  = pos != 0 and (converged or timedOut) and not (openShort or openLong)
wasPos = pos
if closeNow
    pos := 0
    openBar := na

// ---------------- plots ----------------
hline(0, "Mean", color = color.new(#8e8e93, 40), linestyle = hline.style_dashed)
hline(zOpen, "Open (short A)", color = color.new(#ff453a, 50))
hline(-zOpen, "Open (buy A)", color = color.new(#30d158, 50))
zCol = math.abs(z) >= zOpen ? #f4b942 : #2997ff
plot(z, "Z-score", color = zCol, linewidth = 2)
bgcolor(pos == -1 or closeNow and wasPos == -1 ? color.new(#ff453a, 88) : pos == 1 or closeNow and wasPos == 1 ? color.new(#30d158, 88) : na, title = "Open trade")
plotshape(openShort, "Open: short A, buy B", shape.triangledown, location.top, #ff453a, size = size.small)
plotshape(openLong, "Open: buy A, short B", shape.triangleup, location.bottom, #30d158, size = size.small)
plotshape(closeNow and converged, "Close: converged", shape.circle, location.absolute, #f5f5f7, size = size.tiny)
plotshape(closeNow and timedOut, "Close: time ran out", shape.xcross, location.absolute, #ff9f0a, size = size.tiny)

alertcondition(openShort, "Open: short A, buy B", "{{ticker}} pair: spread above the open level, short A and buy B")
alertcondition(openLong, "Open: buy A, short B", "{{ticker}} pair: spread below the open level, buy A and short B")
alertcondition(closeNow, "Close the pair", "{{ticker}} pair: spread back to the mean (or time ran out), close both legs")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 2, 5, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if showTbl and barstate.islast
    lc = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    vc = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "Partner (B)", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, symB, text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 1, "Correlation", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 1, na(corr) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(corr, "#.00"), text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 2, "Z-score now", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 2, na(z) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(z, "#.00"), text_color = na(z) ? vc : math.abs(z) >= zOpen ? color.new(#f4b942, 0) : vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "Position", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, pos == -1 ? "short A, long B" : pos == 1 ? "long A, short B" : "flat", text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 4, "Days held", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 4, pos != 0 ? str.tostring(held) : "-", text_color = vc, text_size = size.small)

Questions

How are the pairs chosen?

By distance, not industry: the smallest sum of squared gaps between prices normalized to start at $1 over the 12-month formation period.

How long is a pair traded?

For the next six months. Anything still open then is closed, and a new cycle starts every month.

How did it do in the paper?

Up to about 11% a year above the risk-free rate on liquid US stocks from 1962 to 2002.

Does pairs trading work?

Not in our test: 1,457 pair trades lost about 0.19% a trade after costs.

Try it on paper first.

Pick five pairs of similar large stocks (two soda makers, two railroads, two home-improvement chains). Add the indicator to each and write down every open and close signal for three months, with the prices the day after. Count the ones that never crossed.

  • Fix the window and the open level before you start.
  • Note the borrow cost of every short leg.
  • Write down what you'll do if one of the pair gets news.
