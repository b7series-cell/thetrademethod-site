Sector Rotation

Industries go through long runs of leading and lagging. Rank the sectors on what they've returned lately, own the top few, and swap out any that fall behind. No view on the economy needed: the ranking does the rotating. Own the leaders, swap the laggards.

What is sector rotation?

Sector rotation ranks industry sectors on their recent returns, holds the top few and swaps out any that fall behind. Based on Moskowitz and Grinblatt (1999) and tested by Mebane Faber (2010), it ranks ten sectors each month and holds the top three, a third each, with no view on the economy needed.

Rank and hold

The whole system is one ranking a month. Faber kept it deliberately plain so anyone could follow it with a list of sector funds.

Ten sectors, top three

1

The rule

  • At each month-end, work out every sector's trailing total return. Faber's combined score averages the 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12-month returns.
  • Hold the top three sectors, a third of the money in each.
  • Next month-end, rank again. Sell any sector that dropped out of the top three and buy the one that replaced it.

Industries trend for months at a time as money, news and earnings revisions flow into them. Ranking on recent returns rides those trends without having to explain them.

Two ways to rotate

And which one this is.

momentumFollow the ranking

Hold whatever has been strongest. This page, and the research behind it.

business cycleForecast the economy

Pick sectors by where you think the cycle is heading. Popular, but it needs a forecast, and there's no comparable test behind it.

How it rotates

Trailing returns change slowly, so the top three do too. Most months nothing happens; now and then one leader fades and another takes its place.

Ranks over time

Why industries

Moskowitz and Grinblatt asked where stock momentum comes from. Much of it, they found, comes from the industry a stock belongs to.

Stocks move with their industry

2

What the research found

  • Industry momentum: buying stocks in recently winning industries and selling those in losing industries was strongly profitable.
  • Controlling for industry momentum made individual-stock momentum much weaker; the reverse wasn't true.
  • It held after controlling for size, book-to-market, individual stock momentum and microstructure effects.

If much of the momentum effect lives in industries, you can capture a large part of it with ten sector funds instead of hundreds of stocks.

Beat buy and hold in roughly 70% of years. Faber's 2010 test of the top sectors, back to the 1920s: about 3 to 6 percentage points a year over an equal-weighted mix of all ten, before trading costs and taxes.

The bear-market problem

A ranking always picks something, even when everything is falling. Faber's answer was the same trend filter as his 10-month moving average rule.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Month-end

  • On the last trading day, look up each sector's trailing total return.
  • Pick one lookback and stick with it.

One check a month.

2

Rank

  • Sort the sectors from best to worst.
  • No forecasts, no opinions about the economy.

The ranking makes the call.

3

Hold

  • Buy the top three, a third of the money in each.
  • Hold them until the next month-end.

Ride the leaders.

4

Swap

  • Sell any sector that dropped out of the top three.
  • Buy the one that replaced it; leave the rest alone.

Few trades, low costs.

5

Filter

  • Optional: if the S&P 500 closed below its 10-month average, hold T-bills.
  • Back into the top three when it closes above.

Step aside in long bear markets.

We tested it Within noise

About the same as holding every sector

Holding the top 3 of the 11 sector SPDRs beat an equal mix of all of them by about a point a year, which is within the noise for five years of monthly results.

  • +15.6%a year, top 3
  • +14.5%a year, all 11 sectors

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. //@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Sector Rotation Rank — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/sector-rotation.html
//
// Mebane Faber, Relative Strength Strategies for Investing (2010), on
// the industry momentum documented by Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999).
// At each month-end:
//
//   1  Rank the sectors on trailing total return (Faber's combined
//      lookback: the average of the 1-, 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-month returns)
//   2  Hold the top 3, a third each; replace any that drop out
//   3  Optional trend filter: if the S&P 500 closed below its 10-month
//      average, hold T-bills instead
//
// The 11 SPDR sector ETFs stand in for Faber's ten industry groups.
// Works on any chart; the ranking uses completed months only.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Sector Rotation Rank", shorttitle = "Sectors", overlay = false)

lbMode = input.string("Combined 1-3-6-9-12", "Lookback", options = ["Combined 1-3-6-9-12", "12 months", "6 months", "3 months", "1 month"])
topN   = input.int(3, "Hold the top", minval = 1, maxval = 11)
useTF  = input.bool(true, "Trend filter: T-bills when the S&P 500 is below its 10-month average")
bench  = input.symbol("AMEX:SPY", "S&P 500 for the filter")

names = array.from("Technology", "Financials", "Energy", "Health care", "Consumer disc.", "Staples", "Industrials", "Materials", "Utilities", "Real estate", "Communication")

// total return momentum from completed months, dividends added back
mom(string sym) =>
    t = ticker.modify(sym, adjustment = adjustment.dividends)
    request.security(t, "M", lbMode == "Combined 1-3-6-9-12" ? ((close[1] / close[2] - 1) + (close[1] / close[4] - 1) + (close[1] / close[7] - 1) + (close[1] / close[10] - 1) + (close[1] / close[13] - 1)) / 5 * 100 : lbMode == "12 months" ? (close[1] / close[13] - 1) * 100 : lbMode == "6 months" ? (close[1] / close[7] - 1) * 100 : lbMode == "3 months" ? (close[1] / close[4] - 1) * 100 : (close[1] / close[2] - 1) * 100, lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)

m0  = mom("AMEX:XLK")
m1  = mom("AMEX:XLF")
m2  = mom("AMEX:XLE")
m3  = mom("AMEX:XLV")
m4  = mom("AMEX:XLY")
m5  = mom("AMEX:XLP")
m6  = mom("AMEX:XLI")
m7  = mom("AMEX:XLB")
m8  = mom("AMEX:XLU")
m9  = mom("AMEX:XLRE")
m10 = mom("AMEX:XLC")
vals = array.from(m0, m1, m2, m3, m4, m5, m6, m7, m8, m9, m10)

bC  = request.security(bench, "M", close[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
bMa = request.security(bench, "M", ta.sma(close, 10)[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
riskOff = useTF and not na(bMa) and bC < bMa

// rank: count how many sectors beat each one
rankOf(int i) =>
    v = array.get(vals, i)
    r = 1
    for j = 0 to array.size(vals) - 1
        w = array.get(vals, j)
        if j != i and not na(w) and not na(v) and w > v
            r += 1
    na(v) ? 99 : r

// a code for the current top-N set, to spot changes
code = 0
for i = 0 to 10
    if rankOf(i) <= topN
        code += int(math.pow(2, i))
// compare once per month, at the first bar of the new month
var int prevCode = 0
var bool prevOff = false
changed = false
offFlip = false
if timeframe.change("M")
    changed := prevCode != 0 and code != 0 and code != prevCode
    offFlip := riskOff != prevOff
    prevCode := code
    prevOff := riskOff

plot(m0, "Technology", color = color.new(#2997ff, 0))
plot(m2, "Energy", color = color.new(#ff9f0a, 0))
plot(m3, "Health care", color = color.new(#30d158, 0))
plot(m1, "Financials", color = color.new(#bf5af2, 0))
hline(0, "Zero", color = color.new(#48484a, 0), linestyle = hline.style_dotted)
bgcolor(riskOff ? color.new(#ff453a, 88) : na, title = "Trend filter: T-bills")

alertcondition(changed, "Sectors: top set changed", "Sector rotation: the top sectors changed at month-end. Check the table.")
alertcondition(offFlip and riskOff, "Sectors: to T-bills", "Sector rotation: the S&P 500 closed below its 10-month average. Trend filter says T-bills.")
alertcondition(offFlip and not riskOff, "Sectors: back in", "Sector rotation: the S&P 500 closed back above its 10-month average.")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 3, 13, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if barstate.islast
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "#", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, "Sector", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 0, "Momentum", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    row = 1
    for r = 1 to 11
        for i = 0 to 10
            if rankOf(i) == r and row <= 11
                v = array.get(vals, i)
                held = r <= topN and not riskOff
                table.cell(tb, 0, row, str.tostring(r), text_color = held ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
                table.cell(tb, 1, row, array.get(names, i) + (held ? "  ●" : ""), text_color = held ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
                table.cell(tb, 2, row, str.tostring(v, "+#.0;-#.0") + "%", text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
                row += 1
    table.cell(tb, 0, 12, "", text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 12, riskOff ? "Trend filter: hold T-bills" : "Hold the top " + str.tostring(topN) + ", equal weights", text_color = riskOff ? color.new(#ff453a, 0) : color.new(#30d158, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 12, useTF ? (riskOff ? "S&P < 10M avg" : "S&P > 10M avg") : "", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)

Questions

How are sectors ranked?

By trailing total return. Faber's combined score averages the 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12-month returns.

How did it do in Faber's test?

About 3 to 6% a year over holding all ten sectors, before costs, beating buy and hold in roughly 70% of years.

Is this business cycle rotation?

No. Business cycle rotation picks sectors from a forecast of the economy. This version just follows the ranking.

Does sector rotation work?

Within the noise in our test: the top 3 of the 11 sector SPDRs made +15.6% a year against +14.5% for an equal mix of all of them.

Try it on paper first.

On the last trading day of each month, rank the sector ETFs on one lookback and write down the top three. Track them for six months next to SPY, and count how many swaps you made.

  • Use the same lookback every month.
  • Write the ranking down before the next month starts.
  • Note whether the trend filter would have been on.
