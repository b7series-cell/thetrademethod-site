Opening Range Breakout

The first minutes of the session are when overnight news gets priced. On a stock with real news, the direction of the first five minutes tends to continue. Trade the break of that first candle, keep the stop tight, and hold to the close, but only on stocks in play. The first five minutes set the day.

What is the opening range breakout strategy?

This version, from Toby Crabel (1990) as tested by Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2024), trades the first 5-minute candle. A green candle means longs only, red shorts only. You place a stop order at its high or low, risk 10% of the 14-day ATR sized to 1% of capital, and hold to the 4:00 close, on stocks in play.

Five minutes, one side

Crabel's idea was simple: mark the range of the open and trade whichever way price breaks it. The modern version tested by Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz uses the first 5-minute candle twice: its color picks the side, and its high or low is the entry.

One day, one stock

1

The rule

  • At 9:35, look at the first 5-minute candle. Green: longs only today. Red: shorts only. A doji: no trade.
  • Place a stop order to buy at its high (or sell short at its low). If price never gets there, there's no trade.
  • Stop loss 10% of the stock's 14-day ATR from the entry, sized so a stop-out costs 1% of capital. No target: close whatever is left at 4:00 pm.

A tight stop with no target means many small losses and a few days that pay several times the risk. It only adds up on stocks with a reason to trend.

Only stocks in play

A stock in play is one trading far more than usual, usually because of news: earnings, an upgrade, a deal. The paper measured it with relative volume, and that filter turned out to be most of the result.

Relative volume at 9:35

2

Finding stocks in play

  • Relative volume: the first 5 minutes' volume divided by the average first-5-minute volume over the last 14 days. It has to be at least 100%.
  • Basic filters: opening price over $5, 14-day average volume of at least 1 million shares, 14-day ATR over $0.50.
  • Rank what's left by relative volume and trade the top 20.

Unusual volume at the open means new information is being priced. Those are the stocks most likely to keep going the way the open points.

The filter is the strategy. On every stock that passed the basic filters, the same 5-minute rules made 29% in eight years. On the top 20 stocks by relative volume, they made 1,637%.

Living with it

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Scan

  • At 9:35, rank stocks by relative volume: first-5-minute volume vs its 14-day average.
  • Keep 100%+, over $5, 1M+ shares a day, ATR over $0.50.

Stocks with news are the ones that trend.

2

Side

  • Green first 5-minute candle: longs only.
  • Red: shorts only. Doji: skip it.

The open's direction is the bet.

3

Order

  • Buy stop at the first candle's high, or sell stop at its low.
  • No break, no trade.

Price has to confirm the direction first.

4

Stop

  • 10% of the 14-day ATR from the entry.
  • Size it so a stop-out costs 1% of capital.

A small, fixed loss on every failure.

5

Close

  • No profit target.
  • Close what's left at 4:00 pm. Never hold overnight.

The trend days pay for the rest.

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It marks the opening range, checks relative volume and the paper's filters, draws the entry and the 10% ATR stop, and alerts on the break. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Opening Range Breakout — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/opening-range-breakout.html
//
// The rules tested by Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024) on US "stocks in
// play", 2016-2023:
//
//   1  The first 5-minute candle sets the side: green = longs only,
//      red = shorts only, a doji = no trade.
//   2  Long: stop buy at its high. Short: stop sell at its low.
//   3  Stop loss 10% of the 14-day ATR from the entry.
//   4  No target: whatever is left is closed at the 4:00 pm close.
//   5  Only stocks in play: first-5-minute volume at least 100% of its
//      14-day average for that window (relative volume), price over $5,
//      14-day average volume of 1M+ shares, 14-day ATR over $0.50.
//      (The paper traded the 20 stocks with the highest relative volume.)
//
// Use a 1- or 5-minute chart of a US stock, regular hours only.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Opening Range Breakout", shorttitle = "ORB", overlay = true, max_labels_count = 200)

orMin    = input.int(5, "Opening range (minutes)", options = [5, 15, 30, 60])
atrFrac  = input.float(10, "Stop: % of the 14-day ATR", step = 1)
rvMin    = input.float(100, "In play: relative volume at least (%)", step = 10)
rvLen    = input.int(14, "Relative volume: days averaged", minval = 2)
minPx    = input.float(5, "In play: price above ($)")
minAvgV  = input.float(1000000, "In play: 14-day average volume at least (shares)")
minAtr   = input.float(0.5, "In play: 14-day ATR above ($)", step = 0.05)
onlyPlay = input.bool(true, "Signal only on stocks in play")

// daily values from yesterday (no look-ahead)
atrD  = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", ta.atr(14)[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
avgVD = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", ta.sma(volume, 14)[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)

var float sessStart = na
var float orH = na
var float orL = na
var float orO = na
var float orC = na
var float orV = 0.0
var bool  orDone = false
var int   dir = 0
var float relVol = na
var bool  inPlay = false
var history = array.new<float>()

var int   pos = 0
var float entry = na
var float stopL = na
var bool  tradedToday = false

newDay = session.isfirstbar_regular
if newDay
    sessStart := time
    orH := high
    orL := low
    orO := open
    orC := close
    orV := volume
    orDone := false
    dir := 0
    relVol := na
    inPlay := false
    pos := 0
    entry := na
    stopL := na
    tradedToday := false

inOR = not na(sessStart) and time < sessStart + orMin * 60000
if inOR and not newDay
    orH := math.max(orH, high)
    orL := math.min(orL, low)
    orC := close
    orV += volume

// the first bar after the range: decide the side and whether it's in play
orJustDone = not inOR and not orDone and not na(sessStart)
if orJustDone
    orDone := true
    dir := orC > orO ? 1 : orC < orO ? -1 : 0
    n = array.size(history)
    if n > 0
        avgOR = array.avg(history)
        relVol := avgOR > 0 ? orV / avgOR * 100 : na
    array.push(history, orV)
    if array.size(history) > rvLen
        array.shift(history)
    inPlay := not na(relVol) and relVol >= rvMin and orO > minPx and avgVD >= minAvgV and atrD > minAtr

canTrade = orDone and not tradedToday and dir != 0 and (inPlay or not onlyPlay) and not na(atrD)
longIn  = canTrade and dir == 1 and high > orH
shortIn = canTrade and dir == -1 and low < orL
stopHit = pos != 0 and ((pos == 1 and low <= stopL) or (pos == -1 and high >= stopL))
if stopHit
    label.new(bar_index, pos == 1 ? low : high, "stop", style = pos == 1 ? label.style_label_up : label.style_label_down, color = color.new(#ff453a, 10), textcolor = color.white, size = size.tiny)
    pos := 0
if longIn
    entry := math.max(open, orH)
    stopL := entry - atrD * atrFrac / 100
    pos := 1
    tradedToday := true
    label.new(bar_index, low, "ORB long" + (na(relVol) ? "" : "\nRV " + str.tostring(relVol, "#") + "%"), style = label.style_label_up, color = color.new(#30d158, 10), textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)
if shortIn
    entry := math.min(open, orL)
    stopL := entry + atrD * atrFrac / 100
    pos := -1
    tradedToday := true
    label.new(bar_index, high, "ORB short" + (na(relVol) ? "" : "\nRV " + str.tostring(relVol, "#") + "%"), style = label.style_label_down, color = color.new(#ff453a, 10), textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)
closeOut = session.islastbar_regular and pos != 0
if closeOut
    label.new(bar_index, close, "out at the close", style = label.style_label_left, color = color.new(#86868b, 20), textcolor = color.white, size = size.tiny)
    pos := 0

plot(orDone ? orH : na, "Range high", color = color.new(#30d158, 0), style = plot.style_linebr, linewidth = 2)
plot(orDone ? orL : na, "Range low", color = color.new(#ff453a, 0), style = plot.style_linebr, linewidth = 2)
plot(pos != 0 or stopHit ? stopL : na, "Stop (10% ATR)", color = color.new(#f4b942, 0), style = plot.style_linebr)
bgcolor(inOR ? color.new(#2997ff, 88) : na, title = "Opening range window")

alertcondition(longIn, "ORB long", "{{ticker}}: broke the opening-range high on a green first candle")
alertcondition(shortIn, "ORB short", "{{ticker}}: broke the opening-range low on a red first candle")
alertcondition(stopHit, "ORB stop hit", "{{ticker}}: hit the 10% ATR stop")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 2, 7, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
row(int r, string k, string v, color c) =>
    table.cell(tb, 0, r, k, text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, r, v, text_color = c, text_size = size.small)
if barstate.islast
    ink = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    row(0, "Range (" + str.tostring(orMin) + " min)", orDone ? str.tostring(orL, format.mintick) + " – " + str.tostring(orH, format.mintick) : "forming", ink)
    row(1, "First candle", not orDone ? "–" : dir == 1 ? "green: longs only" : dir == -1 ? "red: shorts only" : "doji: no trade", dir == 1 ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : dir == -1 ? color.new(#ff453a, 0) : ink)
    row(2, "Relative volume", na(relVol) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(relVol, "#") + "%", not na(relVol) and relVol >= rvMin ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : ink)
    row(3, "14-day ATR", na(atrD) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(atrD, "#.00"), ink)
    row(4, "Stop distance", na(atrD) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(atrD * atrFrac / 100, "#.00"), color.new(#f4b942, 0))
    row(5, "In play", orDone ? (inPlay ? "yes" : "no") : "–", inPlay ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : color.new(#ff453a, 0))
    row(6, "Position", pos == 1 ? "long from " + str.tostring(entry, format.mintick) : pos == -1 ? "short from " + str.tostring(entry, format.mintick) : tradedToday ? "done for today" : "flat", ink)

Questions

What counts as a stock in play?

In the paper, the 20 US stocks with the highest relative volume that day, at 100% or more above normal.

How did it do in the paper?

1,637% net from 2016 to 2023 on stocks in play, with a 2.81 Sharpe ratio. The same rules on every stock made 29%.

Why no profit target?

A tight stop with no target means many small losses and a few days that pay several times the risk. It only adds up on stocks with a reason to trend.

Does it work?

A small edge on QQQ in our test: +0.07R a trade over 1,158 trades on 1-minute bars, about 0.14R better than random direction. On SPY and 10 single stocks it lost.

Try it on paper first.

Every morning for a month, write down the three stocks with the highest relative volume at 9:35, the color of each first candle, the entry and the stop. Mark each one at the stop or the close, in R, and compare your fills with the levels.

  • Write the levels down before the break.
  • Never widen the stop.
  • Count your slippage in R: with a stop this tight it adds up fast.
