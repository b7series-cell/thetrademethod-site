Dual Momentum

Two kinds of momentum, used together. Relative momentum picks the stronger of two stock markets. Absolute momentum asks whether stocks are beating T-bills at all, and if they aren't, it holds bonds instead. One decision a month. The stronger market, or bonds.

What is dual momentum?

Dual momentum is Gary Antonacci's method from Dual Momentum Investing (2014). Once a month, relative momentum picks the stronger of US and non-US stocks over 12 months, and absolute momentum checks whether US stocks beat T-bills. If they didn't, the money goes to US aggregate bonds. His Global Equities Momentum (GEM) model uses both.

Relative momentum

The first test is the one behind most momentum research: among a few choices, hold whatever has done best lately. Antonacci uses just two, the US stock market and the rest of the world's.

The stronger market

Absolute momentum

Relative momentum always picks something, even when both markets are falling. The second test fixes that: the pick has to be beating cash, or the money goes to bonds.

Beating T-bills

1

The rule (GEM)

  • At each month-end, take the 12-month total returns of US stocks (the S&P 500), non-US stocks (MSCI ACWI ex-US) and T-bills.
  • If US stocks beat T-bills, hold whichever stock market did better over the 12 months.
  • If US stocks trailed T-bills, hold US aggregate bonds. Check again next month-end.

Relative momentum adds return by riding the stronger market. Absolute momentum cuts risk by stepping out of stocks when their trend has turned down.

Two tests, two jobs

Why it uses both.

relativeWhich market?

Picks the stronger of US and non-US stocks. On its own, it stays fully in stocks through bear markets.

absoluteStocks at all?

Compares US stocks with T-bills. When they trail, the whole account goes to bonds.

Over a cycle

Put month after month together and the strategy spends most of its time in one stock market or the other, and moves to bonds for the long declines.

What it held

2

What it's like to follow

  • Few trades: most months nothing changes, and there are only three places the money can be.
  • Always late: a 12-month lookback reacts after a decline has started and after a recovery is under way.
  • Fast reversals hurt most: a sharp drop and a quick rebound can switch it out near the low and back in higher.

The rule pays off over long bear markets, like 2000–02 and 2008, and pays for it in quick V-shaped selloffs.

Momentum, twice. Antonacci's point was that relative momentum (Jegadeesh and Titman's winners keep winning) and absolute momentum (an asset's own trend) do different jobs, and work better together than either does alone.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Month-end

  • On the last trading day, look up 12-month total returns.
  • US stocks, non-US stocks and T-bills.

One check a month.

2

Relative

  • Compare US with non-US stocks.
  • The higher 12-month return is the pick.

Ride the stronger market.

3

Absolute

  • Compare US stocks with T-bills.
  • Trailing T-bills means the trend is down.

Decide whether to own stocks at all.

4

Hold

  • US stocks beat T-bills: hold the pick.
  • They didn't: hold US aggregate bonds.

All of the money in one place.

5

Repeat

  • Switch only when one of the tests flips.
  • Most months, nothing changes.

Few trades, low costs.

We tested it Trails holding SPY

Trailed simply holding SPY

Switching between US stocks, foreign stocks and bonds made a solid return, but less than staying in SPY.

  • +13.8%a year, GEM
  • +17.6%a year, SPY held

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. //@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Dual Momentum (GEM) — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/dual-momentum.html
//
// Gary Antonacci, Dual Momentum Investing (2014): Global Equities
// Momentum. Once a month, using the last 12 months:
//
//   Relative momentum: pick the stronger of US stocks and non-US stocks
//   Absolute momentum: if US stocks beat T-bills, hold the pick;
//                      if not, hold bonds until they do
//
// ETFs stand in for the book's indexes: SPY (S&P 500), VEU (all-world
// ex-US), BIL (T-bills) and AGG (US aggregate bonds). Returns are total
// returns: dividends and interest are added back, as in the book.
//
// Works on any timeframe: the signal uses completed months only.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Dual Momentum (GEM)", shorttitle = "GEM", overlay = false)

lb   = input.int(12, "Lookback (months)", minval = 1)
sUS  = input.symbol("AMEX:SPY", "US stocks")
sEX  = input.symbol("AMEX:VEU", "Non-US stocks")
sTB  = input.symbol("AMEX:BIL", "T-bills")
sBD  = input.symbol("AMEX:AGG", "Bonds")

// the total return over the last lb completed months (no look-ahead)
ret(string sym) =>
    t = ticker.modify(sym, adjustment = adjustment.dividends)
    request.security(t, "M", (close[1] / close[1 + lb] - 1) * 100, lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on, ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
rUS = ret(sUS)
rEX = ret(sEX)
rTB = ret(sTB)
rBD = ret(sBD)

ok = not na(rUS) and not na(rEX) and not na(rTB)
pick = not ok ? 0 : rUS > rTB ? (rUS >= rEX ? 1 : 2) : 3   // 1 US, 2 non-US, 3 bonds
nameOf(int p) => p == 1 ? "US stocks" : p == 2 ? "Non-US stocks" : p == 3 ? "Bonds" : "n/a"
colOf(int p) => p == 1 ? color.new(#2997ff, 0) : p == 2 ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : p == 3 ? color.new(#f4b942, 0) : color.new(#86868b, 0)

plot(rUS, "US stocks, 12-month %", color = colOf(1), linewidth = 2)
plot(rEX, "Non-US stocks, 12-month %", color = colOf(2), linewidth = 2)
plot(rTB, "T-bills, 12-month %", color = color.new(#86868b, 0), style = plot.style_stepline)
hline(0, "Zero", color = color.new(#48484a, 0), linestyle = hline.style_dotted)
bgcolor(pick == 0 ? na : color.new(colOf(pick), 88), title = "Holding")

switched = pick != pick[1] and pick[1] != 0 and pick != 0
if switched
    label.new(bar_index, math.max(rUS, rEX), "→ " + nameOf(pick), style = label.style_label_down, color = colOf(pick), textcolor = color.black, size = size.small)

alertcondition(switched, "GEM: switch", "Dual momentum: the holding changed at month-end. Check the indicator.")
alertcondition(switched and pick == 3, "GEM: to bonds", "Dual momentum: US stocks trail T-bills over 12 months. Hold bonds.")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 2, 6, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
f(float v) => na(v) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(v, "+#.0;-#.0") + "%"
if barstate.islast
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, str.tostring(lb) + "-month return", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, "completed months", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 1, "US stocks", text_color = colOf(1), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 1, f(rUS), text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 2, "Non-US stocks", text_color = colOf(2), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 2, f(rEX), text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "T-bills", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, f(rTB), text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 4, "Bonds", text_color = colOf(3), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 4, f(rBD), text_color = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 5, "Hold", text_color = color.new(#86868b, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 5, nameOf(pick), text_color = colOf(pick), text_size = size.small)

Questions

What is the difference between relative and absolute momentum?

Relative momentum picks which market is stronger, so it always holds something. Absolute momentum asks whether stocks are beating cash at all, and steps into bonds when they aren't.

How often do you check it?

At each month-end, using 12-month total returns.

What did the book's backtest show?

From 1974 to 2013, before costs and taxes, GEM made 17.43% a year with a worst drawdown of 22.72%, against 8.85% and 60.21% for world stocks.

Does dual momentum work?

It trailed holding SPY in our test: +13.8% a year for GEM against +17.6% for SPY held, over five years with no long bear market.

Try it on paper first.

On the last trading day of each month, write down the 12-month total returns of SPY, VEU and BIL, and what GEM says to hold. Do it for a year and count the switches, including any that reversed the month after.

  • Check only at month-end.
  • Use total returns, with dividends.
  • Write the holding down before the next month starts.
