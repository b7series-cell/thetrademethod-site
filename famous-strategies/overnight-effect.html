The Overnight Effect

Split each trading day into two halves: the night, from the 4 pm close to the next morning's open, and the day, from the open to the close. Since the 1990s, the US market's gain has come almost entirely from the night. Earned while the market was shut.

What is the overnight effect?

The overnight effect is the finding that since the 1990s the US market's gain has come almost entirely between the 4:00 pm close and the next morning's open, not during the trading day. Cliff, Cooper and Gulen (2008) found nights beat days in all 14 big ETFs they studied.

Two halves of every day

Every daily candle hides two returns: the gap from yesterday's close to today's open, and the move from the open to the close.

Night and day

1

The rule

  • Buy a broad index fund such as SPY at the close, 4:00 pm New York time.
  • Hold it overnight, while the exchanges are shut.
  • Sell at the next morning's open, 9:30 am, and stay flat until the close. Repeat every trading day.

If the market's return really arrives overnight, owning only the nights keeps that return and sits out the day's swings.

How to use it

Two ways traders apply it.

on its ownEvery night, flat every day

About 252 round trips a year. Only works if each costs less than the average night earns.

as timingWhen to buy and sell

Already buying or selling an index fund? Buying near the close and selling near the open leans on the pattern.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Close

  • Buy a broad index fund at the close.
  • A market-on-close order fills at the closing price.

The night starts at 4 pm.

2

Night

  • Hold while the exchanges are shut.
  • This is where the research found the return.

News lands overnight.

3

Open

  • Sell at the next morning's open.
  • A market-on-open order fills at the opening price.

The night ends at 9:30 am.

4

Day

  • No position from the open to the close.
  • On average the day has earned about nothing.

Sit out the session.

5

Repeat

  • Every trading day.
  • Track every fill and cost.

Costs decide if it pays.

We tested it Trails holding

Nights beat days before costs, and both trail holding

Holding SPY only overnight made +33% before costs and +17.3% after them. The daytime made +18.2%, and simply holding made +58.3%.

  • +33%nights, before costs
  • +17.3%nights, after costs
  • +18.2%days
  • +58.3%SPY held
  • Trading in and out every day costs about half of the overnight return.

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It splits each daily bar into its overnight and intraday halves, draws what owning only each half would have done, and on intraday charts marks the session's last and first bars with alerts. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • Overnight Effect

    alerts

    In its own pane: a green growth-of-100 line for owning only the nights (close to the next open) and a gold one for owning only the days (open to close), from the start year you choose.

    A table with the average night and day, what 100 grew to in each half, how often each was up, and the days counted. On SPY's daily history since 1993, our run showed +0.033% a night and +0.007% a day, and 100 growing to about 1,359 overnight against 124 by day, price only.

    On an intraday chart it marks the last bar of each session (buy at the close) and the first (sell at the open), with alerts for both. Prices are price only, so ex-dividend drops land in the overnight half.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.
  3. Use a daily chart of SPY, QQQ or another index fund for the split and the table.
  4. For alerts, switch to an intraday chart and create an alert on Overnight: buy at the close or Overnight: sell at the open.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Overnight Effect — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/overnight-effect.html
//
// Cliff, Cooper & Gulen (2008): from 1993 to 2006, US stock returns
// came overnight (close to next open); the trading day itself earned
// about nothing on average.
//
// Splits each daily bar into two halves:
//   overnight  = yesterday's close to today's open
//   intraday   = today's open to today's close
// and draws a growth-of-100 line for owning only each half.
//
// Daily chart (any stock or ETF) for the split and the table.
// On an intraday chart it marks the last bar of the session (buy at
// the close) and the first bar (sell at the open), with alerts.
// Price only: ex-dividend drops land in the overnight half.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Overnight Effect", shorttitle = "Overnight", overlay = false)

startYear = input.int(1993, "Count from year", minval = 1900, maxval = 2100)

isD = timeframe.isdaily
on = isD and year >= startYear and not na(close[1])

rNight = (open / close[1] - 1) * 100
rDay   = (close / open - 1) * 100

var float eqNight = 100.0
var float eqDay   = 100.0
var float sumN = 0.0
var float sumD = 0.0
var int   n = 0
var int   upN = 0
var int   upD = 0
if on
    eqNight *= 1 + rNight / 100
    eqDay   *= 1 + rDay / 100
    sumN += rNight
    sumD += rDay
    n += 1
    upN += rNight > 0 ? 1 : 0
    upD += rDay > 0 ? 1 : 0

plot(on ? eqNight : na, "Overnight only (close to open)", color = color.new(#30d158, 0), linewidth = 2)
plot(on ? eqDay : na, "Intraday only (open to close)", color = color.new(#f4b942, 0), linewidth = 2)
hline(100, "Start", color = color.new(#86868b, 60), linestyle = hline.style_dashed)

// intraday charts: session edges
buyBar  = timeframe.isintraday and session.islastbar_regular
sellBar = timeframe.isintraday and session.isfirstbar_regular
plotshape(buyBar, "Buy at the close", shape.triangleup, location.bottom, color.new(#2997ff, 0), size = size.tiny)
plotshape(sellBar, "Sell at the open", shape.triangledown, location.top, color.new(#f4b942, 0), size = size.tiny)
alertcondition(buyBar, "Overnight: buy at the close", "{{ticker}}: last bar of the session. Overnight effect: buy at the close.")
alertcondition(sellBar, "Overnight: sell at the open", "{{ticker}}: first bar of the session. Overnight effect: sell at the open.")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_left, 2, 5, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if barstate.islast
    ink = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    dim = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    fmt = "+#.000;-#.000"
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "Avg night (close to open)", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, n > 0 ? str.tostring(sumN / n, fmt) + "%" : "daily chart", text_color = color.new(#30d158, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 1, "Avg day (open to close)", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 1, n > 0 ? str.tostring(sumD / n, fmt) + "%" : "daily chart", text_color = color.new(#f4b942, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 2, "100 grew to, night / day", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 2, n > 0 ? str.tostring(eqNight, "#") + " / " + str.tostring(eqDay, "#") : "-", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "Up nights / up days", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, n > 0 ? str.tostring(100.0 * upN / n, "#.0") + "% / " + str.tostring(100.0 * upD / n, "#.0") + "%" : "-", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 4, "Days counted", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 4, str.tostring(n) + " since " + str.tostring(startYear), text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)

Questions

How do you trade the overnight effect?

Buy a broad index fund such as SPY at the 4:00 pm close, sell at the 9:30 am open, and stay flat during the day. That's about 252 round trips a year.

What did the research find?

From 1993 to 2006, S&P 500 stocks averaged 0.028% to 0.048% a night and -0.028% to 0.002% a day. On SPY, nights beat days by 0.068% a day.

Can you use it without trading every night?

Yes, as timing: buy index funds near the close and sell near the open.

Does the overnight effect work?

Nights beat days before costs in our test, but both trailed holding. SPY held only overnight made +33% before costs and +17.3% after, against +58.3% for simply holding.

Try it on paper first.

Put the indicator on a daily SPY chart, then for a month write down the closing price each day and the next morning's opening price. Price your own spread and commissions per round trip and see what's left of the average night.

  • Record real closing and opening prints.
  • Count every round trip's cost.
  • Compare with simply holding SPY.
