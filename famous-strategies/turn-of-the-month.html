Turn of the Month

For most of the last century, US stocks earned their return in a few days each month. The last trading day of the month and the first three of the next did the work; the days in between, on average, did nothing. Four days a month.

What is the turn of the month effect?

The turn of the month effect is the finding that US stocks earned most of their return on four trading days each month: the last day of the month and the first three of the next. McConnell and Xu (2008) found 0.15% a day in that window from 1926 to 2005, and about nothing on other days.

Which four days

The window is defined by trading days, not calendar dates, so it moves around weekends and holidays.

The window

1

The rule

  • Find the month's last trading day. Buy a broad index fund such as SPY at the close of the trading day before it.
  • Hold through the last trading day and the first three trading days of the new month.
  • Sell at the close of the third trading day, and hold cash or T-bills until the next month's turn.

If the market's return really is concentrated in these days, owning only them keeps most of the return with a fraction of the time in the market.

How to use it

Two ways traders apply it.

on its ownIn four days, out the rest

Hold the index only in the window. About a fifth of the time in the market.

as a tiltTiming other trades

Keep a long-term portfolio, and time new money or rebalancing around the window.

Where the return was

The surprising part isn't that the turn of the month was good. It's that the rest of the month, on average, earned nothing.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Count

  • Find the month's last trading day.
  • Skip weekends and market holidays.

Trading days, not dates.

2

Buy

  • Buy a broad index fund at the close of the day before the last trading day.
  • That way you own the last day's return.

The window starts on the last day.

3

Hold

  • Hold through the last trading day.
  • And the first three trading days of the new month.

Four days in the market.

4

Sell

  • Sell at the close of the third trading day.
  • Move to cash or T-bills.

The window ends there.

5

Wait

  • No position for the rest of the month.
  • Repeat at the next turn.

On average, those days earned nothing.

The turn of the month wasn't special in these years

The days around the turn are 19% of trading days and made 9.8% in total. The rest of the month made 46.9%, so the turn earned about its share and no more.

  • +9.8%total, turn-of-month days
  • 19%of trading days
  • +46.9%total, the rest of the month

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. //@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Turn of the Month — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/turn-of-the-month.html
//
// Lakonishok & Smidt (1988); McConnell & Xu (2008): US stock returns
// have been concentrated in four trading days around each month-end:
// the last trading day of the month and the first three of the next.
//
//   Buy at the close of the day BEFORE the last trading day
//   Sell at the close of the third trading day of the new month
//
// The last trading day is worked out from the calendar (weekends only;
// market holidays aren't known to the script, so check those by hand).
// Daily chart of SPY or another broad index fund.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Turn of the Month", shorttitle = "TOM", overlay = true, max_labels_count = 300)

firstDays = input.int(3, "Trading days held into the new month", minval = 1, maxval = 10)
tz = "America/New_York"
DAY = 86400000

nextWd(int t) =>
    d = dayofweek(t, tz)
    t + (d == dayofweek.friday ? 3 : d == dayofweek.saturday ? 2 : 1) * DAY

var int tdm = 0
tdm := timeframe.change("M") ? 1 : tdm + 1

n1 = nextWd(time)
n2 = nextWd(n1)
isLast  = month(n1, tz) != month(time, tz)
isEntry = not isLast and month(n2, tz) != month(time, tz)
inTOM   = isLast or tdm <= firstDays
isExit  = tdm == firstDays

// average daily return in and out of the window, on this chart's history
r = (close / close[1] - 1) * 100
var float sumT = 0.0
var int nT = 0
var float sumO = 0.0
var int nO = 0
if not na(r) and timeframe.isdaily
    if inTOM
        sumT += r
        nT += 1
    else
        sumO += r
        nO += 1

bgcolor(inTOM ? color.new(#30d158, 85) : na, title = "Turn-of-the-month days")
if isEntry and timeframe.isdaily
    label.new(bar_index, low, "buy at close", style = label.style_label_up, color = color.new(#2997ff, 15), textcolor = color.white, size = size.tiny)
if isExit and timeframe.isdaily
    label.new(bar_index, high, "sell at close", style = label.style_label_down, color = color.new(#f4b942, 15), textcolor = color.black, size = size.tiny)

alertcondition(isEntry, "TOM: buy at the close", "{{ticker}}: tomorrow is the month's last trading day. Turn of the month: buy at today's close.")
alertcondition(isExit, "TOM: sell at the close", "{{ticker}}: third trading day of the month. Turn of the month: sell at today's close.")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 2, 5, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if barstate.islast
    ink = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    dim = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "Today", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, isEntry ? "buy at the close" : isLast ? "last trading day: hold" : tdm <= firstDays ? "day " + str.tostring(tdm) + " of the month: hold" : "outside the window", text_color = inTOM or isEntry ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 1, "Avg day, turn of month", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 1, nT > 0 ? str.tostring(sumT / nT, "+#.000;-#.000") + "%" : "daily chart", text_color = color.new(#30d158, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 2, "Avg day, rest of month", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 2, nO > 0 ? str.tostring(sumO / nO, "+#.000;-#.000") + "%" : "daily chart", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "Days counted", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, str.tostring(nT) + " in / " + str.tostring(nO) + " out", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 4, "Holidays", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 4, "not known: check by hand", text_color = color.new(#f4b942, 0), text_size = size.small)

Questions

How do you trade the turn of the month?

Buy a broad index fund at the close of the day before the month's last trading day, and sell at the close of the third trading day of the new month.

Is it only a US effect?

No. McConnell and Xu found it in 31 of 35 countries.

Is it caused by fund inflows?

They found no extra volume or fund inflows that explained it.

Does the turn of the month still work?

Not in our test. The turn days were 19% of trading days and made 9.8% in total, while the rest of the month made 46.9%, so they earned about their share and no more.

Try it on paper first.

Put the indicator on a daily SPY chart. For the next six months, write down the buy price at the close before each month's last trading day and the sell price at the close of day three, and compare the total with simply holding SPY.

  • Check the holiday calendar each month.
  • Record actual closing prices.
  • Count costs for 12 round trips a year.
