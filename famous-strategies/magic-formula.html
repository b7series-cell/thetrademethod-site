Magic Formula

Can two numbers pick stocks? Rank every company on how cheap it is and how good a business it is, add the two ranks, and buy the 20 to 30 best totals. Hold each for a year, then replace it. Good companies at bargain prices.

Two numbers

Greenblatt boiled value investing down to two questions any business owner would ask: how much does the business earn for what I'm paying, and how much does it earn on the money it needs to run?

Cheap, and good

1

The rule

  • Start with the largest US stocks (the book used 3,500), and leave out financials and utilities, whose balance sheets don't fit the formula.
  • Rank them all on earnings yield (EBIT ÷ enterprise value), best first, and again on return on capital (EBIT ÷ net working capital plus net fixed assets).
  • Add the two ranks, buy the stocks with the lowest totals, hold each for a year, and replace it from the current list.

Both numbers use EBIT, operating profit before interest and taxes, so companies with different debt and tax situations compare on the same footing.

Why these two, and not P/E and ROE?

The book's versions fix two common distortions.

earnings yieldUses enterprise value

Price plus debt, minus excess cash. A cheap P/E bought with a pile of debt isn't really cheap.

return on capitalUses tangible capital

Working capital plus plants and equipment, the money the business actually needs. Goodwill and accounting leverage don't flatter it.

Rank, add, buy

Neither number decides alone. A stock has to rank well on both to reach the top, which keeps out cheap businesses that are cheap for a reason and great businesses at silly prices.

Two ranks, one list

2

The book's buying schedule

  • Buy 5 to 7 of the top-ranked stocks.
  • Every two or three months, buy another 5 to 7 from the current list, until you hold 20 to 30.
  • Sell each one after a year and replace it. In a taxable account, the book suggests selling losers just before the year is up and winners just after.

Staggering the buys means no single ranking date decides the whole portfolio, and it turns the portfolio over a little at a time.

Good businesses at bargain prices. That's the whole book in four words, and the formula is just a mechanical way to find them without having to judge each business yourself.

The hard part is sticking with it

Greenblatt is blunt about it: the formula doesn't work every year, and he thinks that's exactly why it keeps working.

The strategy, step by step

The whole thing in five steps. Click through them.

1

Screen

  • Large enough US stocks (the book used the largest 3,500; above $50 million to $1 billion in its tests).
  • Leave out financials and utilities.

The formula's numbers don't make sense for banks' and utilities' balance sheets.

2

Score

  • Earnings yield: EBIT ÷ enterprise value.
  • Return on capital: EBIT ÷ (net working capital + net fixed assets).

How cheap, and how good.

3

Rank

  • Rank everything on each number, best = 1.
  • Add the two ranks; lowest total first.

Good on both beats great on one.

4

Buy

  • 5 to 7 stocks from the top of the list.
  • Repeat every two or three months until you hold 20 to 30.

No single ranking date decides everything.

5

Replace

  • Sell each stock after a year and buy from the current list.
  • Keep going for years; the book says three to five at the least.

The losing years are part of the deal.

The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It pulls TradingView's fundamental data to compute both numbers for the chart's stock, ranks it against a list of up to 7 others the way the book does, and alerts when it moves into or out of the top. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • Magic Formula Rank

    alerts

    A pane under your chart with the stock's earnings yield (gold) and return on capital (blue), stepping as each new quarter's numbers arrive. The background turns green while the stock is in the top N of your list.

    A table ranks the whole list on the combined score: both numbers, each stock's place, the top N in green and the chart's stock highlighted. It ships with seven large US stocks; swap in your own shortlist.

    EBIT is the trailing twelve months, the balance sheet the latest quarter. TradingView doesn't separate excess cash, so working capital includes all cash: a close version of the book's formula, not an exact one. Use it on stocks only, and leave banks and utilities out of the list.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.
  3. Open any timeframe chart of a US stock, then put your own shortlist into the settings (up to 7 symbols) and set how many count as the top.
  4. To get alerted, create an alert on the chart and pick Moved into the top or Dropped out of the top.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Magic Formula Rank — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/magic-formula.html
//
// Joel Greenblatt, The Little Book That Beats the Market (2005): buy good
// companies (high return on capital) at bargain prices (high earnings
// yield). Rank every stock on each, add the two ranks, buy the lowest
// totals.
//
//   Earnings yield     EBIT / enterprise value
//   Return on capital  EBIT / (net working capital + net fixed assets)
//
// A chart can't rank the whole market, so this ranks a list you give it
// (up to 7 symbols plus the chart's own stock) from TradingView's
// fundamental data, and shows where the chart's stock lands. EBIT is
// trailing twelve months; the balance sheet is the latest quarter.
// Cash is left in working capital (TradingView has no "excess cash"),
// so it's a close version of the book's formula, not an exact one.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("Magic Formula Rank", shorttitle = "Magic Formula", overlay = false)

// ---------------- inputs ----------------
grpL = "The list to rank"
s1 = input.symbol("NASDAQ:AAPL", "1", group = grpL)
s2 = input.symbol("NASDAQ:MSFT", "2", group = grpL)
s3 = input.symbol("NYSE:JNJ", "3", group = grpL)
s4 = input.symbol("NYSE:HD", "4", group = grpL)
s5 = input.symbol("NYSE:MO", "5", group = grpL)
s6 = input.symbol("NASDAQ:CSCO", "6", group = grpL)
s7 = input.symbol("NYSE:KO", "7", group = grpL)
topN    = input.int(3, "Count as top", minval = 1, maxval = 8)
showTbl = input.bool(true, "Show the table")

// ---------------- the two numbers ----------------
mf(string sym) =>
    ebit = request.financial(sym, "EBIT", "TTM", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    ev   = request.financial(sym, "ENTERPRISE_VALUE", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    ca   = request.financial(sym, "TOTAL_CURRENT_ASSETS", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    cl   = request.financial(sym, "TOTAL_CURRENT_LIABILITIES", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    ppe  = request.financial(sym, "PPE_TOTAL_NET", "FQ", ignore_invalid_symbol = true)
    nwc  = math.max(nz(ca) - nz(cl), 0)
    cap  = nwc + nz(ppe)
    ey   = not na(ebit) and ev > 0 ? ebit / ev * 100 : na
    roc  = not na(ebit) and cap > 0 ? ebit / cap * 100 : na
    [ey, roc]

syms = array.from(syminfo.tickerid, s1, s2, s3, s4, s5, s6, s7)
n = array.size(syms)
eys  = array.new<float>(n, na)
rocs = array.new<float>(n, na)
for i = 0 to n - 1
    [e, r] = mf(array.get(syms, i))
    array.set(eys, i, e)
    array.set(rocs, i, r)

// rank: 1 = best (highest). Missing data ranks last.
rankOf(array<float> a, int i) =>
    v = array.get(a, i)
    rk = 1
    for j = 0 to array.size(a) - 1
        w = array.get(a, j)
        if j != i and not na(w) and (na(v) or w > v)
            rk += 1
    rk

combo = array.new<int>(n, 0)
for i = 0 to n - 1
    array.set(combo, i, rankOf(eys, i) + rankOf(rocs, i))

// final place on the combined score (lower is better, ties share a place)
placeOf(int i) =>
    c = array.get(combo, i)
    p = 1
    for j = 0 to n - 1
        if j != i and array.get(combo, j) < c
            p += 1
    p

myPlace = placeOf(0)
inTop = myPlace <= topN
myEY  = array.get(eys, 0)
myROC = array.get(rocs, 0)

// ---------------- plots ----------------
plot(myEY, "Earnings yield %", color = #f4b942, linewidth = 2, style = plot.style_stepline)
plot(myROC, "Return on capital %", color = #2997ff, linewidth = 2, style = plot.style_stepline)
hline(0, "Zero", color = color.new(#8e8e93, 50), linestyle = hline.style_dashed)
bgcolor(inTop ? color.new(#30d158, 90) : na, title = "In the top of the list")
alertcondition(inTop and not inTop[1], "Moved into the top", "{{ticker}} moved into the top of your Magic Formula list")
alertcondition(not inTop and inTop[1], "Dropped out of the top", "{{ticker}} dropped out of the top of your Magic Formula list")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 4, 10, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if showTbl and barstate.islast
    lc = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    vc = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "#", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, "Symbol", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 2, 0, "Earn. yield", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 3, 0, "ROC", text_color = lc, text_size = size.small)
    // sort indexes by place
    idx = array.new<int>()
    for p = 1 to n
        for i = 0 to n - 1
            if placeOf(i) == p and not array.includes(idx, i)
                array.push(idx, i)
    for k = 0 to array.size(idx) - 1
        i = array.get(idx, k)
        top = k < topN
        bg = i == 0 ? color.new(#5b4a1f, 0) : top ? color.new(#173d24, 0) : color.new(#1d1d1f, 0)
        e = array.get(eys, i)
        r = array.get(rocs, i)
        table.cell(tb, 0, k + 1, str.tostring(placeOf(i)), text_color = lc, bgcolor = bg, text_size = size.small)
        table.cell(tb, 1, k + 1, i == 0 ? syminfo.ticker + " (chart)" : array.get(syms, i), text_color = vc, bgcolor = bg, text_size = size.small)
        table.cell(tb, 2, k + 1, na(e) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(e, "#.0") + "%", text_color = vc, bgcolor = bg, text_size = size.small)
        table.cell(tb, 3, k + 1, na(r) ? "n/a" : str.tostring(r, "#") + "%", text_color = vc, bgcolor = bg, text_size = size.small)

Try it on paper first.

Run a free Magic Formula screen today, write down the top 30, and pick 6 to follow. Add 6 more every three months. Check them against the S&P 500 after a year, and note how you felt in the months they lagged.

  • Fix the size cut-off and the number of stocks before you start.
  • Write down the buy date of every batch, so you know when each is due.
  • Decide now what you'll do after a losing year.
