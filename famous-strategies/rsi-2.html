RSI(2)

In a rising market, sharp two- or three-day pullbacks in an index tend to snap back. A 2-period RSI finds them. Buy when it's deeply oversold and the long-term trend is up, and get out on the first bounce. Buy the dip, but only in an uptrend.

What is the RSI(2) strategy?

RSI(2) is Larry Connors and Cesar Alvarez's pullback strategy for index ETFs like SPY and QQQ. Buy when the 2-period RSI closes at or below 5 while price is above its 200-day average, and sell on the first close above the 5-day average.

A very short RSI

The Relative Strength Index compares recent gains with recent losses. Connors' change was to look back two days instead of fourteen, which turns a slow gauge into one that hits extremes on almost every pullback.

RSI(2) next to price

One trade

The rules fit on an index card: a trend filter, an oversold entry and a simple exit.

Buy the dip, sell the bounce

1

The rule

  • Use daily bars of a broad index ETF, such as SPY or QQQ. Only buy when the close is above the 200-day simple moving average.
  • Buy when the 2-period RSI closes at or below 5. Connors tested levels from 0 to 10; the lower the reading, the better the results.
  • Sell on the first close above the 5-day simple moving average. Connors entered just before the close and used no stop loss.

Index funds hold hundreds of stocks, so short pullbacks in an uptrend are usually noise rather than news. Buying the noise and selling the bounce is a bet on that mean reversion.

Why the 200-day

Without the trend filter, the same signals would buy every leg of a bear market. The 200-day average keeps the strategy on the side of the long-term trend.

What it's like to trade

The appeal is a high win rate and short holds. The cost is the occasional dip that keeps going, with no stop in the rules to cap it.

  • Or at the next open, accepting the gap.

  • Sell on the first close above the 5-day simple moving average.
  • No profit target.

We tested it Too few trades

Works as described, but too rare to judge

It won 80% of the time on SPY and QQQ, as the book says, but it only fired about 20 times on each in five years.

  • 20trades each, SPY and QQQ
  • 80%win rate
  • +1.0%a trade, SPY
  • +0.7%a trade, QQQ

How we tested. Every US stock that traded from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, delisted ones included, so there's no survivorship bias. Company figures from SEC filings, used only from the day they were filed. 0.5 bp a side on SPY, QQQ and ETFs, 5 bp a side on single stocks. Stock returns are price only; ETF returns include dividends. Five years is short for a monthly strategy, and 2022 to 2026 was a strong large-cap market that is hard for any timing rule to beat. +1R means a trade won what it risked. The indicator

One TradingView script for this strategy (Pine Script v6). It plots the 200-day and 5-day averages, marks every RSI(2) buy and exit with the trade's gain or loss, and alerts on both. It marks and alerts. It doesn't place trades.

  • RSI(2) Pullback

    alerts

    On the chart: the 200-day average (the trend filter) and the 5-day average (the exit), a buy label with the RSI(2) reading on each signal, and an exit label with the trade's percentage gain or loss. Open trades are shaded.

    A table with today's RSI(2), whether the trend allows buys, the exit line, the open position and days held. Settings let you change the buy level, add Connors' mirror-image shorts below the 200-day, or add a time stop.

    On SPY daily bars over about the last 12 years, the default settings marked 127 trades, 80% of them winners, averaging +0.77% each, before costs and without dividends. That's our run of this indicator, not a published study.

Add it to TradingView

  1. Copy the code below, or download the .pine file.
  2. In TradingView, open the Pine Editor at the bottom of a chart, paste it in, and click Add to chart.
  3. Use a daily chart of a broad index ETF such as SPY or QQQ.
  4. To get alerted, create an alert on the chart and pick RSI(2) buy or RSI(2) exit long. Connors acted just before the close, so set the alert to fire on each update.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// RSI(2) Pullback — thetrademethod.com
// https://thetrademethod.com/famous-strategies/rsi-2.html
//
// Larry Connors & Cesar Alvarez, Short Term Trading Strategies That Work
// (2008). On a daily chart of an index ETF such as SPY or QQQ:
//
//   Trend:  only buy above the 200-day simple moving average
//   Entry:  the 2-period RSI closes at or below 5 (Connors tested 0-10)
//   Exit:   the close moves above the 5-day simple moving average
//   Stops:  Connors used none; a time limit is offered as a setting
//
// Shorts (below the 200-day, RSI(2) at or above 95) are optional.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================
indicator("RSI(2) Pullback", shorttitle = "RSI(2)", overlay = true, max_labels_count = 200)

buyLvl   = input.float(5, "Buy when RSI(2) is at or below", minval = 0, maxval = 50)
shortLvl = input.float(95, "Short when RSI(2) is at or above", minval = 50, maxval = 100)
doShort  = input.bool(false, "Also mark shorts (below the 200-day)")
maxDays  = input.int(0, "Time stop: exit after this many days (0 = off)", minval = 0)

r2    = ta.rsi(close, 2)
ma200 = ta.sma(close, 200)
ma5   = ta.sma(close, 5)
upTrend = close > ma200

var int pos = 0
var float entry = na
var int held = 0

longSig  = pos == 0 and upTrend and r2 <= buyLvl
shortSig = doShort and pos == 0 and not upTrend and r2 >= shortLvl
exitLong  = pos == 1 and (close > ma5 or (maxDays > 0 and held >= maxDays))
exitShort = pos == -1 and (close < ma5 or (maxDays > 0 and held >= maxDays))

if exitLong or exitShort
    pct = pos == 1 ? (close / entry - 1) * 100 : (entry / close - 1) * 100
    label.new(bar_index, pos == 1 ? high : low, "exit " + str.tostring(pct, "+#.0;-#.0") + "%", style = pos == 1 ? label.style_label_down : label.style_label_up, color = pct >= 0 ? color.new(#30d158, 20) : color.new(#ff453a, 20), textcolor = color.white, size = size.tiny)
    pos := 0
    entry := na
    held := 0
if longSig
    pos := 1
    entry := close
    held := 0
    label.new(bar_index, low, "RSI(2) " + str.tostring(r2, "#.0") + "\nbuy", style = label.style_label_up, color = color.new(#2997ff, 10), textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)
if shortSig
    pos := -1
    entry := close
    held := 0
    label.new(bar_index, high, "RSI(2) " + str.tostring(r2, "#.0") + "\nshort", style = label.style_label_down, color = color.new(#ff453a, 10), textcolor = color.white, size = size.small)
if pos != 0 and not longSig and not shortSig
    held += 1

plot(ma200, "200-day SMA", color = color.new(#f4b942, 0), linewidth = 2)
plot(ma5, "5-day SMA (exit)", color = color.new(#86868b, 0))
bgcolor(pos == 1 ? color.new(#2997ff, 90) : pos == -1 ? color.new(#ff453a, 90) : na, title = "In a trade")

alertcondition(longSig, "RSI(2) buy", "{{ticker}}: above the 200-day and RSI(2) at or below the buy level. Connors buys near the close.")
alertcondition(exitLong, "RSI(2) exit long", "{{ticker}}: closed above the 5-day average. Exit.")
alertcondition(shortSig, "RSI(2) short", "{{ticker}}: below the 200-day and RSI(2) at or above the short level.")
alertcondition(exitShort, "RSI(2) exit short", "{{ticker}}: closed below the 5-day average. Cover.")

var table tb = table.new(position.top_right, 2, 5, bgcolor = color.new(#1d1d1f, 0), border_width = 1, border_color = color.new(#2c2c2e, 0))
if barstate.islast
    ink = color.new(#f5f5f7, 0)
    dim = color.new(#86868b, 0)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 0, "RSI(2)", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 0, str.tostring(r2, "#.0"), text_color = r2 <= buyLvl ? color.new(#2997ff, 0) : r2 >= shortLvl ? color.new(#ff453a, 0) : ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 1, "Trend (200-day)", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 1, upTrend ? "above: buys only" : "below: no buys", text_color = upTrend ? color.new(#30d158, 0) : color.new(#ff453a, 0), text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 2, "Exit line (5-day)", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 2, str.tostring(ma5, format.mintick), text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 3, "Position", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 3, pos == 1 ? "long from " + str.tostring(entry, format.mintick) : pos == -1 ? "short from " + str.tostring(entry, format.mintick) : "flat", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 0, 4, "Days held", text_color = dim, text_size = size.small)
    table.cell(tb, 1, 4, pos != 0 ? str.tostring(held) : "–", text_color = ink, text_size = size.small)

Questions

Why a 2-period RSI?

Looking back two days instead of fourteen turns a slow gauge into one that hits extremes on almost every pullback.

Does RSI(2) use a stop?

No. Connors found stops hurt results on indexes.

Can you short with RSI(2)?

The mirror image: below the 200-day, short when RSI(2) is at or above 95.

Does RSI(2) work?

As described, but too rarely to judge in our test. It won 80% of the time on SPY and QQQ but fired only about 20 times on each in five years, making +1.0% a trade on SPY and +0.7% on QQQ.

Try it on paper first.

Put the indicator on a daily SPY chart and write down every signal for three months: the RSI(2) reading, your entry, the exit and the result. Note the worst trade and how many average winners it cost.

  • Only take signals above the 200-day.
  • Exit on the first close above the 5-day.
  • Size so the worst trade you've seen wouldn't change your plan.
