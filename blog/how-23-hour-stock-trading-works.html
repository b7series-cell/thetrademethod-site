How does 23-hour stock trading work?

The new US stock schedule from Sunday, Dec 6, the brokers that already trade overnight, and how much volume to expect. Plus the liquidity rules to check before a night trade.

The short answer

Starting Sunday, Dec 6, 2026, US stocks trade from Sunday 9 PM to Friday 8 PM Eastern, with a one-hour pause from 8 to 9 PM each weeknight. That makes each trading day 23 hours long, 9 PM to 8 PM. The new overnight session (9 PM to 4 AM) runs on Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, Cboe EDGX, MEMX and 24X, takes limit orders only, and carries only about 1% of share volume.

Data: exchange notices and rulebooks for the Dec 6, 2026 launch, SEC staff data on overnight volume (August 2026), SEC orders on overnight price bands and NSCC clearing hours, and broker help pages checked November 24, 2026. Published November 24, 2026; last updated November 24, 2026.

Key facts

  • Start: the first overnight session opens Sunday, Dec 6, 2026 at 9 PM ET.
  • Week: Sunday 9 PM to Friday 8 PM ET, with a pause from 8 to 9 PM Monday through Thursday. Saturday stays closed.
  • Day: each trading day runs 23 hours, from 9 PM the evening before to 8 PM.
  • Hours: 115 trading hours a week, up from 80 (4 AM to 8 PM, five days). Regular hours stay at 32.5 a week.
  • Venues overnight: Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, Cboe EDGX, MEMX and 24X.
  • Halts: fixed 20% price bands overnight with no automatic pauses; a stock halted overnight stays halted until 4 AM.
  • Clearing: NSCC clears overnight trades 24x5; settlement stays T+1 from the next-day trade date.
  • Unchanged: premarket (4:00 to 9:30), regular hours (9:30 to 4:00), after-hours (4:00 to 8:00), the open and close auctions, and the official close.
  • Volume: about 0.9% of US share volume traded overnight in August 2026, per SEC staff data. Overnight trades add to volume but don't set the day's last price, high or low.

Key terms

Overnight session
The new exchange session from 9 PM to 4 AM ET; Nasdaq's rulebook calls it the Night Session.
23-hour trading
The schedule from Dec 6, 2026: trading from 9 PM to 8 PM each weekday, with an 8 to 9 PM pause between trading days.
24/5 trading
The name brokers use for round-the-clock trading Sunday night to Friday night; in practice it has a short daily break.
ATS (alternative trading system)
A trading venue that is not an exchange; broker overnight trading before Dec 6 runs mostly through ATSs such as Blue Ocean.
Trade date
The day a trade is booked to; trades from 9 PM to midnight are booked to the next day.
Unpriced order
An order with no limit price, such as a market order; exchanges reject these overnight.
Liquidity
How much can trade near the current price without moving it; overnight liquidity is a small fraction of daytime liquidity.
Bid-ask spread
The gap between the best price to buy and the best price to sell; it is usually wider overnight.

How is one 23-hour trading day built?

A trading day now starts at 9 PM the evening before and ends at 8 PM. Tuesday's trading day, for example, opens Monday at 9 PM and closes Tuesday at 8 PM. The only new piece is the overnight session. Everything after it is the day you already know.

The 8 to 9 PM hour is the break between one trading day and the next. Nasdaq's rules cancel orders still resting on its book at 8 PM, start taking Night Session orders at 9 PM, and cancel anything still open at 4 AM, so day-session orders and overnight orders don't carry into each other.

How does the 23-hour trading week run?

The trading week opens Sunday at 9 PM ET with Monday's overnight session and closes Friday at 8 PM ET. There is no overnight session on Friday night or Saturday night, so the weekend break is 49 hours, from Friday 8 PM to Sunday 9 PM. Weekend news still piles up into Sunday night: stocks can now react from 9 PM Sunday instead of waiting for Monday's 4 AM premarket, three hours after stock index futures reopen at 6 PM.

Which day does an overnight trade count toward?

An overnight trade counts toward the next trading day. A buy at 10 PM Monday is a Tuesday trade, the way stock index futures already book their evening session. A buy at 2 AM Tuesday is also a Tuesday trade, because it happens on Tuesday's calendar date inside Tuesday's trading day.

Settlement follows the trade date. US stocks settle one business day after the trade date (T+1), so the 10 PM Monday trade has a Tuesday trade date and settles Wednesday. E*TRADE's overnight notes put it the same way: trades from 8 PM to 11:59 PM settle two business days from the calendar day they were placed. For day-trade counting and buying power, check how your broker books trades placed before midnight.

Overnight trades add to the next day's volume, but they don't set that day's last price, high or low. Tuesday's open is still the 9:30 auction, and Tuesday's high and low still come from the session you know.

How are overnight trades cleared and settled?

The same way as daytime trades, with the next day's trade date. The National Securities Clearing Corporation (NSCC), part of DTCC, is the central counterparty for US stock trades: it steps in as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer and guarantees settlement. Since late June 2026, NSCC's trade capture runs 24x5, from Sunday 8 PM to Friday 8 PM ET, so overnight trades are taken in and guaranteed soon after they execute instead of waiting for the morning.

  • Trade date: NSCC starts taking trades for the next trade date at about 8 PM, which is why a 10 PM Monday trade is a Tuesday trade.
  • Settlement: unchanged at T+1, one business day after the trade date. The 10 PM Monday buy settles Wednesday.
  • The 8 to 9 PM pause: the exchanges and the consolidated tape use it to close one trading day and open the next.
  • Your broker: before Dec 6, broker overnight trades ran through ATSs that already cleared through NSCC. Brokers still set their own rules on when cash from an overnight sale is usable and how a pre-midnight trade counts for day-trade limits, so check yours.

Which orders work overnight?

Only limit orders. Nasdaq's rulebook rejects unpriced orders, such as market orders, in the Night Session, and peg orders and orders meant for the opening and closing auctions aren't available either.

  • Limit orders: accepted. Price them at or inside the spread and don't chase.
  • Market orders: rejected by the exchange overnight.
  • Stop orders: generally not available overnight at the brokers listed below. Webull, for one, doesn't take stop-type orders in its overnight session.
  • Auction orders (market-on-open, limit-on-close and the like): not part of the overnight session.
  • Short sales: not accepted overnight at E*TRADE, Webull or moomoo.
  • Open orders at 4 AM: cancelled by Nasdaq at the end of the Night Session; re-enter them for the premarket.

Because stop orders mostly can't protect an overnight position, size it for the gap: the price can move past your planned stop before you can act. The Dynamic Risk Sizing page shows how to size from the stop distance.

What happens to trading halts overnight?

The overnight session has price limits but no automatic trading pauses. Under the SEC-approved overnight rules for limit up-limit down (LULD), each stock gets fixed price bands for the whole 9 PM to 4 AM session, and trades outside them are blocked. If a stock needs to stop, the exchange that lists it calls a regulatory halt by hand, and a stock halted overnight doesn't reopen until 4 AM.

The bands are published before 9 PM each night and stop at 4 AM, so morning earnings and news can move prices freely in premarket. The SEC calls this setup Phase 1: the exchanges plan to propose revised overnight protections after studying how the session trades.

What it means for a night trade: a stock can sit pinned at its band for hours with no pause to collect orders, and a halt locks you in the position until 4 AM at the earliest. That's one more reason to keep overnight size small.

What time is the overnight session outside Eastern time?

The schedule follows US Eastern time, so it shifts by an hour in other countries when US daylight saving time starts or ends. The launch on Dec 6, 2026 falls in Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5).

Which brokers offer overnight stock trading?

As of November 24, 2026, at least six large US brokers offer an overnight or 24/5 session in US stocks and ETFs: Charles Schwab (thinkorswim), Interactive Brokers, Robinhood, Webull, moomoo and E*TRADE. Fidelity's help pages list premarket and after-hours sessions but no overnight session. Each broker limits which symbols trade overnight.

Expect these hours to change. Broker overnight sessions before Dec 6 start at 8 PM because they run mostly through alternative trading systems such as Blue Ocean, not exchanges. The exchange overnight session starts at 9 PM, and each broker decides whether and when to route there. Check your broker's announcement for its post-Dec 6 hours, the symbol list, and whether stop orders or margin work overnight.

Three things I'd check in your own account before the first night trade: that overnight trading is turned on (Interactive Brokers says some accounts must enable it), that the symbol carries the broker's 24-hour marker, and how your chart's extended-hours setting shows overnight bars.

How much volume trades overnight?

Very little. In August 2026, about 0.9% of US share volume traded in the overnight session, according to SEC staff data (up sharply from a year earlier, but still under 1 share in every 100). It's also lumpy. SEC staff found that nine of the ten most-traded overnight names by share count were stocks under $1, so a big overnight share count doesn't mean real liquidity in the names most of us trade.

For scale, a stock that trades 50 million shares a day would see about 500,000 shares overnight if it got an average share, spread over seven hours. A stock that trades 2 million shares a day would see about 20,000 shares across the whole night, often in a handful of trades. Many symbols get far less than their average share, and some nights none.

  • Where to look: the largest stocks and ETFs, the names on every broker's overnight list (S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stocks, SPY, QQQ and similar funds). That's where you're most likely to find a two-sided market.
  • When it spikes: around news, such as earnings released after the close, overnight economic data and moves in Asian and European markets.
  • What changes on Dec 6: five exchanges join the ATSs that already trade overnight, so more brokers and market makers can take part. That should help, but the session starts from a thin base, and the first weeks are likely to stay thin.

The Relative Volume page explains how to compare a stock's volume to its own normal, which is the right yardstick overnight too: compare tonight's overnight volume to that stock's past overnight sessions, not to its daytime volume.

What should you check before an overnight trade?

Check four things before entering at night: that the symbol is a large, widely traded name, that it has actually traded tonight, that the spread is tight, and that your order is small next to what's there. The table gives starting numbers. These are my starting rules for thin markets, not tested results. I'll tighten or loosen them once real overnight fills come in, and you should do the same with yours.

Starting liquidity rules for an overnight trade
CheckStarting ruleWhy
SymbolAn S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 stock, or a large ETF such as SPY, QQQ or IWMThese are on every broker's overnight list and draw most overnight volume
Volume tonightAt least 50,000 shares traded in the session before you enterShows real two-way trading tonight, not one stale quote
Spread0.10% of price or less; skip above 0.25%A $100 stock with a 25-cent spread costs 0.25% just to get in and out
Order sizeNo more than 1% of tonight's volume so far, and no more than the size shown at the bid or askLarger orders walk the book and move the price against you
Position sizeOne quarter to one half of your regular-hours sizeNo stop orders and thin books mean bigger slippage when wrong
Order typeLimit, at or inside the spreadThe only order type exchanges take overnight

Worked example: at 11 PM, a Nasdaq-100 stock has traded 80,000 shares overnight and is quoted $200.00 bid, $200.12 ask (a 0.06% spread) with 300 shares on each side. The size cap is 300 shares, the smaller of 1% of 80,000 (800 shares) and the 300 shown. If your regular position is 1,000 shares, the quarter-to-half rule puts tonight's size at 250 to 500 shares, so 250 to 300 shares, with a limit order at or inside $200.12.

What does 23-hour trading change for your levels?

Very little for levels built on premarket and regular hours. Those windows and both auctions stay exactly where they are.

The overnight session does add a new reference, the overnight high and low, much like the evening session high and low in stock index futures. A stock can now react to 11 PM news hours before premarket, so by 4 AM part of a gap may already have traded. Setups like Gap Give and Go still work off the premarket gap; just know some of it may have traded overnight. The 24-hour stock trading note sums up the change on one page.

How I trade the overnight session

I already trade the overnight session on Webull, but very small. Sometimes I only use it to enter or exit a position, then manage the rest in premarket or regular hours. The reason is volume. On most nights there isn't much size behind the quotes, so anything bigger than a small order moves the price on me.

I think Dec 6 helps. Right now most overnight trading runs through one alternative trading system. When Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, Cboe EDGX, MEMX and 24X open their own overnight sessions, more brokers and market makers can take part, and that should mean more volume and tighter spreads over time. I don't expect that to happen in the first week.

Until it does, I'll keep doing what I do now: small size, limit orders, and only the large names that pass the checks above. I'll update this page after the first overnight week with what I actually see in the volume and spreads.

What the rules and research say

“Unpriced orders are not permitted during the Night Session.”

The Nasdaq Stock Market, Nasdaq Rulebook, Equity 4, 2026.

Research on the overnight market before the exchanges joined points the same way as the volume numbers. Gregory Eaton, Andriy Shkilko and Ingrid Werner study the 8 PM to 4 AM session in “Nocturnal Trading” (presented at the 2026 American Economic Association meetings). They find that a single platform dominated the session with only a few liquidity providers, that each trade moved prices several times more than a trade of the same kind during regular hours, and that meaningful price discovery still happened overnight. In plain terms: overnight prices carry real information, but each order pushes the price more, which is why size and spread matter more at night.

Frequently asked questions

When does 23-hour stock trading start?

23-hour US stock trading starts Sunday, Dec 6, 2026 at 9 PM Eastern. From then on, stocks trade from Sunday 9 PM to Friday 8 PM Eastern, with a one-hour pause from 8 to 9 PM each weeknight.

Is the US stock market open 24 hours a day now?

No. The US stock market is open 23 hours each weekday from Dec 6, 2026: 9 PM to 8 PM Eastern, with an 8 to 9 PM pause. It closes from Friday 8 PM to Sunday 9 PM, so there is no weekend trading.

Which brokers offer overnight stock trading?

As of November 24, 2026, Charles Schwab (on thinkorswim), Interactive Brokers, Robinhood, Webull, moomoo and E*TRADE offer overnight or 24/5 trading in a list of US stocks and ETFs. Fidelity lists premarket and after-hours sessions but no overnight session. Each broker sets its own hours and symbol list.

How much volume trades in the overnight session?

About 0.9% of US share volume traded overnight in August 2026, according to SEC staff data. The most usable liquidity is in large stocks and ETFs such as S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 names, SPY and QQQ. Many smaller stocks trade little or nothing overnight.

Can you use market or stop orders in the overnight session?

No market orders: Nasdaq rejects unpriced orders in its Night Session, and brokers take limit orders only overnight. Stop orders are generally not available overnight either, so overnight positions should be sized for a gap past the planned stop.

Which day does a trade at 10 PM count toward?

A trade between 9 PM and midnight counts toward the next trading day. A 10 PM Monday trade is a Tuesday trade and, with T+1 settlement, settles Wednesday.

Does overnight trading change the premarket high or the open?

No. Premarket still runs 4:00 to 9:30 Eastern, the open is still the 9:30 auction, and overnight trades don't set the day's last price, high or low.

What time does the overnight session start in Pacific time?

The overnight session runs 6 PM to 1 AM Pacific (9 PM to 4 AM Eastern). The trading week opens Sunday at 6 PM Pacific and closes Friday at 5 PM Pacific, with a pause from 5 to 6 PM Pacific on weeknights.

Are there trading halts in the overnight session?

Not automatic ones. From Dec 6, 2026, each stock has fixed overnight price bands, 20% beyond its reference prices, and trades outside them are blocked with no 5-minute pause. The listing exchange can still call a regulatory halt, and a stock halted overnight stays halted until at least 4 AM Eastern.

Who clears overnight stock trades?

The National Securities Clearing Corporation (NSCC), part of DTCC, the same central counterparty that clears daytime trades. It has run 24x5 trade capture since June 2026, from Sunday 8 PM to Friday 8 PM Eastern, and overnight trades still settle T+1 from their next-day trade date.
