Does the hitchhiker scalp work?

Five years of 2-minute bars, 10 large-cap stocks and 2,291 trades of SMB Capital's first-pullback scalp. What held up, what didn't, and the one filter worth a closer look.

The short answer

Not on its own. Tested on 2,291 trades over five years of 2-minute bars, the hitchhiker scalp (the first pullback to the 9 EMA after a strong move, stopped on a close past the 21 EMA) made +0.01R per trade after costs, a profit factor of 1.03. That is break-even. A few slices did better, but none clearly enough to call an edge.

Data: The Trade Method backtest of the Hitchhiker Scalp rules on 2-minute bars of 10 large-cap US stocks, November 2021 to November 2026, plus a TradingView run of the indicator on AMD, September 9 to November 20, 2026. Published November 25, 2026; last updated November 25, 2026.

In this article

The rules as I coded them, five years of results, and how they split by side, hour, year and stock. Then where the profit really came from, a check in TradingView, and the one change I think is worth testing next.

Why I tested it

I tested the hitchhiker scalp because it's one of the most widely taught pullback setups, and I wanted to know if the rules held up on their own before putting real money behind them. The first-hour result matches what I expected: the good pullbacks come right after the open, when the volume is there.

From here I'm only taking hitchhiker longs before 10:30, and I'm tracking every trade. I'll update this post once I have 50 of my own.

Key results

  • Overall: 2,291 trades, 40% winners, profit factor 1.03, +0.01R per trade, +27.9R in total over five years.
  • Training vs holdout: −0.003R per trade before June 2024 (1,083 trades) and +0.026R after it (1,208 trades). Both are close to zero.
  • Side: shorts +0.023R per trade, longs +0.002R.
  • Time of day: entries from 9:30 to 10:30 made +0.018R per trade; entries after 10:30 lost −0.021R.
  • Stocks: 6 of 10 were positive. HOOD (+0.12R) and META (+0.08R) were best; MSFT and AMD (both −0.06R) were worst.
  • Drawdown: the running total fell 46R from its September 2023 peak to its October 2025 low before recovering.
  • Shape: the median trade lost 0.23R. The best 5% of trades made +237R; the other 95% lost about 209R.

Key terms

Hitchhiker scalp
An intraday momentum setup taught by SMB Capital: after a stock in play makes an outsized move on heavy volume, wait for the first pullback instead of chasing, then ride the next leg.
9 EMA and 21 EMA
Exponential moving averages of the last 9 and 21 bars. In the hitchhiker scalp the 9 EMA marks the entry area and a bar closing past the 21 EMA ends the trade.
R
The amount risked on a trade. +1R means the trade made what it risked; −0.5R means it lost half of it.
Profit factor
Total won divided by total lost. 1.0 is break-even; 1.03 means the winners covered the losers with 3% to spare.
Holdout
The later part of the data, kept apart from the period the rules were checked on, used to see if a result still holds on data it has not seen.
Drawdown
The drop in the running total from a high point to the lowest point after it.

The rules that were tested

The test coded the rules from The Trade Method's Hitchhiker Scalp page at its default settings, long and short:

  1. The move: a fast, one-directional run on the 2-minute chart, well away from the 9 EMA, on volume well above normal.
  2. The pullback: the first pullback to the 9 EMA after that move, holding the 21 EMA, ideally on lighter volume than the move.
  3. The entry: as the pullback slows and turns, a tick past the prior bar.
  4. The stop: a 2-minute close past the 21 EMA.
  5. The exit: 70% sold in small pieces into new highs (or lows), the last 30% trailed on the prior 2-minute bar.

Fills were at the bar close, 0.5 basis points a side were charged for the spread, and any single trade was capped at +10R. Every entry in the data came between 9:34 and 11:22 Eastern, because that is when the big opening moves the setup needs show up.

Those costs are light for a 2-minute scalp. Real stop entries and stop-outs usually fill a cent or more worse than the bar shows, and with the stop often close to the entry, that slippage alone would likely push +0.01R per trade below zero.

One limit matters: the setup is meant for a stock in play, one with abnormal volume, a large range and a reason to move that day. A backtest can't pick those the way a trader does each morning, so the test ran on the same 10 liquid stocks every day (AAPL, AMD, COIN, GOOG, HOOD, META, MSFT, MU, NVDA and TSLA).

How the hitchhiker scalp results moved over time

The running total ended five years at +27.9R, but the path matters more than the end point. The hitchhiker scalp was up 28.6R by September 2023, gave all of it back and more by October 2025 (−17.8R), then made it back in 2026.

Long or short, early or late

Shorts did a little better than longs (+0.023R against +0.002R per trade), and entries in the first hour did better than later ones (+0.018R against −0.021R). Both gaps point the same way traders expect, since the strongest moves and the heaviest volume come right after the open. Neither gap is large enough to stand on its own: 0.02R per trade is about the size of a slightly worse fill.

The time-of-day split is still the most useful finding here. First-hour entries were positive in both halves of the data (+0.008R per trade in training, +0.028R in the holdout). Later entries lost overall, mostly from late longs (−0.15R per trade over 170 trades), so skipping hitchhiker longs after 10:30 cost nothing in this test. Late shorts went the other way, about +0.09R per trade on 196 trades, which is too small a sample to build on.

Where the hitchhiker scalp's profit came from

The typical hitchhiker trade lost money. The median trade was −0.23R, and 6 of every 10 trades were losers, most of them small, as the pullback failed and price closed back past the 21 EMA. The 70/30 exit is built for the few trades that keep running: 36 trades made +2R or more, the best made +7.3R, and the top 5% of trades (about 115) added up to +237R. The other 95% lost about 209R between them.

That shape has two practical effects. Skipping a few trades, or cutting a runner early, can turn the whole result negative. And the stop is a close past the 21 EMA, not a fixed price, so 159 trades lost more than 1R when a bar closed well beyond the average. Sizing on the distance to the 21 EMA, as the setup page shows, is what keeps those losses close to 1R.

The AMD check in TradingView

A separate run of the Hitchhiker Scalp indicator in TradingView on AMD 2-minute bars, from September 9 to November 20, 2026, gave a similar first read: 62 trades, 40% winners and −12.1R, partly because that first version could hold a trade overnight. Two changes were then added to the indicator as options: close any open trade at the end of the day (now on by default), and trade the first hour only.

  • Flat at the close: 64 trades, −11.7R.
  • Flat at the close and first hour only: 33 trades, 58% winners, +1.8R.

That lines up with the five-year test, where first-hour entries were the better half. It is still 33 trades on one stock over about ten weeks, far too few to prove anything by itself.

How to use the hitchhiker scalp

As tested, the hitchhiker scalp is a break-even setup. That is not the same as useless: it describes a sound habit (don't chase, wait for the first pullback, define the risk at the 21 EMA), and break-even before a trader's own stock selection leaves room for that selection to add something. What the data does not support is trading every 9 EMA pullback on a fixed list of stocks and expecting it to pay.

  • Skip longs after 10:30. Late longs lost −0.15R per trade over 170 trades. Late shorts did better (about +0.09R on 196 trades), but that is too few to rely on, so first-hour trades remain the core.
  • Pick the stock in play. The test couldn't, and the more volatile names did better, so this is where a trader's own work has to come in.
  • Keep the runner. The result lives in the top 5% of trades; the trailed 30% is what catches them.
  • Size on the 21 EMA. The stop is a close, so the distance to the 21 EMA is the real risk.
  • Track it before sizing up. A 46R drawdown was part of the five-year path; small size until a trader's own record shows an edge.

What the research says

“We prove that high simulated performance is easily achievable after backtesting” a relatively small number of strategy configurations.

David H. Bailey, Jonathan Borwein, Marcos López de Prado and Qiji Jim Zhu, “Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance”, Notices of the American Mathematical Society 61(5), 2014.

This article cut one test seven ways (side, hour, year, stock and more), and some cuts look good: HOOD at +0.12R per trade, 2026 at +0.09R, the AMD first-hour run at +1.8R. That is the trap Bailey and his co-authors describe. Slice any break-even result enough times and some slices will look like an edge. The first-hour filter is the one worth testing on its own, because there is a reason behind it (the opening moves carry the volume the setup needs) and it showed up in two separate runs, but it needs new data before it counts.

Frequently asked questions

Is the hitchhiker scalp profitable?

Not on its own in testing. Over 2,291 trades on 2-minute bars of 10 large-cap stocks from November 2021 to November 2026, the hitchhiker scalp made +0.01R per trade after costs, with a profit factor of 1.03 and a 40% win rate. That is break-even.

What is the hitchhiker scalp?

The hitchhiker scalp is an intraday momentum setup taught by SMB Capital. After a stock in play makes an outsized move on heavy volume, the trader waits for the first pullback to the 9 EMA on the 2-minute chart, enters as the pullback slows, exits on a 2-minute close past the 21 EMA, takes 70% into momentum and trails the last 30%.

What time of day is best for the hitchhiker scalp?

The first hour after the open. In the five-year test, hitchhiker entries from 9:30 to 10:30 Eastern made +0.018R per trade, while entries after 10:30 lost 0.021R per trade. A separate TradingView run on AMD also did better when limited to the first hour. The losses after 10:30 came from longs; late shorts made about +0.09R per trade on 196 trades, too few to rely on.

Does the hitchhiker scalp work better long or short?

Shorts did slightly better in testing: +0.023R per trade on 1,111 short trades against +0.002R on 1,180 long trades. The gap is too small to rely on.

What is the win rate of the hitchhiker scalp?

About 40%. The hitchhiker scalp depends on a few large winners: the median trade lost 0.23R, while the best 5% of trades made +237R in total, enough to cover the other 95%.

Why use the 21 EMA for the hitchhiker stop?

The 21 EMA marks where the move's trend is broken. A pullback that holds above it is still a pullback; a 2-minute close beyond it means the move has likely failed. Because the stop is a close, losses can run past 1R, so position size should be based on the distance to the 21 EMA.

Is there a TradingView indicator for the hitchhiker scalp?

Yes. The Trade Method's Hitchhiker Scalp indicator (Pine Script v6) finds the move, waits for the first pullback to the 9 EMA, checks pullback volume, marks entries and stops, and has options to close flat at the end of the day and trade the first hour only. It is free on the Hitchhiker Scalp setup page.
