Does the hitchhiker scalp work?

Five years of 2-minute bars, 10 large-cap stocks plus SPY and QQQ, and 16,136 trades of SMB Capital's first-pullback scalp, run through the site's own indicator. What held up, what didn't, and the one filter worth a closer look.

The short answer

Not on its own. Tested on 16,136 trades over five years of 2-minute bars, the hitchhiker scalp (the first pullback to the 9 EMA after a strong move, stopped on a close past the 21 EMA) made +0.006R per trade after 0.5 bp a side in costs, a profit factor of 1.01. That is break-even, and at 1 bp a side it loses money. The best version, first-hour entries after a strong-volume move, made +0.03R per trade, still too thin to call an edge.

Data: The Trade Method backtest of the Hitchhiker Scalp indicator at its default settings on 2-minute bars of 10 large-cap US stocks plus SPY and QQQ, November 2021 to November 2026, plus a TradingView run of the indicator on AMD, September 9 to November 20, 2026. Why I tested it

I tested the hitchhiker scalp because it's one of the most widely taught pullback setups, and I wanted to know if the rules held up on their own before putting real money behind them. The first-hour result matches what I expected: the good pullbacks come right after the open, when the volume is there.

From here I'm only taking hitchhiker longs before 10:30, and I'm tracking every trade. I'll update this post once I have 50 of my own.

Hitchhiker scalp key results

  • Overall: 16,136 trades, 46% winners, profit factor 1.01, +0.006R per trade, +93R in total over five years.
  • Training vs holdout: +0.017R per trade before June 2024 (8,109 trades) and −0.006R after it (8,027 trades). Both are close to zero.
  • Side: shorts +0.009R per trade, longs +0.003R.
  • Time of day: entries from 9:30 to 10:30 made +0.025R per trade; entries after 10:30 lost −0.011R.

Key terms

Hitchhiker scalp
An intraday momentum setup taught by SMB Capital: after a stock in play makes an outsized move on heavy volume, wait for the first pullback instead of chasing, then ride the next leg.
9 EMA and 21 EMA
Exponential moving averages of the last 9 and 21 bars. In the hitchhiker scalp the 9 EMA marks the entry area and a bar closing past the 21 EMA ends the trade.
R
The amount risked on a trade. +1R means the trade made what it risked; −0.5R means it lost half of it.
Profit factor
Total won divided by total lost. 1.0 is break-even; 1.01 means the winners covered the losers with 1% to spare.
Holdout
The later part of the data, kept apart from the period the rules were checked on, used to see if a result still holds on data it has not seen.
Drawdown
The drop in the running total from a high point to the lowest point after it.

The hitchhiker scalp rules that were tested

The test ran The Trade Method's Hitchhiker Scalp indicator, ported line for line, at its default settings, long and short:

  1. The move: price travels at least 2.5 ATRs in 6 bars on the 2-minute chart, at least 4 of them in its direction, on 1.5× normal volume, and ends at least 0.8 ATR from the 9 EMA.
  2. The pullback: the first pullback to the 9 EMA after that move, holding the 21 EMA on closes, within 12 bars. It is skipped if its volume runs heavier than the move's.
  3. The entry: as the pullback turns, a tick past the prior bar's high (low for shorts). A bar that opens beyond that fills at its open.
  4. The stop: a 2-minute close past the 21 EMA, tightened to the prior bar once the move's extreme is taken out.
  5. The exit: 70% sold in 5% pieces into new highs (or lows) while in profit, the last 30% trailed on the prior 2-minute bar, and anything open is closed on the last bar of the day.

0.5 basis points a side were charged for the spread, and any single trade was capped at ±10R. Entries came all through the session; about half came after 11:30 Eastern.

Those costs are light for a 2-minute scalp, where the stop is often close to the entry. Rerun with the same rules at higher costs, the small edge disappears: at 1 bp a side the result drops to −0.038R per trade, and at 2 bp a side to −0.125R per trade (profit factor 0.76). Even the best version, first hour with strong volume, falls to +0.004R at 1 bp.

One limit matters: the setup is meant for a stock in play, one with abnormal volume, a large range and a reason to move that day. A backtest can't pick those the way a trader does each morning, so the test ran on the same 12 liquid symbols every day (AAPL, AMD, COIN, GOOG, HOOD, META, MSFT, MU, NVDA and TSLA, plus SPY and QQQ).

How the hitchhiker scalp results moved over time

The running total ended five years at +93R, but the path matters more than the end point. The hitchhiker scalp was up 276R by January 2023, then gave back 235R of it by August 2024. One day, April 9, 2025, when the market jumped on the tariff pause, added +54R on its own.

Hitchhiker scalp: long or short, early or late

Shorts did a little better than longs (+0.009R against +0.003R per trade), and entries in the first hour did better than later ones (+0.025R against −0.011R). Both gaps point the same way traders expect, since the strongest moves and the heaviest volume come right after the open.

The time-of-day split is the most useful finding here. First-hour entries were flat in training (−0.000R per trade) and positive in the holdout (+0.050R), and they were positive on both sides (longs +0.025R, shorts +0.025R). Later entries lost on both sides (longs −0.017R over 4,409 trades, shorts −0.004R over 4,242), so skipping trades after 10:30 cost nothing in this test.

The indicator has switches for this, and for the two things the video says to pass on, so each was run the same way:

Where the hitchhiker scalp's profit came from

The typical hitchhiker trade lost money. The median trade was −0.11R, and 54% of trades were losers, most of them small, as the pullback failed and price closed back past the 21 EMA. The 70/30 exit is built for the trades that keep running: 839 trades made +2R or more, 17 hit the +10R cap, and the top 5% of trades (807 of them) added up to +2,735R. The other 95% lost about 2,642R between them.

That shape has two practical effects. Skipping a few trades, or cutting a runner early, can turn the whole result negative. And the stop is a close past the 21 EMA, not a fixed price, while the risk is measured to the 21 EMA at entry, which can be only a few cents away. One trade in five (3,242) lost more than 1R. Sizing on the distance to the 21 EMA, with room for a close beyond it, is what keeps those losses in check.

The hitchhiker scalp AMD check in TradingView

A separate run of the Hitchhiker Scalp indicator in TradingView on AMD 2-minute bars, from September 9 to November 20, 2026, gave a similar first read: 62 trades, 40% winners and −12.1R, partly because that first version could hold a trade overnight. Two changes were then added to the indicator as options: close any open trade at the end of the day (now on by default), and trade the first hour only.

  • Flat at the close: 64 trades, −11.7R.
  • Flat at the close and first hour only: 33 trades, 58% winners, +1.8R.

That lines up with the five-year test, where first-hour entries were the better half. It is still 33 trades on one stock over about ten weeks, far too few to prove anything by itself.

How to use the hitchhiker scalp

As tested, the hitchhiker scalp is a break-even setup. That is not the same as useless: it describes a sound habit (don't chase, wait for the first pullback, define the risk at the 21 EMA), and break-even before a trader's own stock selection leaves room for that selection to add something. What the data does not support is trading every 9 EMA pullback on a fixed list of stocks and expecting it to pay.

  • Skip trades after 10:30. Late entries lost on both sides over 8,651 trades. First-hour trades, on a strong-volume move, are the core.
  • Pick the stock in play. The test couldn't, and the more volatile names did better while SPY and QQQ did worse, so this is where a trader's own work has to come in.
  • Keep the runner. The result lives in the top 5% of trades; the trailed 30% is what catches them.
  • Size on the 21 EMA. The stop is a close, so the distance to the 21 EMA is the real risk, and one trade in five lost more than 1R.
  • Track it before sizing up. A 235R drawdown was part of the five-year path; small size until a trader's own record shows an edge.

What the research says about results like the hitchhiker scalp's

“We prove that high simulated performance is easily achievable after backtesting” a relatively small number of strategy configurations.

David H. Bailey, Jonathan Borwein, Marcos López de Prado and Qiji Jim Zhu, “Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance”, Notices of the American Mathematical Society 61(5), 2014.

This article cut one test seven ways (side, hour, year, stock and more), and some cuts look good: COIN at +0.08R per trade, 2022 at +0.08R, the AMD first-hour run at +1.8R. That is the trap Bailey and his co-authors describe. Slice any break-even result enough times and some slices will look like an edge. The first-hour filter is the one worth testing on its own, because there is a reason behind it (the opening moves carry the volume the setup needs) and it showed up in both halves of the data and in a separate run, but it needs new data before it counts.

Frequently asked questions

Is the hitchhiker scalp profitable?

Not on its own in testing. Over 16,136 trades on 2-minute bars of 10 large-cap stocks plus SPY and QQQ from November 2021 to November 2026, the hitchhiker scalp indicator made +0.006R per trade after 0.5 bp a side in costs, with a profit factor of 1.01 and a 46% win rate. That is break-even, and at 1 bp a side or more it loses money (−0.038R per trade at 1 bp).

What is the hitchhiker scalp?

The hitchhiker scalp is an intraday momentum setup taught by SMB Capital. After a stock in play makes an outsized move on heavy volume, the trader waits for the first pullback to the 9 EMA on the 2-minute chart, enters as the pullback slows, exits on a 2-minute close past the 21 EMA, takes 70% into momentum and trails the last 30%.

What time of day is best for the hitchhiker scalp?

The first hour after the open. In the five-year test, hitchhiker entries from 9:30 to 10:30 Eastern made +0.025R per trade, while entries after 10:30 lost 0.011R per trade, on both longs and shorts. First-hour entries after a move on 2.5 times normal volume made +0.03R per trade. A separate TradingView run on AMD also did better when limited to the first hour.

Does the hitchhiker scalp work better long or short?

Shorts did slightly better in testing: +0.009R per trade on 7,766 short trades against +0.003R on 8,370 long trades. The gap is too small to rely on.

What is the win rate of the hitchhiker scalp?

About 46%. The hitchhiker scalp depends on a few large winners: the median trade lost 0.11R, while the best 5% of trades made +2,735R in total, enough to cover the other 95%.

Why use the 21 EMA for the hitchhiker stop?

The 21 EMA marks where the move's trend is broken. A pullback that holds above it is still a pullback; a 2-minute close beyond it means the move has likely failed. Because the stop is a close, losses can run past 1R, so position size should be based on the distance to the 21 EMA.

Is there a TradingView indicator for the hitchhiker scalp?

Yes. The Trade Method's Hitchhiker Scalp indicator (Pine Script v6) finds the move, waits for the first pullback to the 9 EMA, checks pullback volume, marks entries and stops, and has options to close flat at the end of the day, trade the first hour only, take strong-volume moves only, and skip chop or mid-range stocks. It is free on the Hitchhiker Scalp setup page.
