What the tests taught us

  • Beat random, not zero

    Long trend rules on big stocks in a bull market make money almost no matter where you enter. A setup only earns its keep if it beats random entries with the same exits.

  • Costs eat fast charts

    On 1 to 5-minute bars, a cent of spread is a big share of a tight stop. Several setups were positive before costs and negative after.

  • Look inside the bar

    3-Candle CRT looked like +0.21R until the test checked what happened inside each bar. Then it lost. Coarse bars flatter tight stops.

  • Win rate isn't edge

    Volume S/R Rejection won 62% of its trades and still lost a fifth of its risk on each one.

How we tested

Setups ran on SPY, QQQ and 10 large-cap stocks (AAPL, AMD, COIN, GOOG, HOOD, META, MSFT, MU, NVDA, TSLA), mostly on five years of 1-minute to daily bars from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026, with a few earlier TradingView runs back to 2010. Each rule set was coded from the page or its indicator at default settings. Fills at the bar close, the stop wins when stop and target hit in the same bar, and 0.5 to 1 bp a side covers the spread. Results are in R, capped at +10R a trade, and split into a training half (before June 2024) and a holdout half. Wherever we could, the same exits ran on random entries, or the same rules on fake levels, as a benchmark.

Famous strategies ran on every US stock that traded in those five years, delisted ones included, with company figures from SEC filings used only from the day they were filed.

The limits: the 10 stocks were picked with hindsight, five years is short, and some setups (order flow, catalysts, tape) can't be tested from bars. Each page lists its own caveats. Past results don't promise future ones, and nothing here is trading advice.
