Step one: build the levels on the higher time frames

Levels come from the weekly, daily, 4-hour and hourly charts, and from nothing lower. If you're going to take an intraday trade, it should come off a major inflection point, not a little pivot from yesterday.

From lows to a zone

1

A zone, not a line

  • Start with the clearest daily low. Then find the other lows close to it.
  • Put the zone between the lowest low and the highest of those nearby lows.
  • Keep it within reason: about 20 to 30 points on the S&P, about 100 on the Nasdaq. A 200-point "demand zone" isn't a zone.
  • The more touches, the better. A low that held three or four times over six months is a huge daily inflection point.

One line at the extreme low would leave you blind to a major low sitting just above it on your intraday chart.

2

Less work than it sounds

  • Skip levels above a high that's clearly rejecting. If price gets above it, add levels the next day.
  • The major highs and lows stay on the chart until they break. Most mornings it's only small adjustments inside the range.
  • He uses horizontal lines, not trend lines.

The morning run-through

Coffee, then the same order every day. Levels first, then a feel for which sectors are strong and which are weak, because money rotates almost daily.

  • Futures and ETFs: Nasdaq future, S&P future, SPY, QQQ, IWM, SMH.
  • Equities: Tesla and Nvidia, then semiconductors, memory names, large-cap tech, the neoclouds, healthcare, software, financials.
  • Macro: gold and the 10-year.

Adjusted or not adjusted?

He keeps the futures chart adjusted for the contract change and watches the ETF beside it, which never adjusts: the S&P next to SPY, the Nasdaq next to QQQ. If they disagree at a level, that's worth knowing. On Friday's trade the QQQ rejecting its previous day low was the guide for the Nasdaq short.

The story and the game plan

3

Zoom out before you panic

  • After a big down day it's easy to think the market is about to tank. On the daily, the S&P was just chopping sideways above a low it had defended three or four times.
  • That's status quo, not a bear market. A break and failure below those major lows would change the story.
  • Short term, the structure is what flips first: lower highs under 7,600 after the bulls lost the key lows.

The higher time frame sets the macro idea. The intraday chart is only where you act on it.

Only the candles inside colored zones

Levels remove the noise. Instead of watching every candle that prints, you only care about candles inside the zones. "I only care what happens at 7,600 to 7,590. I only care what happens at 7,490 to 7,469."

at a zoneFull attention

Watch for the hold, the fail or the trap, and be ready to act.

between zonesLeave it alone

No cute setups on a one-minute chart in the middle of nowhere.

Step three: at the level, hold, fail, or trap?

Getting to the level isn't the signal. Price has to show you something there, and very often it plays a game first: it breaks the level, traps the people who reacted, and then gets back above it.

The sweep and the reclaim

4

Expect the games

  • Buying just because price is at the level, in a downtrend, is what blew him up early on.
  • Now he walks into every day expecting something tricky at the zone: a trap, a sweep, something to wait for.
  • His favorite trade of all is the sweep and reclaim, which he calls the "reclaim ripper". It's a form of break and retest.

Traps and sweeps came into his system over the last two years. It's an advanced read, and it only works because the levels are good.

Once a level breaks, look for one of two things

with the breakRetest and reject

The level breaks, price comes back to it from the other side and gets rejected. Trade in the direction of the break.

against the breakReclaim and hold

The level breaks, price gets back through it, retests it and holds with a higher low. Trade the reclaim.

If you don't chase the break, he says, something clean usually sets up later in the session.

Step four: the break and retest, after confirmation

The break and retest is the entry model, and it only works at the inflection points from steps one to three. The hard part isn't seeing it, it's waiting for it.

5

The 7,490 long from the stream

  • The S&P pulled back into the hourly 7,469 to 7,490 zone.
  • On the hourly it started to hold: a bounce, higher lows, buyers stepping up at 7,490. That's the break and retest of the key level.
  • Entry there, stop under the level (or lower, at 7,470), target back up into the highs.
  • It ran almost 100 points on the ES. No fancy words, just the level and the price action at it.

The vehicle doesn't matter. The same level held on SPY around 744 to 745 for a swing in shares or options.

6

Can you enter on the break?

  • You can, but he'd strongly advise against it. The break is where the traps happen.
  • The entry belongs at or after the level starts to hold and confirms it's holding.

Idea vs entry

Two separate grades. The idea can be S tier, the best trade you could ask for, and the entry can still be early. Entering before the retest confirms is called front-running. It can pay well, and it has very high negatives when it doesn't.

front-runningIn before the retest

More drawdown, a worse reward-to-risk, and no confirmation behind it.

confirmedIn after the hold

Stop right under the retest candle's low. A better reward-to-risk and a better win rate.

"You didn't have to get in early. You just had to be patient." — Vincent Desiano

Step five: manage it with intraday structure

Friday was a trending day down, and a lesson in what not to do as much as what to do. No level, no trade. Don't short into the 200 or the previous day low. Short the break and retest of the PDL, then let the structure tell you when to get out.

Break and retest a PDL, then hold it

7

Random shots get rewarded, until they don't

  • On a day like Friday, someone who just thinks "it's going down, I'm short" can outperform an experienced trader.
  • That behavior gets rewarded often, which is the problem. There's no system to stop it when it goes wrong, and it gives everything back and then some.
  • A break and retest of the previous day low is repeatable. A random short into a 200 moving average is a house of cards.

The more you know, the more cautious you get. That isn't a bad thing.

8

What is price actually doing?

  • Instead of panicking out of a position, look at the structure: lows, highs, lows, highs.
  • Long: hold while price stays above the last higher low and keeps stepping up. Short: hold while it makes lower highs under the EMA cloud.
  • When price holds back above a previous low (on a short), the structure is gone. That's the exit.
  • This is for trending markets only.

An experiment: the first candle low

Something he's been watching more and more this year: mark the low of the first one-minute candle of the session. It has kept showing up as an inflection, including Friday's rejections. He's clear it won't work every time and asks people to watch it with him and report back. Treat it as a level to observe, not a rule.

The last piece: self-control

Levels and price action are the whole system. The third piece, and the hardest, is battling yourself: not self-sabotaging, not chasing, not front-running. It's the part he says everyone, him included, keeps working on.

Every morning, thesis to trade

The whole routine in five steps. Click through them.

1

Build the levels

  • Weekly, daily, 4-hour, hourly only. Group the lowest low and the lows near it into one zone.
  • Keep the big zones until they break. Skip levels above a high that's clearly rejecting.

Good levels come first. Nothing after this works without them.

2

Think through them

  • Read the structure: highs turning into lows, or a lower high under a level.
  • Write the plan for each zone: if it pushes in and fails, fade it to the next zone. If it never gets there, no trade.

A line isn't a plan. The story is.

3

Read the price action there

  • Only at the zones. Is it holding, failing, or setting a trap?
  • Expect a sweep. A break and reclaim is often the best thing that can happen.

Use all your attention at the inflection points and none on the slide between them.

4

Break and retest entry

  • Retest and reject, or reclaim and hold. Enter after it confirms, never on the break.
  • Stop under the retest candle's low (or above its high on a short).

The idea and the entry are two different grades. Don't front-run an S-tier idea.

5

Manage, and control yourself

  • In a trend, hold while the structure holds and price stays on the right side of the EMA cloud. Exit when it breaks.
  • Then the hard part: no chasing, no random shots, no self-sabotage.

Levels, price action, self-control. Every day, the same thing.

Build the levels. Watch them. Wait.

Most traders would do better trading only the major inflection points: if price gets to the zone, look for the trade, and if it doesn't, see you tomorrow. The skill is patience for the level, patience for the retest, and patience for it to develop.

  • Levels from the weekly, daily, 4-hour and hourly. Nothing lower.
  • Group nearby lows into a zone: 20–30 points on the ES.
  • Write the story for each zone before the open.
  • Between zones, leave it alone.
  • At a zone, expect a trap. Wait for the hold or the reclaim.
  • Enter on the confirmed retest, never on the break.
  • Hold with the structure; exit when it breaks.
