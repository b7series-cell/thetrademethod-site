Six Momentum Trades

Most strategy names aren't different trades. They're different ways of describing six things price can actually do. Learn the six mechanisms and you stop asking which pattern this is. You start asking what the market is actually doing.

Why strategy names get confusing

The list of names keeps getting longer and the trading doesn't get better, because most names mix four different kinds of information and call every one of them a setup. Some name a place. Some name a shape. Some name a behavior. Only a few name the trade.

a locationVWAP pullback

VWAP is where it happens, an opportunity zone. The trade underneath is a pullback continuation.

a locationHigh of day break

The high of day is another zone. The trade underneath is a breakout.

a location9 EMA pullback

Same trade as the VWAP pullback in a different place: a pullback continuation.

a shapeBull flag

Describes how it looks. Price compresses instead of pulling back, then expands: a consolidation continuation.

a behaviorFailed breakout

The expected move fails, traders get trapped, and momentum turns the other way. Not the same category as a flag at all.

The checklist before you enter

Five layers, in this order. They sit in the gap between “I see a pattern” and “I'm in”, and they give you the two-second pause most traders skip.

  1. Stock in play

    Why does this stock deserve your attention today? What's the catalyst, and why would big players be moving positions here today? You're only as good as the stocks you trade, so this filter always comes first.

  2. Setup

    The larger context on the higher time frame, the daily and the 65-minute. Is it trending, breaking out of a range, making a second-day move, reacting to news? This is where the direction comes from.

  3. Momentum trade

    Which of the six things price is doing inside that setup. This page.

  4. Opportunity zone

    The specific area where it should happen: VWAP, the 9 EMA, high of day, prior resistance. These are places to look, not trades.

  5. Trigger

    The specific price action event that tells you it's actually happening. Not that it might happen. That it is.

  6. Then the plan

    Only after all five: allocation, stop, entry and target.

Fewer trades survive, and that's the point

Run the five layers every time and many trades you used to take simply won't make it through.

1 · Breakout

The core question is simple: can price escape a defined trading range?

Escape the range

1

The level isn't the trade

  • A breakout happens when price moves through a meaningful boundary and starts expanding beyond it.
  • The boundary could be high of day, the premarket high, the top or bottom of a range, a daily resistance level, or a whole dollar.
  • The level is only where it happens. The momentum trade is the act of escaping that level and expanding away from it.

Picture a stock stuck between $49 and $50 for an hour. Sold at 50, bought at 49, again and again. Eventually there aren't enough sellers left at 50, and the ceiling simply disappears.

2

Read the setup before the break

  • Out of a higher time frame range break, an opening range break can be the beginning of a large expansion.
  • Inside an overextension, the exact same break can be the final push before exhaustion.
  • Same drawing, same level, very different odds.

That's why the setup gets identified before the momentum trade gets evaluated.

2 · Breakout and retest

Same level, same drawing, same break. The bet is different: the market already escaped, and now it has to prove the level changed function.

The old ceiling becomes the floor

3

A different question

  • Price breaks 50 and runs to 50.70, then pulls back. Did 50 break? That already happened.
  • The question now is what happens when price comes back to 50. If buyers step in at 50.10, 50.05, even 50.00, the market has handed you new information.
  • Resistance has become support. On the way down it's the mirror: support becomes resistance.

This is the trade: the broken level being tested and proven.

4

Classify it, and the plan gets sharper

  • A common mistake is calling every pullback after a breakout a pullback trade. If the defining feature is the broken level being tested, it's a breakout and retest.
  • Once it's classified correctly, the stop and the target both get clearer.

“Buy the dip” has no level. A retest has one.

3 · Pullback continuation

One of the most important momentum trades in intraday trading, and the one where the most strategy names collapse into a single mechanism.

Retrace, hold, resume

5

Five names, one bet

  • First pullback, second pullback, 9 EMA pullback, VWAP pullback, micro pullback. Five names, and underneath them the same bet.
  • An established move is underway. Price temporarily moves back against it.
  • The counter-move can't do structural damage to the trend, and the original direction resumes.

You're not betting on the 9 EMA. It doesn't make stocks go up. You're betting the original buying pressure is still intact and the selloff failed to change the auction.

6

The removal test

  • Take the 9 EMA off the chart. The trade should still make complete sense.
  • Existing momentum pauses through a retracement, structure holds, momentum resumes. That's the whole mechanism.

If the trade stops making sense without the line, you were trading the indicator, not the mechanism.

4 · Consolidation continuation

Close to the pullback, with one useful difference: a pullback gives back price, a consolidation gives back mostly time.

Time instead of price

7

Sideways says something

  • A stock runs from $20 to $23. Instead of pulling back to 22, it goes sideways: 22.85, 22.90, 22.80, 22.90.
  • Despite the strong move, sellers can't push price meaningfully lower. The market is digesting the move through time, not price.
  • Eventually that compression can turn into a new expansion. That's the momentum trade.

Bull flag, bear flag, pennant, flat top, high of day consolidation: different visual expressions of the same idea.

8

Often the cleanest risk on the page

  • The stop sits just below the consolidation low.
  • The trigger is clear too: price breaking the top of the range into a new leg.
  • A clear trigger gives a clear entry and a clear stop, and if the checks line up, room to run to the next target.

Tight, defined risk is why the best consolidation trades are worth waiting for.

Two continuations, kept separate

Closely related, but worth keeping distinct.

5 · Trend continuation

Use this one carefully. Without discipline it turns into a junk drawer for anything going up or down.

The trend is the edge

9

Not a junk drawer

  • If it's clearly a pullback, call it a pullback continuation. If it's clearly a flag, call it a consolidation continuation.
  • Trend continuation is for the times when the broader directional move itself is the main source of the edge.
  • Higher high, higher low, higher high, higher low: buyers keep winning the auction at higher prices. Downtrends are the mirror, lower lows and lower highs.

Channel trading is this momentum trade.

10

When it doesn't fit one pattern

  • It rides a rising 9 EMA. It stair-steps higher. It keeps holding a trending channel.
  • Every shallow dip gets bought almost immediately.
  • None of that is one textbook pattern. The trade is based on the persistence of the trend.

So call trend continuation in trending environments, and nowhere else.

Context changes the odds

The setup classification matters most on this one.

6 · Reclaim

The one most often misunderstood, because traders treat the level itself as the setup. The reclaim defines the trade, not the location.

Lost, then taken back

11

The sequence

  • An important level exists: VWAP, a prior close, the premarket high.
  • Price loses that level for a while, and the sellers seem to have control.
  • Then price recovers the level and, importantly, doesn't lose it again right away. Buyers re-establish control on the original side.

That's the momentum trade. VWAP isn't magic. The market accepted prices below it, failed to hold that control, and shifted back above.

12

Why the trap matters

  • The transition from below to above can trap participants, especially short-term shorts.
  • As they get out, their buying adds to the push and leaves more room on the upside.

The reclaim defines the expectancy of the move, not the opportunity zone.

Reclaim vs breakout and retest

They can look alike. The starting side is what separates them.

breakout and retestStarts on one side

Breaks through, then tests the new side of the level.

Price had settled on the wrong side, then recovered it.

Stop collecting names. Learn what price is doing.

Hundreds of strategy names sit on top of six mechanisms. Once you can see the mechanism, the names stop mattering, and every chart gets the same better question: what is the market actually doing here?

  • A location is where a trade happens. It's never the trade.
  • A shape describes how a trade looks. The mechanism is why it works.
  • Name the setup first. The same momentum trade means different things in different setups.
  • No stock in play, no setup, no trigger: no trade, however good the pattern looks.
