Inch Wide, Mile Deep

Edge doesn't come from a belief about a stock, or a good mood about your day. It comes from reading the tape, scoping a catalyst and setup, and starting with your stop — and once you actually have it, it's an inch wide and miles deep.

What is a trading edge?

A trading edge is a reason a trade should work that doesn't come from a belief about the stock or a good mood about your day. On this page it is built in a fixed order: read the price action across time frames, structure the idea as catalyst, setup and trade, and start with your stop. Once you have it, it's an inch wide and miles deep.

1

Read the price action across every time frame

  • Start on the higher time frame — usually the daily — then step down: hourly, 15-minute, lower.
  • You're not hunting a different pattern on each one. You're checking whether they all tell the same story — uniformity, not noise.
  • Describe what you see the way traders actually talk: aggressive, passive, trending, choppy, boring — or, the best sign of all, a little scary.

Price action is the actual language of trading. The words you reach for to describe a chart are the same words that tell you whether an edge is even in play.

2

Know why your trade has edge

  • Edge never comes from a belief about the stock, or a good mood about your day. "I think it's going up" isn't edge — it's a feeling.
  • Structure it as catalyst → setup → trade. The catalyst is the reason other people have to act. The setup is the specific price-action combination. The trade is the scoped execution.
  • Flesh out four or five ideas for every one you actually put risk on. Know who's on the wrong side of the trade and losing money — that tells you how they'll act, and gives you permission to walk away when it isn't there.

The exact same aggressive price action can make total sense with one catalyst and setup, and make no sense at all with a different one. The combination is what scopes the edge — no single piece of it does that alone.

3

Start with your stop

  • Before you size anything, find the point in the price action where your edge actually began. That's your stop.
  • Your stop sets your risk. Your risk sets your size. You can't get sizing right without knowing where you're wrong first.
  • You can move a stop when the price action hands you a new reason to — a new edge, more of the other side's money changing hands. You cannot move a stop because of your P&L.

A stop based on how your account feels isn't risk management, it's hope wearing a number. A stop based on where the price action actually invalidates the idea is the only kind that means anything.

4

Your edge is an inch wide and miles deep

  • In the moment you need to act, the what-ifs show up — what if it doesn't work, what if I'm wrong, what if, what if. That's a mile of fear, FOMO, and greed, and it's only an inch deep.
  • Real trading edge looks the opposite: narrow. One catalyst, one setup, one read. But it goes miles deep once you actually stand in it.
  • The fix isn't more confidence in general. It's planting your feet in the specific inch-wide edge you already identified, instead of standing in the mile-wide field of everything that could go wrong.

The traders who scale aren't the ones who found a wider edge. They're the ones who stopped discounting the narrow one they already had.

Four ways this gets it backwards

Same four components, read wrong. Catch yourself here before it costs you.

1  Describing the story, not the tape

You describe the stock — "it's a good company," "it's due for a bounce" — instead of the price action actually in front of you.

Kills: trading a story instead of a chart.

2  Calling conviction an edge

You feel good about a trade with no catalyst, no defined setup, and no specific trade scoped out behind it.

Kills: trading a hunch and calling it edge.

3  Moving your stop for the wrong reason

You move your stop because your account balance makes you nervous, not because the price action gave you a new reason to.

Kills: turning risk management into a wish.

4  Standing in the mile, not the inch

You're replaying every "what if" instead of standing in the one specific edge you already flushed out.

Kills: talking yourself out of the one idea you did the work on.

Questions

How do you know why your trade has edge?

Structure it as catalyst, setup and trade. The catalyst is why other people have to act, the setup is the price action combination, and the trade is the scoped execution. Know who is on the wrong side and losing money.

Why start with the stop?

Your stop sets your risk and your risk sets your size. Put it where the price action shows your edge began, and move it only when price action gives a new reason, never because of your P&L.

How many ideas should you screen per trade?

Flesh out four or five ideas for every one you actually put risk on.

What does inch wide, mile deep mean?

The what-ifs of fear, FOMO and greed are a mile wide and only an inch deep. Real edge is the opposite: one catalyst, one setup, one read, held with conviction once you're in it.

Edge is an inch wide. Stand in it anyway.
