Knock Twice

The first break of an old high is a question, not an answer. The trade is the knock: price comes back, touches the old high once and closes back above it, with the stop a tick under that candle and the break of the new high as the confirmation.

6 / 6

Watch one long, start to finish

1

Mark the old high

  • A swing high is a bar with 3 lower highs on each side of it. It only counts once the third bar after it has closed.
  • Of the swing highs in the last 48 bars (4 hours on a 5-minute chart), watch the highest one that hasn't already been broken and used.
  • Shorts are the mirror: the lowest unbroken swing low.

The highest recent swing is where the last buyers gave up. If price can get through it and then hold it, the sellers who defended it are gone.

2

The break opens the setup

  • A candle closes at least 1 tick above the old high, after the one before it closed at or below it.
  • Only breaks between 9:35 and 3:30 open a setup. The first five minutes and the last half hour are skipped.
  • This isn't an entry. A break that closes straight back under the level cancels the setup.

Plenty of first breaks close straight back under the level. Waiting to see the level hold from above is what separates a real break from a stop run.

3

Stay away

  • Price makes a new high. It's confirmed once 3 bars after it fail to go higher.
  • Then at least 5 bars in a row have to stay clear of the old high, wicks included. An early touch resets the count.
  • The leg, from the old high to the new one, has to be between 0.6 and 3 times the 14-bar ATR.

A touch right after the break is just the break still chopping. Going away first and then coming back is the second look the setup is named for.

4

The knock: enter on the close back above

  • A candle's range touches the old high.
  • That candle, or the next one, closes back above the old high. That's the knock. Enter at that close.
  • Stop: 1 tick under the knock candle's low, or 1 tick under a line 4 ticks below the old high if that's lower.

The old ceiling just acted as a floor. The stop goes where that read is proven wrong, which is only a few cents away.

5

The new high has to break

  • A close above the new high confirms the break of structure. The indicator marks it with a check.
  • It has to come within 24 bars of the knock. If it doesn't, the setup is off.
  • A close more than 4 ticks back under the old high also ends it.

A level that held on the knock should let price go straight back to the high and through it. If it can't, the buyers who held the level aren't there.

6

T2 is one more leg, risk 1%

  • T2 = the new high plus the leg (new high minus old high). Minus for shorts.
  • Size every trade so the stop costs 1% of the account, then check the share count against your buying power.

A measured move: the leg that broke the old high, repeated from the new one. With the stop this close, T2 is usually several times the risk.

What cancels it

Every decision is made on a closed bar, never on a wick or a bar that's still forming.

A close back through the level

More than 4 ticks under the old high (over it, for shorts). Before the knock, the setup is gone. After it, get out on that close if the stop hasn't already filled.

The clock runs out

No close above the new high within 24 bars of the knock, or 120 bars since the break. The idea has gone stale.

A break the other way

A fresh break of a swing low cancels every long setup still open, and the reverse. The old high is also retired once a break above it confirms.

The four filters

Each one removes a retest that looks like the setup but isn't.

1  Five bars clear

After the break, at least 5 bars in a row can't touch the old high. A touch before that resets the count.

Kills: a break that's still chopping around the level.

2  Leg 0.6 to 3 ATR

The move from the old high to the new one is measured in 14-bar ATRs. Under 0.6 is noise. Over 3 is a spike that rarely retests cleanly.

Kills: tiny pokes and blow-off spikes.

3  Close back on the right side

The knock candle, or the one after it, has to close back above the old high. A touch that closes below it isn't a knock.

Kills: retests that are really breakdowns.

4  9:35 to 3:30

New breaks only count inside the session. Setups already open keep running after 3:30.

Kills: the opening print and the closing rush.

The first break asks. The knock answers.

Buying the first break of an old high means guessing whether it holds. Waiting for price to leave, come back and hold the level means buying where you're proven wrong a few cents lower, with a whole leg of room above.

  • A touch in the first 5 bars after the break isn't the knock. Let the count reset and wait.
  • A close back through the level ends the idea. That's the exit, not a reason to add.
  • Knocks fail more often than confirmed breaks do. Risk 1% per trade and let the size of T2 do the work.
