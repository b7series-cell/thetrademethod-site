Zero Lag Pullback

The cross only tells you the trend may have turned. The trade is the pullback that closes between the two lines, then closes back over the fast one on real volume, with the slow line as your stop and twice your risk as the target.

What is a zero lag pullback?

A zero lag pullback is a trend pullback trade using a 50 and a 100-period zero-lag EMA. In an uptrend, you wait for a close between the two lines, then buy when a green candle closes back above the 50 on volume at or above average. The stop goes below the 100 and the target is 2R.

1

Two zero-lag lines and volume

  • Add the zero-lag EMA twice: period 50 in white, period 100 in green, both with a thicker line.
  • Add the standard Volume indicator and turn on its moving average (Style tab), colored white so it stands out.
  • 50 and 100 are the most common moving-average settings. Here they track the medium and longer-term momentum.

A zero-lag EMA adds back the gap between an EMA and an EMA of that EMA, so it turns sooner than a plain EMA of the same length. The cross comes earlier, and so does the pullback read.

2

Read the trend from the lines

  • White above green: uptrend. White below green: downtrend.
  • When they cross, the old trend may be ending and a new one starting. Mark it. Don't trade it.

The same cross also prints in a deep pullback that turns back the old way, and right before a range. On its own it can't tell those apart from a real reversal.

3

Wait for a close between the lines

  • In an uptrend, wait for price to close below the white line but still above the green one.
  • That close arms the long. A wick into the zone doesn't count. It has to close there.
  • A close below the green line cancels it. So does the white line crossing back under.

This is the low-risk spot: you're buying a dip inside a trend that's already established, close to the line the trend should hold.

4

Green candle back over white, with volume

  • Now wait for a green candle to close back above the white line.
  • When it closes, check its volume against the volume average. At or above average: the signal is confirmed. Below it: no trade, keep waiting.
  • Shorts are the mirror: a red candle closing back below white, on average-or-better volume.

Volume is the proof the reclaim has buyers behind it. A thin push back over the line is just as likely to fall straight back into the zone.

5

Enter, stop on the green line

  • Enter at the confirmed candle's close.
  • Stop below the green line, as it stood when you entered. Above it for shorts.

If price falls through the green line, the market is turning and the entry was wrong. If the uptrend is real, the green line is where pullbacks should stop.

6

Target at 2R, risk 1%

  • Target = entry plus twice the entry-to-stop distance. Minus for shorts.
  • Size every trade so the stop costs 1% of the account.

This setup still has false signals, and no setup avoids them. At 2R a trade has to win only one time in three to break even, and the 1% rule keeps a string of losers survivable.

Long and short, step for step

Same three conditions, mirrored. In both, the first reclaim of the white line came on thin volume and was skipped.

What a cross can turn into

Why the cross is never the entry. Only the first of these gives you a pullback that holds the green line.

Which closes count

Every decision in this setup is made on a closed bar, never on a wick or a bar that's still forming.

Close between the lines

Below white, at or above green (the mirror for shorts). Arms the pullback. A wick into the zone that closes back above white doesn't count.

Close through the green line

Cancels the pullback. The trend is in doubt. Wait for the lines to settle and a fresh close between them.

Lines cross the other way

Cancels it too, and flips which side you're watching. White under green: only shorts from here.

The four filters

Each one removes a trade that looks like the setup but isn't.

1  Never the cross alone

The cross marks a possible new trend. Deep pullbacks and ranges cross the lines too, so the entry waits for a pullback inside the new trend.

Kills: buying a cross that's about to cross back.

2  A close between the lines

Price has to close below white and above green before the trigger counts. No close in the zone, no pullback, no trade.

Kills: chasing a trend that never pulled back.

3  Volume at or above average

The candle that closes back over white needs volume at or above the average line. Below it, skip that candle and wait for the next one.

Kills: thin reclaims that fall back into the zone.

4  Candle in the trend's color

Green candle for a long, red candle for a short. A candle that closes back over white while still red isn't a buy.

Kills: reclaims the bar itself argues against.

Size off the green line, not a guess

The stop sits on the green line, so the distance changes every trade. Size off it so every stop costs the same 1%.

Account × 1%$50,000 × 1% = $500.00

Stop — the green line at entry103.30

The mistake this prevents

Tuck the stop just under the white line instead, at 104.00, and $500 ÷ $0.65 says buy 769 shares.

The dip to 103.46 stops you out for −$500.

That's the same dip that held at the green line on its way to 107.35. Twice the shares, and the stop sits where normal pullbacks go, not where the trend breaks.

Questions

What is a zero-lag EMA?

An EMA that adds back the gap between an EMA and an EMA of that EMA, so it turns sooner than a plain EMA of the same length.

Why not trade the cross?

The cross only says the trend may have turned. Deep pullbacks and ranges cross the lines too, so the entry waits for a pullback inside the new trend.

What cancels the setup?

A close below the 100-period line, or the 50 crossing back under the 100. A reclaim of the 50 on below-average volume is skipped.

Does the zero lag pullback work?

Not better than chance in our test. On SPY 5-minute bars, 446 trades made +0.01R a trade with a 35% win rate. On slower charts the long side made money, but random long entries in the same uptrend with the same stop and 2R target made as much.

The cross finds the trend. The pullback and the volume find the entry.

Trading the cross means guessing whether it's a reversal, a deep pullback or a range. Waiting for the pullback and a reclaim on volume means trading with a trend that's already established, from a spot where the stop is obvious.

  • A close through the green line means the trend read was wrong. That's the stop, not a reason to add.
  • A reclaim on thin volume isn't a signal. Skip it and wait for the next candle; the pullback is still armed.
  • False signals will happen. Risk 1% per trade and let the 2R payoff work over many trades.
