Liquidity Refill

When buyers take liquidity faster than sellers can add it back, price has only one way to go. You can't see the order book, but you can see its footprint: a big candle out of compression, then a next candle whose early sell-off gets absorbed in seconds, high up in the first candle. Buy the break of the first candle's high, stop under the wick, and get out after three or four candles.

What is a liquidity refill setup?

A liquidity refill is a two-candle pattern from a quant trader's order book research. After a relatively big candle (C1) out of compression, the next candle (C2) dips early and shallow, in the top 10 to 20% of C1, and gets absorbed fast. You buy when C2 breaks C1's high, stop past the wick, and exit after three or four candles.

Watch one liquidity refill long, start to finish

1

Start from compression

  • A stretch of small candles: a low-volatility state, ideally with a simple trend line or market structure you can see.
  • It's what makes the next candle stand out. The candles closest to it should be clearly smaller.
  • Skip choppy charts where big candles of about the same size keep coming in a short stretch.

Compression, then an imbalance candle, is where this gives the most accurate results.

2

Spot C1, a relatively big candle

  • Bigger than the candles around it. Call it whatever you like; it's just a relatively big candle.
  • Assume one side was aggressive: buyers took liquidity at a much higher rate than sellers could add it back (or the reverse, short).
  • A structural break at the same time, through a trend line or a few prior highs, makes it more interesting. It isn't required.

One candle isn't enough to know there's an imbalance. It's the first assumption. What happens next decides it.

3

Watch the first seconds of C2

  • For a long, a quick sell-off right as C2 opens. On a 1-minute chart, in the first 10 to 15 seconds.
  • The wick stays small, in the top 10–20% of C1. It shouldn't come near C1's 50% level.
  • Then buyers absorb it and turn aggressive again: the candle flips. For a short it's all mirrored: a quick pop, absorbed, near C1's low.

Buyers are now lining up liquidity at a much higher price than before, and doing it quickly enough to absorb the selling instantly.

4

Enter when C2 crosses C1's high

  • Long: when price crosses above C1's high. Short: when it crosses below C1's low.
  • It has to be C2. If C2 doesn't break it and C3 does, there's no trade.
  • C2 has to be on your side: bullish for a long. A small wick on a bearish C2 is no trade.

C1's high is the highest price buyers paid. Crossing it says they're now willing to do price discovery above it.

5

Stop just past the wick

  • Long: a little below C2's wick. Short: a little above it.
  • Roughly, not to the tick. Philip puts it a bit away from the wick; you can put it right at it.
  • If the wick is too big, the stop is too wide for the trade to pay. Consider skipping it.

The tight stop is the whole idea. You can lose many times; when it works the win makes it all back.

6

Ride three or four candles, then out

  • An imbalance only lasts a few candles. Out after three or four, as long as each closes in your direction.
  • Out early on the first opposite candle: a red close on a long, a green one on a short. The imbalance is gone.
  • Out earlier at an obvious level in the way, like the previous day's low or high.

Don't expect 20 candles. It's a quick scalp: you want at least 1:2, most likely 1:3.

When to skip it

Simple, but not on every chart.

Chop

Big candles of about the same size, one after another in a short stretch. No compression, no clear structure break.

Ignore the wick entirely.

A late wick

The sell-off (or pop, short) came later in C2, not in its first moments. That's not the absorption you're looking for.

Late wick: no trade.

A wick too deep

It goes past C1's 50% level, or far enough that the stop behind it leaves no room for 1:2.

Risk-reward gone: pass.

No break on C2

C2 closes against you or never crosses C1's high (low). C3 breaking it instead doesn't count.

C1, C2. Not C3.

Aggression meets refill

Two ideas from order book microstructure. Aggression is who's taking liquidity: buyers crossing the spread to take what the ask offers. Liquidity refill is the rate at which new limit orders are added back as the old ones get consumed. Together they show an imbalance, and an imbalance moves price.

The whiteboard example

Buyers are willing to buy 10,000 contracts a second by crossing the spread. Sellers can only add back 2,000 a second. The ask is refilling, but at a much slower rate, and buyers easily consume whatever comes back.

If that continues, there's only one way price can go: up.

Most traders don't have this data, and most order execution today is automated. You don't need it: the next section is how the same imbalance shows up on a plain price chart. The 4,000 contracts a level in the sketch is ours, just to make it move.

What C2 has to do

Same compression, same C1. Only the second candle changes. The wick has to come early, stay shallow, and C2 itself has to flip and break C1's high. The strip under each chart is C2's first minute, with the first 15 seconds in green.

In the live NASDAQ trade, the first imbalance candle was skipped for exactly the third reason: the wick on the next candle formed later in its 15 minutes, so it wasn't marked.

Grade the wick, then size it

Type C1's high and low, where C2's early wick reached, and when it was done. The defaults are the walkthrough's. The stop buffer and the dollar risk are only examples; Philip says his stop is placed roughly, not by a rule.

The wick is your risk

The smaller the wick, the closer the stop, the better the trade. Inside the top 5% of C1 is an even stronger case than 20%. Past 50%, it isn't the pattern any more.

At least 1:2. Most likely 1:3.

The price has to make a pretty big move to get there, so if the wick is a bit too big and throws off the risk-reward, Philip would consider not taking it, even with everything else in place.

Missed it? The second chance

If you spot the pattern on a higher time frame and miss the break, drop to a lower one. As long as price stays above C2's low, the initial wick, look for a retest of C2's opening level. When price closes back above the open, that can be a second entry, often with a tighter stop.

The rules for the retest

  • C2's low, the initial wick, must not be violated. That's also where the first trade's stop is.
  • Price has to come back and test C2's opening level. It doesn't have to go far below it: a quick bounce or a rejection wick is fine. Be a little open-minded.
  • When price closes back above the open, that's the second entry, with the stop under the retest.
  • One retest only. If it comes back a second time, the imbalance most likely isn't in place.

Skip it on dead candles

If the retest happens on very low volatility candles, ignore it. Technical analysis works best with participants in the market and real volatility.

Philip takes the initial entry, not the retest.

“If I miss the trade, I'm fine. I move on to the next day.” He doesn't want to spend hours watching the chart or chase it.

Three ways out

It's a quick setup. You don't need to decide how long to hold it: count candles and watch their color, and know where the obvious level is.

01

Three or four candles

Each one closing higher than the last on a long (lower, short). After three or four, you're out. The move most likely pulls back next.

02

The first opposite candle

If a candle closes red after your long entry (green after a short), close it. The imbalance isn't in place any more.

03

An obvious level

Like the previous day's low in the live short. Levels everyone can see tend to get retested and respected, often with a bounce, before a break.

How you trade it

The whole setup, in order.

  1. Find compression: small candles, low volatility, ideally a simple trend line or structure. Skip chop.
  2. Spot C1, a relatively big candle. Assume aggression is outrunning the refill.
  3. Watch the first moments of C2: a quick sell-off (pop, short) that stays in C1's top 10–20% and gets absorbed. Late or deep: no trade.
  4. Enter when C2 crosses C1's high (low, short). Only C2, and only if C2 is on your side.
  5. Stop just past C2's wick. If that makes the risk too big for 1:2, consider passing.
  6. Out after three or four candles, on the first opposite candle, or at an obvious level like the previous day's low or high.
  7. Missed it? One retest of C2's open on a lower time frame, above C2's low, with real volatility. Or just move on.

The indicator

One TradingView script for the setup (Pine Script v6), on any time frame. It finds C1, reads C2's first moments from lower time frame data, and draws the entry, the stop and the exit on your chart. It marks and alerts. It does not place trades.

  • Liquidity Refill

    alerts

    C1 counts when its range is at least 2× the average of the 8 quiet bars before it, its body is at least 60% of its range, it closes in the top (bottom) quarter, and no bar among the quiet ones was nearly as big (chop). On the next candle it checks that the wick against C1 printed in the early window and held, and went no deeper than 20% into C1. Deeper wicks up to 50% show as gray “shaky” entries. Entry is marked when C2 crosses C1's high (low), the stop 2 ticks past the wick, and the exit at the close of the 4th candle, the first opposite close or the previous day's high/low, whichever comes first. A panel keeps the trade count, wins and average R. Every number is a setting.

    The early window sets itself from the chart: 15 seconds on 1-minute, the first minute on 5-minute, 2 minutes on 15-minute, 4 hours on daily. On 1-minute charts it needs seconds data, which only some TradingView plans have. Without it, turn “Require intrabar timing” off and judge the timing yourself. The second-chance retest is left to you. The 2×, 60% and quarter thresholds are ours, not the video's.

//@version=6
// =====================================================================
// Liquidity Refill — thetrademethod.com
//
// A quant's two-candle imbalance pattern (aggression against liquidity
// refill), on any time frame, long or short:
//
//   1. C1          A relatively big candle out of compression: its range
//                  is a multiple of the quiet bars before it, it has a
//                  real body and closes near its extreme. Chop (another
//                  bar about as big among the quiet bars) is skipped.
//   2. C2's wick   The NEXT candle opens with a quick move against C1
//                  (a sell-off after a green C1) that is done in the
//                  first moments of the bar and stays in the top 10-20%
//                  of C1. Timing is read from intrabar data; a wick that
//                  forms later in the bar doesn't count.
//   3. The entry   C2 crosses C1's high (low, short). Only C2: a break
//                  on C3 is no trade. Entry is marked a tick past it.
//   4. The stop    A few ticks past C2's wick.
//   5. The exit    The close of the Nth candle (C2 counts as 1), the
//                  first opposite close, or the previous day's high/low
//                  (intraday), whichever comes first. Or the stop.
//
// Early window, by default (the video's numbers, "not set in stone"):
//   1-minute: first 15 s · 5-minute: first minute · 15-minute: first
//   2 minutes · daily: first 4 hours. In between, it scales.
// Seconds data (used on 1-minute charts) needs a TradingView plan that
// has it. Without intrabar data, turn "Require intrabar timing" off and
// judge the timing yourself.
//
// The second-chance retest on a lower time frame is left to you.
// Marks and alerts only; it places no orders.
// =====================================================================

grpC = "C1 · the imbalance candle"

bigX    = input.float(2.0, "C1 range ≥ × their average range", minval = 1.0, step = 0.1, group = grpC)
bodyMin = input.float(0.6, "C1 body ≥ share of its range", minval = 0.0, maxval = 1.0, step = 0.05, group = grpC)
closeIn = input.float(0.25, "C1 closes within this share of its extreme", minval = 0.05, maxval = 0.5, step = 0.05, group = grpC)
chopX   = input.float(0.75, "Chop: skip if a quiet bar's range ≥ × C1's", minval = 0.3, maxval = 1.0, step = 0.05, group = grpC)
useCmp  = input.bool(true, "Require compression", group = grpC, tooltip = "The quiet bars' average range must be at or below this multiple of ATR(50).")
cmpX    = input.float(1.0, "Quiet average range ≤ × ATR(50)", minval = 0.2, step = 0.1, group = grpC)

grpW = "C2 · the early wick"
maxDepth = input.float(20, "Max wick depth into C1 (%)", minval = 1, maxval = 100, group = grpW)
showShaky = input.bool(true, "Also mark shaky wicks up to 50%", group = grpW, tooltip = "Deeper than the max but under C1's halfway point. The live trade in the video was about 30-35%, taken because it was very quick. Check the risk-reward.")
winIn    = input.int(0, "Early window, seconds (0 = auto)", minval = 0, group = grpW)
ltfIn    = input.timeframe("", "Intrabar timeframe (blank = auto)", group = grpW, tooltip = "Auto: 1S on 1-minute and faster charts, 1 minute up to 15-minute, 5 minutes up to 1-hour, 15 minutes up to 4-hour, 1 hour above.")

grpE = "Entry, stop and exit"
bufT    = input.int(2, "Stop buffer past the wick (ticks)", minval = 0, group = grpE)
nBars   = input.int(4, "Exit at the close of candle N (C2 = 1)", minval = 1, maxval = 10, group = grpE)
oppEx   = input.bool(true, "Exit on the first opposite close", group = grpE)
pdEx    = input.bool(true, "Exit at the previous day's high / low (intraday)", group = grpE)
doLong  = input.bool(true, "Longs", group = grpE)
doShort = input.bool(true, "Shorts", group = grpE)

grpV = "Display"
showC1    = input.bool(true, "Mark every C1", group = grpV)
showPanel = input.bool(true, "Show panel", group = grpV)

cDn  = input.color(color.new(#ff453a, 0), "Short", group = grpV)
cWk  = input.color(color.new(#f4b942, 0), "Early wick", group = grpV)
cOut = input.color(color.new(#22d3ee, 0), "Exit", group = grpV)

tfSec   = timeframe.in_seconds()

winSec  = winIn > 0 ? winIn : autoWin
autoLtf = tfSec <= 60 ? "1S" : tfSec <= 900 ? "1" : tfSec <= 3600 ? "5" : tfSec <= 14400 ? "15" : "60"
ltf     = ltfIn == "" ? autoLtf : ltfIn

// ---------------- intrabar data for C2's timing ----------------
[iL, iH, iT] = request.security_lower_tf(syminfo.tickerid, ltf, [low, high, time], ignore_invalid_timeframe = true)
winEnd = time + winSec * 1000
float eLo = na
float eHi = na
float lLo = na
float lHi = na
nIn = array.size(iT)
if nIn > 0
    for i = 0 to nIn - 1
        lo_ = array.get(iL, i)
        hi_ = array.get(iH, i)
        if array.get(iT, i) < winEnd
            eLo := na(eLo) ? lo_ : math.min(eLo, lo_)
            eHi := na(eHi) ? hi_ : math.max(eHi, hi_)
        else
            lLo := na(lLo) ? lo_ : math.min(lLo, lo_)
            lHi := na(lHi) ? hi_ : math.max(lHi, hi_)
hasTiming = nIn > 0 and not na(eLo)
// the bar's low (long) / high (short) was printed in the early window and not taken out later

var int barsWithData = 0

qAvg  = ta.sma(rng, lenQ)[1]
qMax  = ta.highest(rng, lenQ)[1]

    label.new(bar_index, isC1Up ? low : high, "C1", yloc = yloc.price, style = isC1Up ? label.style_label_up : label.style_label_down,

var float ent      = na
var float stp      = na
var float risk     = na
var int   entBar   = na
var line  lnStop   = na
var line  lnEnt    = na
var int   nTrades  = 0
var int   nWins    = 0
var float sumR     = 0.0
var float lastR    = na
var string lastWhy = ""

exitSig = false
float exitPx = na
string why = ""

// exits on bars after the entry bar
if side != 0 and bar_index > entBar
    if side == 1 and low <= stp
        exitPx := math.min(open, stp)
        why := "stop"
    else if side == -1 and high >= stp
        exitPx := math.max(open, stp)
        why := "stop"
    else if pdEx and timeframe.isintraday and side == 1 and not na(pdh) and pdh > ent and high >= pdh
        exitPx := math.max(open, pdh)
        why := "prior day high"
    else if pdEx and timeframe.isintraday and side == -1 and not na(pdl) and pdl < ent and low <= pdl
        exitPx := math.min(open, pdl)
        why := "prior day low"
    else if barstate.isconfirmed and oppEx and ((side == 1 and close < open) or (side == -1 and close > open))
        exitPx := close
        why := "opposite candle"
    else if barstate.isconfirmed and bar_index - entBar + 1 >= nBars

        why := str.tostring(nBars) + " candles"

c1Dn = isC1Dn[1]
c1Hi = high[1]
c1Lo = low[1]
c1R  = c1Hi - c1Lo

depthS = c1R > 0 ? (high - c1Lo) / c1R * 100 : na
wickL = low < open and (not needTime or lowEarly)
wickS = high > open and (not needTime or highEarly)

okS = depthS <= maxDepth or (showShaky and depthS <= 50)

    shakyNow = longSig ? depthL > maxDepth : depthS > maxDepth
    col = longSig ? cUp : cDn
    dep = longSig ? depthL : depthS
    label.new(bar_index, longSig ? low : high, "", yloc = yloc.price, style = label.style_circle, color = cWk, size = size.tiny)

              style = longSig ? label.style_label_lower_right : label.style_label_upper_right, color = shakyNow ? color.new(color.gray, 20) : col,
              textcolor = color.black, size = size.small)
    lnEnt  := line.new(bar_index, ent, bar_index, ent, color = color.new(color.white, 30), width = 1)
    lnStop := line.new(bar_index, stp, bar_index, stp, color = cDn, style = line.style_dashed, width = 1)

        why := "C2 closed against it"
    else if nBars <= 1

        why := "1 candle"

    side := 0

Questions

What does liquidity refill mean?

It's the rate at which new limit orders are added back as old ones get taken. When buyers take liquidity faster than sellers can refill it, price has only one way to go.

How early does the C2 wick have to come?

On a 1-minute chart, in the first 10 to 15 seconds of C2. On a 5-minute chart roughly the first minute, on a 15-minute chart the first couple of minutes, and on a daily chart the first few hours.

When should you skip a liquidity refill?

In chop with same-size big candles, when the wick comes late in C2, when it goes past C1's 50% level, or when C2 never breaks C1's high. A break on C3 doesn't count.

Has the liquidity refill been backtested?

Not fairly. On 5-minute bars it made +0.83R a trade over 73 trades, but bars can't show whether the wick came first. On 1-minute bars only 10 trades clearly did, so a real test needs tick or second-by-second data.

Fibonacci OTE

Price moves in waves, so don't buy the impulse.

You can't see the order book. You can see what it leaves behind.

A big candle after compression says one side was taking liquidity faster than the other could refill it. A quick, shallow, early wick on the next candle, absorbed right away, says the refill moved up. The break of the first candle's high is where you find out, and three or four candles is all you ask for.

  • Compression, then a relatively big C1.
  • C2's wick: early, quick, in C1's top 10–20%.
  • In on C2's break of C1's high (low); stop past the wick.
  • Out after 3–4 candles, the first opposite one, or a level.
