What is The Strat?

The Strat is a way of reading price that numbers every bar 1, 2 or 3. A 1 stays inside the last bar, a 2 takes out one side and a 3 takes out both. You use the broadening formation as a map, check whether price is above or below the quarter, month, week and day opens, and trade only when a whole group of related assets breaks the same level at the same time.

1

Number every bar

  • Stays inside the last bar's range: a 1, an inside bar.
  • Takes out one side: a 2. Take the high and it's a 2 up; take the low and it's a 2 down.
  • Takes out both sides: a 3, an outside bar. There is no fourth thing price can do.
  • The break decides it, not the color. A red bar that took out the high is still a 2 up, and it has until its close to become a 3 down.

You don't need 50 candlestick patterns. You need three numbers, and nobody can argue with them.

2

Draw the broadening formation

  • Every 3 is a broadening formation: a higher high and a lower low in one bar. Zoomed out, charts do the same thing over many bars.
  • Draw it right to left: connect the highest high and the lowest low that got taken out.
  • Now you know where price can turn before it gets there. When it turns at one side, the target is the other side. That's the magnitude.
  • The turns you'll see the most: the 2-2 reversal (2 up, then 2 down) and the 2-1-2 (2 up, inside bar, 2 down), and their mirror images.

At the highs, people want to buy. When it rejects, price goes back through the range to broaden the other side, and that rejection is usually the bigger move.

3

Check time frame continuity

  • Every candle is a group of participants, and every open starts a new group: the 60-minute, the day, the week, the month. Investors can use the quarter instead of the hour.
  • Last price above all four opens: the buyers in every group are more aggressive. Below all four: the sellers are.
  • The bigger the time frame, the bigger the money.

It answers the one question that decides where price goes next: who is more aggressive right now?

4

Wait for the simultaneous break

  • Sunday night's chart is a setup. It's closer to a reversal, that's all. Don't rush in on Monday.
  • The evidence is a simultaneous break: two or more correlated assets breaking a 2 in the same direction at the same time.
  • Look at the four major indexes and their futures, sector ETFs like tech, QQQ and semis, or a sector and its holdings.
  • When most of a sector breaks at once, only an institution is big enough: the child orders of one huge order split across the group.

Have you ever tried to place ten trades at once? It doesn't work. When a whole sector takes the offer together, the algorithms just turned on.

5

Enter in force, stop just past the bar

  • Enter right as the simultaneous break happens and price goes in force: through the level, and staying there. 3s exist, so it has to hold.
  • At worst, the stop goes just past the current bar's other extreme: for a weekly short, just above this week's high.
  • You don't need a wide stop. If the algorithms are really on, it should go right away.
  • You can widen it with this much evidence. The video doesn't suggest it.

We're not pattern trading reversals. We're trading evidence of algorithmic trading.

6

Tighten, re-enter, hold the winners

  • Stops scale with time frames. A lower time frame inside bar lets you tighten: use the other side of a daily 1 as the stop and walk it down as price makes lower highs.
  • A weekly break's evidence lasts all week. A bounce that turns back down on the 30, 60, 4-hour or daily is a place to get in or add.
  • A tight stop may need a re-entry, because broadening formations exist. It can stop you out and go on the second or third try.
  • Don't hold losing trades. Add to and hold your winners.

Your biggest losing trade was someone else's biggest winner. Hold the winners and you get the really big ones.

Where to look for the break

Two or more correlated things, breaking a 2 the same way at the same time.

The indexes

The four major indexes, futures included. When they all break together, everyone's on one side.

Sector ETFs

Tech, the QQQ, semis and so on. Semis, tech and the QQQ all going 2-2 down on the week is evidence to short the NASDAQ.

A sector and its holdings

Semis with the big chip stocks, energy with its names. Count how many take last week's level, and how fast.

What kills it

Any one of these and there's no trade yet, or it's time to get out.

Just a setup

Sunday night's hammer or 2-1 is only closer to a reversal. Nothing is breaking yet, so there's nothing to see.

No break: no trade.

Not in force

Price breaks last week's level and trades right back through it. A 2 can still become a 3.

Back through: out, or wait for the re-entry.

No evidence either way

The group doesn't break together and the four opens disagree. Nobody has control of the direction.

Mixed and alone: wait.

Chasing the crowd

At the top of the broadening formation everyone has already bought. A crowded trade is at risk of profit taking.

At the top: think about the other side.

Three numbers, and the turns you'll see most

A candle is a window of time: where price opened, how high and low it traded, and where it closed. From one bar to the next, price can only do one of these.

1

Inside bar

Stays inside the last bar's range. Neither side is taken. Compression, and the start of most reversals and continuations.

2↑

2 up

Takes out the last bar's high, not the low. Red or green, it's a 2 up.

2↓

2 down

Takes out the last bar's low, not the high. A green 2 down is still a 2 down.

3

Outside bar

Takes out the high and the low: a broadening formation in a single bar.

The one candle that shows where price is trying to go

A bar takes out the high, then comes back inside the range. Now the low is in play: a 2 going to become a 3. When that 2 up is red, it has until its close to take the low and become a 3 down.

The biggest losing or winning trade you've ever taken? Go look. It's a 3.

That's why the color never decides the number. The break does, and a break that fails points at the other side.

A phenomenon, not a pattern

Look at a cloud. I see a dog, you see a cow, and we argue. But we both agree it's a cloud. A head and shoulders is the dog. A 3 is the cloud: it took out the high and the low, period.

The rejection is bigger

Good-looking, then the worst

At the top of the formation, the higher highs, everybody says it looks good. One 2-2 reversal later it's the worst-looking chart in the world. The chart didn't change. The phenomenon played out.

“As data scientists, we call it price discovery. And it's not a pattern. It's a phenomenon.”From the video. Some traders call the broadening formation a megaphone.

Who's inside the candle

Think of a store. Shoppers who check out between 10 and 11 are one group; 11 to 12 is another. Put them together and that's today's group. If today is Monday, it's also the week's first group, and Tuesday's shoppers join it. Every open starts a new group: the hour, the day, the week, the month.

01

A candle is everyone who traded

Anyone who bought, added, reduced or exited this quarter is in this quarter's candle. Mostly that's algorithms sent by institutions with really big orders to fill.

02

Green means paying up

If the month is green, the monthly shoppers are paying higher prices. Ground beef is up 20% and they're still buying, because they have to.

03

Bigger time frame, bigger money

The month, week, day and 60-minute are the main groups. When all four agree, that group has control of the direction across every time frame.

Check the four opens

The video's simple version: is the last price above or below each group's open? The numbers start as the walkthrough's Monday, after the break.

Why a backtest won't tell you

Nobody knows how much an institution still has to buy. Call the desk and they'll laugh: if the whole world knew, everyone would buy it first and the fund would get a worse price.

What decides where price goes next is who's more aggressive right now.

That's the video's argument for thinking in evidence instead of probabilities with this method. Time frame continuity and simultaneous breaks are how it reads that aggression.

Stops are fuel

People say smart money hunts your stops. Your stops do get taken out, because price takes out highs and lows. But nobody has to hunt anything. Here's what actually happens.

01

Stops sit at the obvious places

Shorts put their stops above an obvious pivot high, or where they got in.

02

A stop is a market order

It's just waiting for price to get there before it executes. A short's stop is a buy.

03

The algorithm takes the offer

Each price level only holds so much stock. Told to buy aggressively, the algorithm takes every offer and has to pay up to the next level.

04

Stops and breakout buyers pile in

Price trades back up through the range to the stops. They buy, breakout buyers jump in, more market orders hit fewer offers, and price speeds up.

05

Then the profit taking

Once the stops are out and the algorithm has its shares, the only thing left is taking profit. Price turns from the top of the formation.

That's why the broadening formation matters: when price trades back through a range, the stops sitting against its highs and lows are what speed it up.

Size it off the bar

The entry is a penny through last week's level as the group breaks. The stop, at worst, is a penny past this week's other extreme. The default numbers are the walkthrough's, and the dollar risk is only an example.

Win rate is set by the stop, not the evidence

Widen the stop and the win rate goes up. That doesn't make the evidence any better; it just risks more on each trade. The video's advice, after six years of trading it: you don't need to.

A tight stop can mean a re-entry. That's the trade-off.

Broadening formations exist, so a tight stop can get taken out before it goes on the second or third try. Use the lower time frame signals to tighten without choking the trade.

How you trade it

The whole method in four steps, as the video closes it out.

  1. Find the evidence, either across four or more time frames (time frame continuity) or through a simultaneous break across a correlated group.
  2. Enter right when the simultaneous break happens and price goes in force. Stop, at worst, just past the current bar's other extreme.
  3. Use reconfirming signals on lower time frames to enter or add in the direction of the higher time frame evidence, like a 30-minute 2 up that turns 2 down during a weekly short.
  4. Don't hold losing trades. Losing trades lose more. Add to and hold your winners: winning trades win more.

Questions

What do 1, 2 and 3 mean in The Strat?

A 1 is an inside bar that stays inside the last bar's range. A 2 takes out the high (2 up) or the low (2 down). A 3 is an outside bar that takes out both. The break decides the number, not the candle's color.

What is time frame continuity?

Checking last price against the opens of four time frames. Above all four, the buyers in every group are more aggressive; below all four, the sellers are. The bigger the time frame, the bigger the money.

What is a simultaneous break?

Two or more correlated assets, like the major indexes, sector ETFs, or a sector and its holdings, breaking a 2 in the same direction at the same time. Only an institution is big enough to do that, so it is the evidence the page waits for.

Where does the stop go?

At worst, just past the current bar's other extreme, for a weekly short just above this week's high. A lower time frame inside bar lets you tighten it.

Trade the evidence, not the pattern.

Price can only do three things. The candle shows which group is more aggressive. The broadening formation is the map for timing reversals and sizing the move. And a simultaneous break, in force, is the algorithms turning on. Up or down, it's the same evidence every time.

  • Number every bar: 1, 2 up, 2 down or 3. Never the color.
  • Target the other side of the broadening formation.
  • Want full time frame continuity or a simultaneous break.
  • Enter in force; stop just past the bar. Cut losers, hold winners.
