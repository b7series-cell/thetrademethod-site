What is the ICT Silver Bullet?

The Silver Bullet is an ICT intraday model traded in three one-hour New York windows: 3 to 4 am, 10 to 11 am and 2 to 3 pm. Price sweeps an untouched level, a strong displacement candle leaves a fair value gap, and you place a limit at the gap's near edge, stop a tick past the sweep, and target the next untouched level.

1

Wait for a window, with the liquidity marked

  • Three windows, New York time: 3 to 4 am (London), 10 to 11 am and 2 to 3 pm. Nothing outside them counts.
  • Liquidity is any level price hasn't traded through yet: swing highs and lows, the Asia and London session ranges, the previous day's high and low, and the range of the hour before the window.

ICT's idea: stops pile up just past these levels, and the market runs them before it moves the other way.

2

The sweep

  • A high is swept when a candle trades at least one tick above it. A low, at least one tick below. A wick is enough; an exact touch isn't.
  • Each level can be swept once, and only the window's first sweep on each side counts.
  • A swept high starts the search for a short. A swept low, for a long.

The sweep is the stop run. On its own it isn't a signal; it only tells the indicator which way to look.

3

Displacement leaves a gap

  • A fair value gap the other way: three candles where the first and third don't overlap. The middle candle's body is at least 1 ATR(14).
  • The gap is at least 0.1 ATR (and 2 ticks). All three candles are inside the window, and the gap can't start before the sweep.
  • A swept high needs a gap down; a swept low needs a gap up. Only the first one per side in a window counts.

A candle that big right after a stop run is the snap-back the model is waiting for.

4

A limit at the gap, stop past the sweep

  • Entry: a limit order at the edge of the gap nearest to price.
  • Stop: one tick beyond the sweep's extreme, the highest high for a short or the lowest low for a long, from the sweep candle to the end of the gap.
  • Target: the nearest untouched level on the other side. Closer than 1.5 times the risk, the setup is skipped. No level at all, the target is 2R.

The order waits for price to come back into the gap rather than chasing the displacement candle.

5

The fill, or no trade

  • The order fills only if price trades at least one tick through it before the window ends.
  • It's cancelled if price reaches the target first, or the window ends.
  • Each window takes one trade at most.

This is where the study found the problem: the orders that fill are mostly the trades going wrong.

6

Target, stop or the clock

  • The trade closes at the target, the stop, or one hour after the window ends, whichever comes first.
  • If the stop and the target are both hit in the same candle, it counts as a stop.
  • Results are counted in R after slippage and fees. R is the distance from entry to stop.

Counting in R makes a trade on a quiet morning comparable with one on a busy afternoon.

What ends it

Every decision is made on a closed bar, never on a bar that's still forming.

No fill in the window

The limit is cancelled when the window ends, or as soon as price reaches the target without filling it.

The time stop

An open trade is closed one hour after its window ends: 5 am, noon or 4 pm New York.

One trade per window

Once one side fills, any order waiting on the other side of the same window is dropped.

The liquidity it hunts

Five kinds of level. Each one is in play until price trades a tick through it.

Swing highs and lows

A high above the 5 candles before it and the 5 after. Marked once those 5 have closed, dropped after 300 candles.

Asia range

The high and low from 8 pm to midnight New York.

London range

The high and low from 2 to 5 am New York.

Previous day

Yesterday's high and low, with the day starting at 6 pm.

The hour before

The high and low of the clock hour just before the window.

Why the Silver Bullet hours look better

At first the three windows looked slightly better than the average hour. They still lost money, and the reason isn't the clock.

ES slippage — 2 ticks × 0.250.50 pt
Commission — $4.50 ÷ $50 a point0.09 pt
Cost per trade0.59 pt
Quiet hour, 3-point stop0.59 ÷ 3 = 0.20R
Busy hour, 8-point stop0.59 ÷ 8 = 0.07R

Busy hours, wider stops, cheaper costs

The windows sit on some of the busiest hours of the day. Bigger moves mean wider stops, so the same fixed cost is a smaller share of each trade. That alone makes them look better than a quiet hour.

Matched against other hours that move just as much, the advantage disappeared on all four futures.

The cost figures above are arithmetic for ES, not results from the study.

The trades you miss are the winners

The same gap, two endings. A limit order only fills when price comes back, and a move that comes all the way back is often a move that's failing.

In the study about three in four of the unfilled setups would have reached the target. That points to a different entry worth testing: a market order at the sweep instead of a limit at the gap. These two charts are illustrative.

Questions

What counts as a sweep?

A candle trading at least one tick through an untouched level such as a swing high or low, a session range, or the previous day's high or low. A wick is enough; an exact touch isn't.

What makes a valid fair value gap here?

Three candles where the first and third don't overlap, a middle candle body of at least 1 ATR(14), a gap of at least 0.1 ATR, all inside the window and after the sweep.

What is the Silver Bullet target?

The nearest untouched level on the other side. If it's closer than 1.5 times the risk the setup is skipped, and with no level at all the target is 2R.

Does the Silver Bullet work?

Not in the study this page is built on. Over 16 years on four futures markets it lost money after costs in all 92 hour and market tests, and about 1 in 4 trades won.

The magic hour is a myth. The sweep might not be.

With rules built from ICT's teaching, the Silver Bullet hours only look better because they're the busiest hours, and the strategy lost money in every hour on every market tested over 16 years. The parts can still be useful on their own.

  • Use the sweep detection as a trigger and combine it with your own setups.
  • Use it as a very short scalp right as a sweep happens, not as a limit order waiting at the gap.
  • Test a market entry at the sweep. The unfilled setups were mostly the winners.
