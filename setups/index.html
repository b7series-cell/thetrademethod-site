Long or shortNY open · 9:45–11:00 ET · 5-minute ES and NQ

The 10 AM ORB

The first break of the opening range is where most traders get faked out. Set the bias before the bell, take nothing from 9:30 to 9:45, mark the 15-minute range and its 50% line, and let 10:00 show the real direction. The break with your bias is confirmation; the retest that holds is the entry. Stop past the reaction, target the next key level at 1:2 or better, break-even once it works, and expect a shakeout into 11:00.

Long or shortNY morning · 5-minute NQ with a footprint and the GEX map

Gamma Wall Footprint

The wall tells you where, not when. Mark the put wall, call wall and zero gamma before the open. A slow drift into a wall: look for the reaction right at it. A fast push: let it overshoot and take the reclaim. Only take it when the footprint knocks (absorption, stacked imbalances, a delta flip or trapped traders). Stop where the idea is wrong, target the next level, half off at the first reaction. If price loses zero gamma and holds, go with it instead.

Long or shortLondon and NY · 1-minute NQ and ES with a liquidity heatmap

Heatmap Scalp

The chart gives the side, the heatmap gives the moment. Higher lows above VWAP means longs only (the mirror for shorts). On a pullback, when the sellers' big orders stop pushing price lower and the bid holds, get long on the bid. Fixed dollar stop, cut it early if big orders stack against you, add on the next pullback, and get out a few ticks before the big wall of liquidity.

ICT Made Simple

Strip ICT down to what moves the needle. Map it on the high time frame: an obvious trend, a close through the swing, the range from the significant low to high and its 0.5, an order block, breaker or fair value gap in the discount, and the draw on liquidity. When price reaches that POI, zoom in and enter on the low time frame breaker. Stop under the turn, target external range liquidity.

Ready, Aim, Fire

Three pieces, and every trade needs all three. Ready: read the higher time frame, trend or balance. Aim: wait for price to reach a level from yesterday's profile, the overnight range, VWAP or a composite high volume node. Fire: only when order flow on the 5-minute shows one side putting in the effort without getting paid, then a rejection. Stop past the fight, target the next level, 1:1 or better.

Long or shortAny session · 1-hour, 5-minute entries

Fibonacci OTE

Price moves in waves, so don't buy the impulse. In a clean trend, draw the Fibonacci retracement on the latest impulse and let the pullback come to you: a limit at 0.702, the middle of ICT's Optimal Trade Entry (0.618 to 0.786), in discount for a buy and premium for a sell. Stop past the swing, half off at the old high, the rest at the next higher time frame level, and only with room to get there.

Long or shortCash open · weekly/daily map, 1-minute ES and NQ

Micro Range

Price isn't value. Map the regime first: one time framing up or down on the weekly and daily means momentum is allowed; anything else is balance, where you fade failed auctions at the value edges. Never buy the first breakout. Wait for the micro range, the range after the range, then buy the absorbed flush back into it (plan A) or aggressive buying out of it (plan B), with the risk split between them.

Look Above and Fail

Markets range more than they trend, and in a range the money is made at the edges. After the first 30 minutes, mark the developing value area high and low and wait. When aggressive buyers push over the high (sellers under the low) and price comes right back inside, they're trapped: trade the reclaim, stop past the big trades, partial at POC, target the other side of value.

Liquidity Refill

When buyers take liquidity faster than sellers can refill it, price has one way to go. After a relatively big candle out of compression, watch the next candle's first seconds: a quick wick that stays in the top 10–20% and gets absorbed. Buy the break of the first candle's high, stop past the wick, out after three or four candles.

The Strat

Price can only do three things: stay inside the last bar (a 1), take out one side (a 2) or both (a 3). Use the broadening formation as the map and time frame continuity to see who's more aggressive, then wait for the evidence: a simultaneous break, when a whole group takes last week's level at once. Enter in force, stop just past the bar, target the other side.
