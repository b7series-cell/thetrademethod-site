PMH Break

There is one trade here, and it is the break of the premarket high. Not the gap. Not the news. Not the first green candle. Everything else on this page exists to keep you out of the bad versions and get you paid on the good ones.

What is a premarket high break?

A premarket high (PMH) break is an opening-drive trade on the 2-minute chart. When a stock with a real catalyst opens above yesterday's high and the prior close, you buy the close of the first 2-minute candle that closes above the premarket high on heavy volume, with the stop under that candle's low.

Watch one morning, start to finish

1

Before the bell

  • Market daily above its 10 and 20 SMA, ideally trending up. The market's chart, not the stock's.
  • Sector moving at least as hard as the market, preferably harder.
  • A real catalyst: news out premarket or after yesterday's close, with heavy premarket volume. Or a price catalyst, where the location is the story — an all-time high, the top of a long base.
  • Room overhead: no obvious resistance for 3–5%.
  • Write down four numbers: prior close, yesterday's high, premarket high, and the opening print when it comes.

On a trade that lives and dies inside ten minutes, you want the tape helping you. If SPY is red, you are paying to fight it.

2

The gate at 9:30

  • The opening print must be above yesterday's high and above the prior close. Both, not one.
  • If it opens below yesterday's high, there is no opening drive trade here. However good the news is.

Open above yesterday's high and everyone who bought yesterday is green — nobody is sitting there waiting to get out at breakeven. Open below it and you are buying into a crowd that wants its money back, and that crowd is exactly who sells into your breakout.

3

The trigger

  • A 2-minute candle closes above the premarket high, on volume.
  • Closed means closed. Not touched, not wicked through, not "it's above right now."
  • Relative volume above 3 — roughly three standard deviations above what is normal for that minute of the day over the last five sessions.

The premarket high is the most anyone would pay all morning, with hours to study the chart and the catalyst. When it goes on volume, that argument is settled. Until then you are guessing inside the range.

4

Check the candle

  • Real green body. Upper wick no bigger than half the body.
  • Closes red → skip. Never closed above the level → it was a false breakout.

A long upper wick means somebody sold into the breakout hard enough to shove price back down before the candle finished. That is not strength; that is someone unloading into your enthusiasm.

5

Entry and stop

  • Entry is the close of the breakout candle. Not the level.
  • Stop goes under the low of that same candle.
  • Size from the stop distance, never from the level.

A premarket high sitting right on top of the open tells you the trigger will come fast. It does not tell you the stop will be tight. On real examples the risk runs 2.5–5% per share.

6

Manage it

  • Trim into strength at whole numbers, prior resistance, or roughly 1 ATR from the open.
  • Trail the runner out on the first 2-minute close under the 9 EMA.
  • Early exit: a 2-minute close back below the premarket high after you are in. Do not wait for the stop.

It is a momentum trade. You are not predicting the high of the day, you are paying yourself as it moves.

The whole trade on one chart

Gate levels, the stuff that traps you, the candle that pays you, and where you get out.

The close is the confirmation

In the middle of a forming candle, a real break and a stuff look identical. That is the whole problem. Stop trying to tell them apart live — wait for the close and look.

The trigger does not run on a clock

These feel like three different trades. They are one trade, and the trigger never changed.

Straight through

The first candle takes the level and you are in at 9:32. Of the setups studied, this is the strongest version.

Sideways first

It grinds inside the range for a few minutes, then goes. Same trigger, later.

Flush, then go

It sells off hard off the open, flushes everyone out, snaps back and takes the level. Same trigger.

The four filters

These do not find the trade — the trigger does that. They exist to kill the bad versions before you are in one.

1  The wick

Price touches the level, pushes through, you buy, you are green for six seconds, then it stuffs and closes back underneath.

Kills: buying inside a forming candle.

2  The catalyst

Fresh news in the premarket or after the close, with heavy premarket volume behind it — or a location significant enough to be its own catalyst.

Kills: breakouts nobody is watching.

3  Room overhead

No obvious resistance within 3–5% of entry. If there is a wall just above you, you are buying somebody else's exit.

Kills: 20-cent winners.

4  Market and sector

Strong market daily, strong group on the day. The tape decides whether momentum gets rewarded this morning or sold into.

Kills: good setups on bad days.

Size off the stop, never off the level

Rocket Lab's premarket high sat 27 cents above the open. The candle that broke it closed two dollars higher. Your fill is the close, so your risk is measured from there — and it will often feel like a chase.

Risk budget for the trade$500.00
Entry — close of breakout candle105.05
Stop — low of that candle103.10
Risk per share$1.95
Position size256 shares

The mistake this prevents

Size off the level instead and you measure risk as 105.05 − 104.00 = $1.05, which says buy 476 shares.

476 × $1.95 = $928 at your actual stop.

Nearly double the risk you budgeted, taken before the trade even had a chance to work.

Example: one morning, 2-minute bars with pre-market

An example session run through the indicator's own rules: a gap up that opens at 102.95, above yesterday's high of 102.40 and close of 101.80, under a pre-market high of 104.00. The 9:32 candle pokes above 104.00 and closes back under it, so nothing fires. The 9:36 candle closes at 105.05 on more than 4× normal volume for that time of day, glows yellow and gets the LONG marker. The stop goes under its low at 103.10. Price never comes back to the pre-market high and tops out just over 109 by 10:40. The bars are made up to show the setup clearly. They aren't a real ticker.

Questions

What has to be true before the open?

The market's daily chart above its 10 and 20 SMA, the sector moving at least as hard as the market, a real catalyst with heavy premarket volume, and no obvious resistance for 3 to 5%.

What is the 9:30 gate?

The opening print must be above both yesterday's high and the prior close. If it opens below yesterday's high, there's no opening drive trade, however good the news is.

What does the breakout candle need?

A real green body that closes above the premarket high, an upper wick no bigger than half the body, and relative volume above 3. A wick through the level without a close doesn't count.

Does the premarket high break work?

Not in our test: 188 trades on 2-minute bars across SPY, QQQ and 10 large-cap stocks from Nov 2021 to Nov 2026 had a 37% win rate, a 0.94 profit factor and -0.03R per trade.

The trade is the premarket high break. Everything else is filters and management.

The story changes every single morning. The trade does not. That is the entire reason to run it mechanically — it removes the guesswork from the fastest part of the day.

  • Trading the gap instead of the break is not a setup, it is a feeling. No level, no trigger, no idea where you are wrong.
  • Opening drives move fast. Paper trade it first, or size it small enough that being wrong feels boring.
  • If it closes back under the premarket high after you are in, that was the whole thesis. Leave.
