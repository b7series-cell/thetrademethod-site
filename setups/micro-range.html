Micro Range

Price isn't value. A burst through the top of a range is only price until buyers and sellers agree to do business up there. So map the regime first, never buy the first breakout, and wait for the range after the range: then buy the absorbed flush back into it, or aggressive buying out of it, with the risk split between the two.

What is a micro range?

A micro range is the small range price builds after it breaks out of a bigger one, from an institutional trader's breakout filter for ES and NQ. A burst out of a range is only price, not value. When price goes sideways beyond the old value, business is being done up there, and that range after the range is what you trade.

Watch one micro range, start to finish

1

Map the regime first

  • On the weekly and daily, check one time framing: a higher high, a higher low and a higher close (up), or the mirror (down).
  • One time framing means one side controls the auction: momentum and breakouts are allowed. Anything else is balance.
  • In balance, don't trade momentum. Fade failed auctions at the value edges instead.

Most day traders, he says, have won or lost the session before the first trade. It comes down to the prep.

2

Filter it with the fundamentals

  • Every number (CPI, PCE, NFP, GDP, a headline) through one lens: does it push the Fed to cut or to hike? The mandate is inflation and employment.
  • Accommodative money flows into growth and risk. Restrictive money rotates out. Don't stand in front of the first.
  • A release matters when the actual is far from what was expected. Don't trade its immediate aftermath; use it for direction and conviction.

Every institution analyzes the fundamentals. None of them are looking at one-minute fair value gaps.

3

Mark the zones

  • Start with the developing weekly profile: value area, point of control, high and low volume nodes. Mark areas, not exact lines.
  • Then the cash session profiles (merge overlapping days into a composite) and the overnight. Where the zones line up across time frames, they matter.
  • Note where you open against yesterday's value. Inside it, expect a fight: you need acceptance outside a control zone first.

Most liquidity sits in the middle of a range, at the point of control, not above the highs.

4

Skip the first breakout

  • A burst out of the range is price, not value. Breakouts are the fat tails: most of the time the market is in balance and they fail.
  • Wait for the micro range: price going sideways beyond the old value. Business being done up there is acceptance.
  • Never front-run a breakout unless you're doing it from value.

Who builds value? Not retail. A range forming past the old one is institutional participation.

5

Plan A, then plan B

  • Plan A, the best price: aggressive sellers flush under the micro range low and get absorbed, big buyers fire, price reclaims. Buy the reclaim.
  • Plan B: aggressive buying breaks the micro range high. This is the breakout to trust, because it comes out of value.
  • Stops sit behind the aggressive traders on your side. Split one risk budget between A and B; don't add risk.

Shorts are the mirror: an absorbed squeeze over the micro range high, or aggressive selling under its low.

6

Ride it until the buyers run out

  • In an imbalance, old value should be a trampoline. A pullback into it that gets absorbed is the same setup again.
  • Target the next zone up. Get out where buyers keep lifting the offer and passive sellers keep filling them.
  • If the market slices back through old value or a low volume node and doesn't turn, the other time frame isn't there. Out.

Institutions take profit into strength. Someone has to buy from them up there: don't be the last one.

Price is not value

All the market does is negotiate fair value. A news candle can rip, but if buyers and sellers don't keep doing business up there, it's given straight back. A trend, to him, is value migrating: one balancing range after another, each a little higher.

BALANCE

Value is a magnet

Buyers at the low, sellers at the high, the point of control in the middle where the most business is done. Pokes outside the value area get violently rejected.

IMBALANCE

Value is a trampoline

Business is done, the market goes looking for a new fair price. Old value should launch price, not pull it back. Low volume nodes are air pockets the other time frame defends.

THE SHIFT

Buyers raise their bids

Higher lows inside the range, the passive offers thinning above, patience turning into urgency before a catalyst. Then the lid comes off.

One time framing: check your map

His rule is stricter than the textbook one. Up needs a higher high, a higher low and a higher close than the last bar; down is the mirror. He applies it to the weekly and the daily before every session. Try the presets or type your own bars.

One time framing up

Buyers are firmly in control. Even a day that gaps down and looks scary counts if it holds the last low and closes higher.

Trade: momentum, breakouts out of value. Not reversals.

Balance

Neither. Candles sweep highs and lows and look like they'll break out, then don't. The choppy stuff people complain about online: the thing that happens 70% of the time.

Trade: the failed auction at the value edges.

One time framing down

Sellers in control. Buying the bottom for a pullback into the middle of the range is a low expected value play.

Trade: momentum shorts. Watch for it reaching the edge of a bigger structure.

In balance: the failed auction, two flavors

His bread and butter when the map says balance. Same idea underneath both: the market isn't done doing business in this range, so a push outside it rotates back through value. The location is what makes it work.

V-SHAPE

The trap snaps shut

Big red sell bubbles under the value area low, then a long wick back in with violent buyers. Cross the spread: buy at market as it reclaims, not with a limit. The aggressive buyers are your shield for the stop.

SLOW BURN

Value fails outside

No big bubbles. Price tries to build value over the high, can't, and breaks back in. Wait for value to build lower: a micro range under the high. Sell its top on aggressive selling (A), or aggressive selling under it (B).

TARGETS

Point of control, then the other side

The point of control is the intermediate target: scale out, trim, move the stop to entry. Then the opposite edge of value. Other time frame traders stepped in and said: not yet.

Plan the split

He doesn't add risk when plan B fires after plan A. He splits the risk he was always going to take between them. Type the levels; the defaults are the walkthrough's long. The dollar risk and the point value (ES, $50) are only examples.

Plan A's stop goes to entry once plan B fills.

Who is on the other side

Retail traders are very good at buying the exact high and selling the exact low. It isn't a conspiracy. Their idea of what the market is doing is the opposite of what the biggest players need it to do.

DAY TIME FRAME

Day traders, algos, CTAs

Intraday to daily horizons, small momentum funds, trend-following commodity trading advisors. They want price to hit X before Y today.

OTHER TIME FRAME

Hedge, pension and mutual funds

Months to years. They can't buy a billion at market: they need stable prices and ranges to fill over days or weeks. Balance is a feature, not a bug.

EXIT LIQUIDITY

They sell into strength

A big holder takes profit while the market rips, with passive offers absorbing every buyer, and lets them think they're winning. When the last buyer is exhausted, the rotation comes.

How you trade it

The whole thing, in order.

  1. Map the regime on the weekly and daily: strict one time framing up, down, or balance.
  2. Read the macro through the Fed's two jobs, inflation and employment. Note any releases and what's expected.
  3. Mark the zones: weekly profile, cash session profiles (composite if they overlap), overnight. Keep the ones that line up.
  4. Balance: trade the failed auction at the value edges, V-shape or slow burn, to the point of control and the other side.
  5. Imbalance: skip the first breakout. Wait for the micro range to form beyond the old value.
  6. Plan A: the flush that gets absorbed, buy the reclaim, stop behind the aggressive buyers. Plan B: aggressive buying out of the micro range, stop back inside.
  7. Split one risk budget between A and B. Stop to entry on A once B fills.
  8. Manage: old value should hold as a trampoline. Out at the next zone where buyers stop getting paid.

Questions

What is one time framing?

On the weekly and daily, a higher high, a higher low and a higher close than the last bar (up), or the mirror (down). It means one side controls the auction. Anything else is balance.

Why skip the first breakout?

A spike out of the range with no value built beyond it is price, not value. The market is in balance about 70% of the time, and those first breakouts usually fail.

What are plan A and plan B?

Plan A buys an absorbed flush under the micro range low as price reclaims it. Plan B buys aggressive buying through the micro range high. One risk budget is split between the two, not added to.

What do you do when the market is in balance?

Don't trade momentum. Fade failed auctions at the value area edges, with the point of control as the first target and the other side of value next.
