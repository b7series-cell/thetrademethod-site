1

Confirm the uptrend before anything else

  • Price should be trading above its 20, 50, and 200-period simple moving averages, with the 50-period average sloping upward — a well-established uptrend, not a guess at one.
  • That rising 50-period average is very often exactly where the base of the cup forms, so this isn't a separate check from the pattern — it's the foundation the pattern needs to be trading in.

A cup and handle that forms outside of a real uptrend is just a random rounding shape. The trend is what gives the pattern a directional reason to resolve higher.

2

Read the cup through its volume, not just its shape

  • A healthy cup has a smooth, rounded bottom. Volume tells you whether it's real: heavy selling volume early as resistance kicks in and profit-taking starts, then volume tapering off as price settles into the base.
  • As the bottom forms and price starts to recover, volume should tick back up above its own moving average — a sign that buying interest, not just an absence of sellers, is what's turning the stock higher.

The shape of a cup can be drawn on almost any chart in hindsight. The volume pattern underneath it is what tells you whether the move is actually driven by changing participation.

3

Confirm the handle is tightening, not falling apart

  • As the handle forms just below the old resistance, volume should taper further — back down below its average — showing that selling pressure is becoming less aggressive, not more.
  • When the breakout actually comes, look for a real expansion of volume on the move through resistance, not a quiet drift above the line.

A handle with rising volume is often still under real selling pressure. A handle with fading volume is a stock quietly running out of sellers right before it needs to.

4

Check for clean air, then check the sector

  • Look left on the chart for old support or resistance zones above the breakout level. In the ideal case, it's clean air — plenty of room for price to run once it clears the cup's resistance.
  • Then check sector alignment: is the broader sector also showing strength, with the majority of its stocks participating in the same move? It's even better when the broader market is confirming on top of that — stock bullish, sector bullish, market bullish, all three together.

This single filter — sector alignment — may save more bad trades than anything else on the checklist. A great chart fighting an unconfirmed sector and market is still fighting the tide.

5

Trigger only on the break — never the prediction

  • Enter with a stop order above the cup's resistance and the handle's high, not by anticipating the breakout before it happens. Predicting the break isn't the same as the market actually confirming it.
  • The market doesn't pay you for being first into a trade. It pays you for being right — and price confirmation is what proves the theory of the trade instead of just hoping for it.

A stop order above the trigger level means you only ever enter with the market already agreeing with you, at the cost of never catching the exact low of the move.

6

Size the stop off the ATR, then scale out in two targets

  • The "baby bear" stop: take the Average True Range and multiply by 1.5 — tighter than a 2.0× position-trading stop, looser than a 1.2× scalp trail. Not too tight, not too loose.
  • Set two profit targets: a conservative one where you scale out half the position and move the rest of the trade risk-free, and a second, still-conservative target well short of the textbook full measured-move objective.

The stop distance and the position size are decided together, off a fixed dollar risk you're fully comfortable with — before the market opens, not while the trade is moving against you.

Which breakout you can trust

The same rounded shape can be a real cup and handle or a trap. What surrounds it decides which.

The trifecta — stacking real odds

One bullish chart is a candidate. Three bullish charts confirming together is an edge.

Stock bullish

Price above its 20, 50, and 200-period moving averages, with the 50 sloping upward through the cup's base.

Sector bullish

The sector (or its tracking ETF) also pressing toward new highs, with the majority of its stocks participating — a sign of real institutional rotation.

Market bullish

The broader index confirming on top of both. A rising tide lifts all boats — three-for-three is what stacks the odds.

The four filters

These don't build the cup — the uptrend and the volume pattern do that. They keep you off the versions that only look like it.

1  Trading the prediction, not the break

Entering because the pattern looks ready, before price has actually confirmed the breakout with a real trigger.

Kills: being in the trade before the market has agreed with you.

2  Volume that doesn't taper

A "cup" where volume rises instead of fades through the base and handle — real selling pressure that never actually let up.

Kills: trading a base that's still under active distribution.

3  No room to run

An old support or resistance zone sitting close above the breakout level, capping the move before it can develop.

Kills: a clean breakout that stalls right into overhead supply.

4  Ignoring the sector and market

A great-looking individual chart taken while its sector and the broader market are weak, flat, or unconfirmed.

Kills: fighting the tide instead of stacking probability in your favor.

Size the stop off the ATR, not a guess

The "baby bear" technique: not too tight (1.2× ATR, built for a fast scalp trail), not too loose (2.0× ATR, built for position trading) — 1.5× splits the difference for a swing hold.

ATR on the daily chart8.00
Stop distance — 1.5× ATR (baby bear)12.00
Position size — kept under a $500 risk cap30 shares
Total risk on the trade$360.00

The mistake this prevents

Set the stop tighter than the ATR justifies — say, less than the 8-point average candle range — and ordinary intraday movement alone has a high probability of hitting it before the trade ever gets a chance to work.

Go the other way and use a full 2.0× ATR position-trading stop on a swing trade, and it's simply too loose — giving back more than the trade type calls for.

1.5× ATR gives the trade room to breathe without being so wide that a real reversal signal gets ignored.

Risk An 8-point ATR reading set a 12-point stop (1.5× ATR), sized at 30 shares to cap total dollar risk near $360 — comfortably under a self-imposed $500 ceiling.

Reward : risk If both targets fill as planned: roughly $300 from target one plus $600 from target two, a $900 total reward against the $360 risked — about a 2.5:1 reward-to-risk ratio.

Plan the trade. Trade the plan.

A great chart on its own is worthless without every one of these decisions made before the market opens: the entry trigger, the stop, both targets, and the position size that fits your risk.

  • You don't need fifty setups. One or two you understand completely, checked the same five ways every time, is enough.
  • Enter on the break, never the prediction. The market pays for being right, not for being first.
  • Decide the stop, both targets, and the position size together, off a fixed dollar risk — before you're in the trade, not while it's moving.
