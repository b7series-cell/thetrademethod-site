The 10 AM ORB

The first break of the opening range is where most traders get faked out. Bias first: know which side the day is on before the bell. Wait: no trades from 9:30 to 9:45, mark the 15-minute range and its 50% line, and let 10:00 show the real direction. Enter on the retest: the break with your bias is confirmation, the pullback that holds is the entry. Stop past the reaction, target the next key level, break-even as soon as it works.

What is the 10 AM ORB?

The 10 AM ORB is an opening range breakout for ES and NQ futures that waits for the clock. You set a bias from the larger time frame before the open, take no trades between 9:30 and 9:45 ET when fakeouts are most common, and mark the high, low and 50% of the first 15 minutes. The first break in the direction of your bias is confirmation, not the entry. Around 10:00, when the real direction for the day usually shows, you enter on the retest of the range edge or a higher low (lower high for shorts), with the stop past the reaction and the target at the next key level.

1

Bias: the larger point of view first

  • Before the session, do the top-down read: is the bigger trend up or down? That's the only side you trade today.
  • A break in the same direction as your bias is a confluence. A break against it is the low-probability side.
  • Check the calendar. Level 3 news or FOMC: skip the day, especially if you're new.

Before, the coach in the video took whichever way it broke: long over the high, short under the low. It didn't work over time. Adding a daily bias is what changed it.

2

Wait: nothing before 9:45

  • The traditional ORB trades the break of the first 5 or 15 minutes. That window, 9:30 to 9:45 ET, has the most fakeouts of the day.
  • Volume is at its highest and big money is placing its orders. The easiest way to get a good fill is to trap the early breakout traders.
  • So you don't take a trade before 9:45. If you're not a lightning-fast 15-second scalper, waiting is the edge.

You know the direction, you're confident, you enter at the open, you get chopped out, and then it goes your way. Waiting fixes that one.

3

Mark: the range, its 50%, the key levels

  • At 9:45, mark the high and low of the first 15 minutes. At 10:00 the 30-minute range prints too.
  • Draw the 50% line. On a big range it's often the most important level of the morning.
  • The top, the 50% and the bottom of the range are key pivot levels (KPLs). Add the bigger ones: yesterday's high and low, premarket, higher time frame levels.

These are where you look for reactions and where you aim. Trades go from one key level to the next.

4

10:00: the true direction for the day

  • 9:45 to 10:00 is for finding the setup. Institutions are still positioning, so fakeouts are still common.
  • Most of the time the real move starts around 10:00, with the best entries from 10:00 to 10:15.
  • No break out of the range between 10:00 and 10:30? Call it a range day and wait for the next 1-hour candle.

Not set in stone. Nothing in the market is. It's what the probabilities favour, tested over a stretch of 30 days or more, not one day.

5

Enter: on the retest, not the break

  • Price breaks the range in the direction of your bias. That confirms your thesis. It isn't the entry.
  • Wait for support to form: a retest of the range high, or (better) a deeper pullback that makes a higher low. For shorts, a retest of the low or a lower high.
  • Enter as it leaves that level, around 10:00. The stop goes just past the reaction candle or the retest low, or outside the 15-minute range.

Market structure 101: higher lows in an up move, lower highs in a down move. The retest proves the break was real.

6

Manage: base hits, break-even, 11:00

  • Target the next key level at 1:2 or better. Once it moves your way, stop to break-even.
  • Inside a wide range, take base hits from one level to the next, not the whole move. Once the move has happened, don't chase the break.
  • The 1-hour candle prints at 11:00. Expect a shakeout into it, and the trend to resume after.

A trend band helps you stay in on trending days: a pullback into the bands is a place to add, not to panic sell.

How you trade it

Start to finish, for one morning.

  1. Before the open: do the top-down read and write down the bias for the day. Mark the key levels. Check the calendar: level 3 news or FOMC means no trade.
  2. 9:30 to 9:45: watch, don't trade. Let the open fake out the breakout traders.
  3. 9:45: mark the 15-minute range high, low and 50%. If the range is huge (news), the 50% line is your level.
  4. 9:45 to 10:00: look for the setup. A break in the direction of your bias confirms it. Don't take that break.
  5. 10:00 to 10:30: enter on the retest of the range edge, or a higher low (lower high for shorts) as price leaves it. Stop past the reaction candle, target the next key level at 1:2 or better.
  6. Manage: stop to break-even once it works. Base hits from level to level; add on pullbacks into the trend bands on a trending day.
  7. Into 11:00: expect a shakeout while the 1-hour candle prints. No break by 10:30 means a range day: wait for the next hour, or be done.

Questions

Why not trade the first break of the opening range?

Because 9:30 to 9:45 is when volume is highest and big money is filling orders, and the easiest fills come from trapping breakout traders. The first break is often faked out in both directions before the real move starts, usually around 10:00.

What is the 50% rule?

When the opening range is very large, often from news, a break of its high or low comes too late. The halfway line of the range becomes the key level instead: in the direction of your bias, look for a reaction there and take a base hit back to the range edge.

Where does the stop go?

Just past the reaction candle or the retest low (high for shorts), or outside the 15-minute range. The coach in the video risks about $250 to $350 a trade, aims for 1:2 or 1:3, and moves the stop to break-even once the trade works.

What happens at 11:00?

The 1-hour candle prints at 11:00, and price often shakes out against the trend into it before the trend resumes. If nothing broke out of the range between 10:00 and 10:30, treat it as a range day and wait for the next hourly candle.

When should you not trade it?

Before 9:45, without a bias or against it, on level 3 news and FOMC days, and after the move has already run from the range.
