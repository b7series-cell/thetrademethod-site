What is a technical catalyst breakout?

A technical catalyst breakout is a breakout with no news behind it, where the level itself is the catalyst. From an SMB Capital video, the stock or ETF clears a 9 or 10 out of 10 higher-timeframe level it couldn't break for weeks, on elevated relative volume, with a volume spike on the breakout bar and buyers paying the offer.

Watch one breakout, start to finish

1

A 9 out of 10 level on the longer-term chart

  • Not a random intraday squiggle, and not a 6 out of 10 inside a sloppy range. A level the stock or ETF couldn't clear for weeks or months.
  • Judge it on the higher timeframe and the intraday chart together. Ask every time: on a scale of 1 to 10, how important is this level?
  • ETHA had been capped under 19.12 for over a month, and Ethereum under 2,550 for weeks: four to five weeks of built-up energy.

When a product can't get above a price for a month and then pushes through, that breach is a catalyst on its own.

2

Elevated relative volume before the break

  • News brings attention; volume brings confirmation. Even without a headline, RVOL has to say big money is involved.
  • Going into Vince's session, full-day RVOL was running over two times normal.
  • Two or three times volume: real order flow. Pressing a monthly ceiling on half its normal volume: a trap.

If RVOL isn't elevated, that's a huge red flag. Maybe you don't stink at momentum trades. Maybe you just take them without a 9 and without volume.

3

A volume spike on the exact breakout bar

  • Look for the see-it moment: the instant the character of the stock changes in front of you.
  • ETHA printed normal volume all morning. The second it cleared 19.12, 1-minute volume went vertical.
  • That's buyers and short covering stepping all over each other.

Volume acceleration on top of a 9 out of 10 daily level is the trigger to get involved.

4

Read the tape to confirm the break

  • Buyers are aggressively paying the offer, and the offers clear without friction, easier than expected.
  • Pullbacks are remarkably shallow, on decreasing volume. Buyers step right back up.
  • It goes higher and faster than anyone expected.

Trades printing on the offer, not the bid, are aggressive accumulation. Big money doesn't just want it, it needs it.

5

Market tailwinds set the size

  • Ask what the most relevant ETF, index or related product is doing, and use it as an indicator.
  • Lined up with the breakout: green light for maximum conviction, A size.
  • Vince's day: SPY near the low end of its range, Bitcoin and Ethereum still in their ranges. So he started with B size.

When the breakout is isolated, respect the setup but control the first risk.

6

Set a clear stop

  • Get long as the level clears (19.13 and up). The stop goes under the low of the breakout candle, or the base right before the break.
  • A real breakout works almost immediately. If it creeps back under the multi-week level, the trade is dead: scratch it or take the planned loss.
  • Never park the stop on a round number like 10, 20 or 25 cents. Give it a penny more: 9, 14 or 24 cents.

That one extra penny keeps you out of a lot of random liquidity sweeps.

7

Be ready to add to the winner

  • When it doesn't come back down and holds higher, don't just sit on your hands.
  • Find the consolidation pauses on the 1-minute chart. Put buy stops just above the wick highs, where sellers stepped in for a moment.
  • Add into each new push, and trail the stop up to the low of the consolidation.

The adds go in only after the trade has proven itself, and each one comes with a tighter stop behind the whole position.

8

A defined exit. Don't negotiate.

  • Use one of three structural exits: a break of the prior 2-minute bar low, a breakdown of a clean consolidation, or a distinct shift on the tape.
  • After charging to 19.60, ETHA's tape changed: buyers stopped lifting offers, sellers hit the bids, and the velocity died. Vince flattened.
  • No shorting the top. Recognize the exhaustion and get out.

And if it never gets going at all, scratch it: flat at break even or a two-cent loss, not a full stop.

Score every level, 1 to 10

The habit SMB trains: every time you spot a level, grade it. Only the top of the scale is a trade.

9 – 10: trade it

A multi-week or multi-month ceiling on the daily chart that kept turning price away. The breach is the news.

6 – 7: pass

A level inside a sloppy range. Aggressive momentum on a mediocre level is how breakout traders get chopped up.

4: not a level

A random intraday squiggle. It doesn't belong anywhere near your size.

What kills it

Any one of these and there's no trade, a smaller one, or a scratch.

Not a 9

A 6 or 7 out of 10 level in a messy range. Mixing these in with the 9s and 10s is what turns results flat.

Under 9/10: skip it.

No RVOL

A monthly ceiling attempted on normal or half volume. There's no real order flow behind it.

Not elevated: red flag, no trade.

No volume on the bar

Price drifts through the level with nothing on the tape. No see-it moment, no change in character.

No spike: no trigger.

No velocity

You paid the new high and nothing happened. Bids soften, the tape dies. The reason you took it is gone.

Scratch it: flat at even or −2 cents.

The edge is the waiting

What Mike Bellafiore sees on the SMB floor: developing traders mix their 9 and 10 out of 10 breakouts with far too many 6s, 7s and frankly 4s. The morning fills up with paper cuts, the day ends flat or red, and the setup gets the blame. It isn't the setup. Sitting in your seat and waiting for only the best technical breakouts is the edge.

9 – 10

The only ones to take

Weeks or months of failures at one price on the higher timeframe. Clean chart, clear level, and the market is watching it too.

6 – 7

The chop machine

Looks like a breakout, sits inside a sloppy range. Taken with momentum size, these are the trades that bleed you.

4

Not a level at all

An intraday line drawn to have something to trade. Then the excuses start: breakouts don't work, it had no news.

A size, B size, or no trade?

Tick what's true about the breakout in front of you. The first four decide whether it's a trade at all; the market decides how big. It starts the way Vince's ETHA trade looked.

Stop blaming the setup

“Technical breakouts just don't work in this market.” “It didn't have a news catalyst, that's why it failed.” “I guess I just suck at breakouts.” No. Taking too many low-quality, undisciplined breakouts doesn't work.

Take only 9 and 10 out of 10 breakouts. That is the edge.

And don't paralyze yourself staring at an empty news feed when a 9 is breaking on heavy volume. Asking “where's the headline?” while it rips another 5% without you is its own mistake. As Brian Shannon puts it, only price pays.

Learn to scratch

To trade this with real size you have to be able to scratch. You pay a new high expecting instant momentum. If the velocity simply isn't there, the bids soften and the tape dies, get flat right away at break even or a two-cent loss. Don't sit there and take the full stop.

Exit 1: the 2-minute bar

Price breaks the low of the prior 2-minute bar. The push is over.

Exit 2: the consolidation

A clean consolidation that held breaks down. The base under the move is gone.

Exit 3: the tape

A distinct shift: buyers stop lifting, sellers hit the bids, upward velocity dies.

The two charts are illustrative. A scratch isn't a failure; it's what lets you take the next 9 out of 10 at full size.

A stop a penny past the round number

The stop goes under the low of the breakout candle. If that lands on a round number (any multiple of 5 cents), take one more penny. The default numbers are the walkthrough's, not Vince's fills, and the dollar risk is only an example.

  • .10 .20 .25round: where the sweeps go
  • .09 .14 .24one penny more
  • Back underthe level: the trade is dead

A real breakout works almost immediately

The structural stop is under the breakout candle or the base before the break. But you shouldn't need it often. If price creeps back under the multi-week level, the reason for the trade is gone.

Scratch it or take the planned loss. Never a bigger one.

B size here is half the A-size risk, just as an example. Vince started at B size because SPY, Bitcoin and Ethereum weren't helping, then added as the trade proved itself.

The indicator

One TradingView script for the setup (Pine Script v6). It finds the multi-week level on the daily chart, checks RVOL and the breakout bar's volume, sizes by the market, and draws the stop, the adds and the exits on your 1-minute chart. It marks and alerts. It does not place trades.

  • Technical Catalyst Breakout

    alerts

    The level is the highest high of the last 25 completed days, and it only counts with at least 3 tests over at least 15 days (the panel shows both). A close through it flags BREAKOUT when time-of-day RVOL is 2× or more and the bar's volume is 3× its recent average; without them it marks “No trade”. It labels A or B size from SPY against its VWAP (set any symbol, like Ethereum for ETHA), puts the stop under the breakout candle a penny past any round number, marks adds over tight pauses with the stop trailed under each one, and exits on the stop, a close back through the level, a scratch after 5 bars with no progress, or a break of the prior two bars' low once the trade is +2R. Every number is a setting.

    Two things stay with you: whether the level is really a 9 out of 10, and reading the tape. Pine can't see the order flow, so the scratch rule stands in for “the velocity isn't there.” Shorts (a break below a multi-week floor) are off by default; switch Trade to Both.

// =====================================================================
// Technical Catalyst Breakout — thetrademethod.com
//
// SMB Capital's eight-step breakout blueprint for when there is no
// headline: the level IS the catalyst.
//
//   1. The level   The highest high (or lowest low) of the last N
//                  completed daily bars, tested several times and held
//                  for weeks. The panel shows the tests and the days.
//                  Whether it's a 9 out of 10 is still your call.
//   2. RVOL        Today's volume so far against what the stock has
//                  normally traded by this time of day (20 sessions).
//   3. The bar     The breakout bar's volume against the recent average.
//   4. The tape    Pine can't read the tape. Watch it yourself; the
//                  scratch rule below is the stand-in for "no velocity".
//   5. The market  A related symbol (SPY by default) above its VWAP for a
//                  long, below it for a short: A size. Otherwise B size.
//   6. The stop    Under the breakout candle (or the base before it), and
//                  a penny past any round number (multiples of 5 cents).
//   7. The add     A tight pause after the entry gets a buy stop a tick
//                  over its high; the stop trails to a tick under its low.
//   8. The exit    The trailing stop, a close back through the level, a
//                  break of the prior bars' low once the trade is paying,
//                  or a scratch when price hasn't moved after N bars.
//
// Use an intraday chart (1-minute in the video). Marks and alerts only;
// it places no orders.
// =====================================================================

// ---------------- 1 · the level, from completed daily bars ----------------
dayLevels(float h, float l, int len, float tol) =>
    float hi = ta.highest(h, len)
    float lo = ta.lowest(l, len)
    int tu = 0
    int td = 0
    int au = 0
    int ad = 0
    for i = 0 to len - 1
        if h[i] >= hi * (1 - tol / 100)
            tu += 1
            au := i + 1
        if l[i] <= lo * (1 + tol / 100)
            td += 1
            ad := i + 1
    [hi, tu, au, lo, td, ad]

// high[1]/low[1] with lookahead on = the previous completed days only (no repainting)
[lvHi, testsHi, daysHi, lvLo, testsLo, daysLo] = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", dayLevels(high[1], low[1], lenD, tolPct), lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)
bool qualHi = testsHi >= minTests and daysHi >= minDays
bool qualLo = testsLo >= minTests and daysLo >= minDays

// ---------------- 2 · cumulative RVOL by time of day ----------------
bool intra = timeframe.isintraday
int  tfSec = timeframe.in_seconds()
var int cols = intra ? int(math.ceil(86400.0 / tfSec)) : 1
int secOfDay = hour(time, syminfo.timezone) * 3600 + minute(time, syminfo.timezone) * 60 + second(time, syminfo.timezone)
int slot     = intra ? math.min(int(secOfDay / tfSec), cols - 1) : 0

var matrix<float> hist  = matrix.new<float>(0, cols, na)
var array<float>  today = array.new<float>(cols, na)
var float cum      = 0.0
var int   lastSlot = -1

fillSlots(array<float> a, int fromIdx, int toIdx, float v) =>
    if toIdx >= fromIdx
        for i = fromIdx to toIdx
            a.set(i, v)

avgAt(matrix<float> m, int c, int n) =>
    int rows = m.rows()
    int k    = math.min(n, rows)
    float sum = 0.0
    if k > 0
        for r = rows - k to rows - 1
            float x = m.get(r, c)
            sum += na(x) ? 0.0 : x
    k > 0 ? sum / k : na

float rvol = na
if intra
    if session.isfirstbar and lastSlot >= 0
        fillSlots(today, lastSlot + 1, cols - 1, cum)
        hist.add_row(hist.rows(), today.copy())
        if hist.rows() > lenRvol
            hist.remove_row(0)
        today.fill(na)
        cum      := 0.0
        lastSlot := -1
    float prevCum = cum
    cum += nz(volume)
    if lastSlot >= 0
        fillSlots(today, lastSlot + 1, slot - 1, prevCum)
    today.set(slot, cum)
    lastSlot := math.max(lastSlot, slot)
    float a = avgAt(hist, slot, lenRvol)
    rvol := a > 0 ? cum / a : na

// ---------------- 3 · breakout bar volume ----------------
float avgVol = ta.sma(volume, barVolLen)[1]
float volX   = avgVol > 0 ? volume / avgVol : na

// ---------------- 5 · the market ----------------
float mktClose = request.security(mktSym, timeframe.period, close)
float mktVwap  = request.security(mktSym, timeframe.period, ta.vwap(hlc3))

// ---------------- helpers ----------------
float tick = syminfo.mintick
// a stop for side d (1 long, -1 short) moved one more tick away when it lands on a round number
pennyPast(float p, int d) =>
    float q = math.round(p / syminfo.mintick) * syminfo.mintick
    bool isRound = roundStep > 0 and math.abs(q / roundStep - math.round(q / roundStep)) < 1e-6
    usePenny and isRound ? q - d * syminfo.mintick : q

float atr     = ta.atr(14)
float pHi     = ta.highest(high, pauseLen)[1]
float pLo     = ta.lowest(low, pauseLen)[1]
float baseLo  = ta.lowest(low, baseLen)[1]
float baseHi  = ta.highest(high, baseLen)[1]
float exLo    = ta.lowest(low, barExN)[1]
float exHi    = ta.highest(high, barExN)[1]

// ---------------- the trade ----------------
var int   pos      = 0        // 1 long, -1 short, 0 flat
var float lvl      = na
var float entry    = na
var float stop     = na
var float risk     = na
var int   entryBar = na
var int   lastAdd  = na
var int   adds     = 0
var float best     = na
var bool  usedHi   = false
var bool  usedLo   = false
var string lastMsg = "Waiting for a qualified level to break"

bool newDay = timeframe.change("D")
if newDay
    usedHi := false
    usedLo := false

bool allowLong  = side != "Short only"
bool allowShort = side != "Long only"
bool rvolOK = not useRvol or (not na(rvol) and rvol >= minRvol)
bool barOK  = not useBarVol or (not na(volX) and volX >= barVolX)

bool sigLong  = false
bool sigShort = false
bool sigAdd   = false
bool sigExit  = false
float exitPx  = na
string exitWhy = ""

// manage an open trade (from the bar after the entry)
if pos != 0 and bar_index > entryBar
    best := pos == 1 ? math.max(best, high) : math.min(best, low)
    float gainR = (best - entry) * pos / risk
    float nowR  = (close - entry) * pos / risk
    if (pos == 1 and low <= stop) or (pos == -1 and high >= stop)
        sigExit := true
        exitPx := stop
        exitWhy := adds > 0 ? "Trailing stop" : "Stop"
    else if (pos == 1 and close < lvl) or (pos == -1 and close > lvl)
        sigExit := true
        exitPx := close
        exitWhy := "Back through the level: dead"
    else if bar_index - entryBar == scratchN and gainR < scratchR
        sigExit := true
        exitPx := close
        exitWhy := "Scratch: no velocity"
    else if useBarEx and nowR >= barExR and ((pos == 1 and low < exLo) or (pos == -1 and high > exHi))
        sigExit := true
        exitPx := pos == 1 ? exLo - tick : exHi + tick
        exitWhy := "Prior bars broken"
    else if useAdds and adds < maxAdds and bar_index - pauseLen > entryBar and (na(lastAdd) or bar_index - pauseLen > lastAdd) and (pHi - pLo) <= pauseAtr * atr[1]
        bool trig = pos == 1 ? (high > pHi and pHi > entry) : (low < pLo and pLo < entry)
        if trig
            sigAdd  := true
            adds    += 1
            lastAdd := bar_index
            float trail = pos == 1 ? pennyPast(pLo - tick, 1) : pennyPast(pHi + tick, -1)
            stop := pos == 1 ? math.max(stop, trail) : math.min(stop, trail)
    if sigExit
        float r = (exitPx - entry) * pos / risk
        lastMsg := exitWhy + " · first unit " + (r >= 0 ? "+" : "") + str.tostring(r, "#.0") + "R"
        label.new(bar_index, pos == 1 ? high : low, exitWhy + "\n" + str.tostring(exitPx, format.mintick) + " · " + (r >= 0 ? "+" : "") + str.tostring(r, "#.0") + "R",
             style = pos == 1 ? label.style_label_down : label.style_label_up, color = cExit, textcolor = color.black, size = size.small)
        pos := 0

// a new breakout (flat, intraday, once per side per day)
if pos == 0 and intra and not sigExit
    bool upBreak = allowLong and not usedHi and qualHi and close > lvHi and open <= lvHi + atr
    bool dnBreak = allowShort and not usedLo and qualLo and close < lvLo and open >= lvLo - atr
    if upBreak or dnBreak
        int d = upBreak ? 1 : -1
        if d == 1
            usedHi := true
        else
            usedLo := true
        if rvolOK and barOK
            lvl   := d == 1 ? lvHi : lvLo
            entry := d == 1 ? (open > lvl ? open : lvl + tick) : (open < lvl ? open : lvl - tick)
            float raw = stopMode == "Breakout candle" ? (d == 1 ? low - tick : high + tick) : (d == 1 ? math.min(baseLo, low) - tick : math.max(baseHi, high) + tick)
            stop  := pennyPast(raw, d)
            risk  := (entry - stop) * d
            if risk > 0
                pos      := d
                entryBar := bar_index
                lastAdd  := na
                adds     := 0
                best     := d == 1 ? high : low
                bool withMkt = not useMkt or (d == 1 ? mktClose > mktVwap : mktClose < mktVwap)
                string sz = withMkt ? "A size" : "B size"
                sigLong  := d == 1
                sigShort := d == -1

Questions

What makes a level 9 out of 10?

A multi-week or multi-month ceiling on the daily chart that kept turning price away. A 6 or 7 inside a sloppy range is a pass, and a random intraday line isn't a level at all.

How much relative volume does a technical breakout need?

It has to be elevated going in. In the ETHA example full-day relative volume was over two times normal. A monthly ceiling attempted on normal or half volume is a red flag and no trade.

Where does the stop go on a technical catalyst breakout?

Under the breakout candle, and never right on a round number. Give it a penny more, 9, 14 or 24 cents instead of 10, 20 or 25, to stay out of random liquidity sweeps.

When do you exit?

On one of three defined signals: a break of the prior 2-minute bar low, a breakdown of a clean consolidation, or a clear shift on the tape. If it never gets going, scratch it at break-even or a two-cent loss.
