What is the Ready, Aim, Fire setup?

Ready, Aim, Fire is an auction market theory and order flow day trade for NQ and ES futures. Ready: read the higher time frame as trend or balance. Aim: wait for price to reach a level from yesterday's profile, the overnight session or a composite. Fire: enter only when order flow shows one side putting in effort and not getting paid for it. The stop goes past the fight and the target is the next level, at 1:1 or better.

Watch one trade, ready to fire

1

Ready: read the higher time frame

  • Start on the daily (or monthly, weekly, daily for pure price action). Higher highs and higher lows? Lower lows and lower highs? Or sweeping back and forth?
  • Trending: trade with it and be careful against it. Balanced: fade the extremes and be careful with breakouts.
  • Check the news: an FOMC week, or a quiet Monday? Before big news, expect more chop.

Not a hard bias, a lean. Constantly shorting a market that breaks the prior day high day after day is how you struggle.

2

Aim: mark the levels before the open

  • From yesterday's full session profile (session changeover to 4 PM): value area high, point of control, value area low. Plus the prior day high and low.
  • The overnight high and low: changeover to 9:30. Today's session VWAP.
  • Big high volume nodes on a composite profile (a year of daily bars is a fair start), and the big GEX walls as secondary levels.

Static levels from yesterday and the overnight, not the developing profile: easier to read order flow against a level already on the chart.

3

Wait for price to reach a level

  • In a trend: a pullback to a level, then trade it in the trend's direction. In balance: the edges, back toward the other side.
  • Best when several levels stack: the POC, VWAP and a composite node at one price.
  • Between levels, you're in the middle of the move. Don't get involved because the market is moving.

The levels are interchangeable. What matters is that price is somewhere traders have reacted before.

4

Fire: effort without reward, then rejection

  • On a 5-minute chart of volume profile candles with big trades: who is putting in effort, and are they being rewarded?
  • A short: buyers push back over the level with size, volume piles up there, price goes nowhere and comes back under.
  • Fire on the break back through (aggressive) or after the candle closes (conservative). A market order, not a limit.

No trigger, no trade. A level with no execution trigger is buying or selling blind, and that gets you smoked.

5

Stop past the fight, with room

  • Past the high the sellers defended (the low the buyers defended, for a long), not pinned right on it.
  • Leave room for a wick or one more test. A starting point on NQ: about 50 ticks, wider in fast markets, tighter in slow ones.

Back through that level, you're dead wrong: the side you bet on lost the fight.

6

Target the next area of interest

  • The next level in the trade's direction: a prior day high or low, the next high volume node, a GEX wall. In balance, the other side of the range.
  • At least 1:1 to the next or the second level. If you can't find it, no trade.
  • Then leave it: no moving the stop, no jumping to break even, no adding.

At coin-flip odds a 1:1 breaks even. Beating 50% by a little, at a little over 1:1, is the whole edge.

Two playbooks, one model

Ready decides which one today is.

Trend

Higher highs and lows (or lower). Wait for a pullback to a level, then trade with the trend: break and retest, or the break if it's aggressive.

Balance

Ranging, sweeping highs and lows. Fade the edges back toward the other side. A clean break out of balance is the natural loss.

Aggressive

Sell mid-candle as price breaks back under the level after the buyers failed.

Conservative

Wait for the candle to close back under, then sell. Fewer fills, cleaner reads.

When it's off

Any one missing piece means no trade.

Against the regime

Shorting a market that breaks higher day over day, or buying the break of a range that held for a month.

Be cautious, or pass.

No level

A good read in the middle of nowhere. Price isn't anywhere traders have reacted before.

Wait for a level.

No trigger

A slow grind, no big trades, little volume at the level. It may still move without you.

Sit on your hands.

Under 1:1

A big wick forces a wide stop and the next two levels are close.

Valid, but not tradable.

The levels on the chart

Everything is levels based: the market moves from one level to the next, and if it breaks one, it heads for the next. The objective ones are marked the same way every day; the composite nodes are drawn as wide zones by eye.

THE LENS

Balance, imbalance, balance

Auction market theory from top to bottom: price rotates in balance, breaks out to explore, and settles into the next area of balance. High volume nodes are where it settled before.

WHY STATIC

Yesterday's levels, not today's

Watching a developing value area high migrate higher and trading off it all day is harder. Levels already on the chart leave the eyes free for the order flow.

STACKED

More than one at a price

A pullback to yesterday's POC with VWAP and a composite node at the same price is the best kind of location. One level can be enough; none never is.

Plan the trade

Type the entry, the stop past the fight and the next two levels. The defaults are the walkthrough's short. The dollar risk is only an example; MNQ is $2 a point, NQ $20, MES $5, ES $50.

Hold the trade

The hard part isn't the model. It's waiting for all three pieces, then holding the trade to the target or the stop. After a loss on a good entry, the common mistakes are chasing the next one and going on tilt.

Once you're in

  • Don't move the stop, and don't move it to break even.
  • Don't add, don't close early because you're nervous.
  • He sometimes closes early in profit on the order flow, and isn't sure it helps. Beginners do better not touching it.

Choppy weeks: higher win rate, smaller wins. Trending weeks: more stops, bigger winners.

How you trade it

Start to finish, for a session into the New York open.

  1. Ready: daily structure: trending up, down, or balanced? Lean with it. Check the news calendar.
  2. Aim: on the 15-minute, mark yesterday's value area high, POC and value area low, the prior day high and low, the overnight high and low, VWAP, composite high volume nodes and the big GEX walls.
  3. Wait: for price to reach a level, ideally several stacked. In a trend, a pullback to it; in balance, an edge.
  4. Fire: on the 5-minute, watch the big trades and each candle's profile. Effort from one side, no reward, a rejection: market order on the break back through, or after the close.
  5. Stop: past the high (or low) the other side defended, with room for a wick. About 50 ticks on NQ to start.
  6. Target: the next area of interest, at least 1:1, or the second one. Under 1:1 to both, skip it.
  7. Manage: hands off. Hold it to the target or the stop.

Questions

Which levels does Ready, Aim, Fire use?

Yesterday's value area high, point of control and value area low, the prior day high and low, the overnight high and low, today's session VWAP, composite high volume nodes, and big GEX walls as secondary levels.

What is effort without reward?

On a 5-minute chart, one side pushes through a level with size and volume piles up there, but price goes nowhere and comes back. That failed push is the trigger. No trigger, no trade.

Where does the stop go?

Past the high the sellers defended (or the low the buyers defended for a long), with room for a wick or one more test. About 50 ticks on NQ is a starting point, wider in fast markets.

What reward does the trade need?

At least 1:1 to the next or second level. If you can't find it, there's no trade. Once in, don't move the stop, jump to break even or add.

Context, location, trigger. All three, every time.
