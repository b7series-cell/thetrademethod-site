What is the Gamma Wall Footprint?

The Gamma Wall Footprint is a set of three NQ futures day trades at the levels from a GEX (gamma exposure) map: the put wall, the call wall and zero gamma. The wall tells you where, not when. A slow drift into a wall means look for the reaction right at it; a fast push means let it overshoot and enter on the reclaim. The footprint chart confirms the entry with absorption, stacked imbalances, a delta flip or trapped traders. The third setup goes with the move when price breaks below zero gamma and holds.

1

Map: put wall, call wall, zero gamma

  • Add up the gamma exposure of every strike and you get the GEX map. Three lines on it matter.
  • The call wall, the most positive gamma: dealers sell rallies there. A ceiling with springs.
  • The put wall, the most negative gamma: dealers buy dips there. A trampoline floor.
  • Zero gamma is the flip point of the whole map: the border between two kinds of market.

Price doesn't react at random prices. It reacts where the dealers' hedging is biggest, and you can know those prices before the open.

2

Regime: the lake or the rapids

  • Above zero gamma, dealers sell rips and buy dips. Their hedging soaks up moves: a calm lake, you paddle back and forth.
  • Below it, they sell weakness and buy strength. Their hedging adds fuel: the rapids, and the current takes you.
  • Before any trade, ask one question: am I on the lake or in the rapids?

Same-day (0DTE) options make this strongest in the first hours of the US session. One or two good setups, then you're done.

3

Approach: how price arrives

  • Slow approach: small candles, a steady drift. Look for the reaction right at the level.
  • Fast approach: big candles, real momentum. Don't catch the ball while it's stretching the net.
  • Let a fast move overshoot, then enter when price gets back to the level. That's the reclaim, and it proves the breakout failed.

Throw a ball softly at a net and it stops right there. Throw it hard and it stretches way past the line, then snaps back.

4

Confirm: the footprint knocks

  • Absorption: sellers keep hitting it, the red numbers pile up, and price won't go lower.
  • Stacked imbalances: three rows or more where one side is three times bigger. Someone just took control.
  • A delta flip: big negative, then big positive, right at the level (or the reverse at a call wall).
  • Trapped traders: they rushed through the door just before it slammed shut behind them.
  • Bonus, big prints: single large trades right at the level. Size showing up where it should.

GEX shows you where the door is. The footprint tells you if someone is knocking.

5

Risk: stop where the idea is wrong

  • Ask: if this wall is supposed to hold, where would price have to go to prove me wrong? On NQ that's often 30 to 50 ticks.
  • The target is the next level. Small stop, big target, but only if you enter near the level.
  • Same wall, same target: enter close and it's 8.5R. Chase it late and it's 0.5R.

The level gives you a short distance to being wrong. Enter far from it and you've thrown that away.

6

Manage: half off, the rest rides

  • With more than one contract, take some off at the first reaction and let the rest ride to the next level.
  • If a wall breaks and price accepts beyond it, don't stand in front of a broken dam holding a bucket. Reassess.
  • Check the calendar: OPEX, triple witching and VIX expiration move the levels. Check yourself after a loss.

On moving day all the furniture is in the hallway. Your usual levels might not be where you left them.

Three ways to find the levels

Three models, one rule: location first, execution second.

Model 1Gamma walls

The call wall, the put wall, and the second biggest ones behind them. That's your map for the day.

Model 2Open interest

Volume is everyone who walked past the stadium today. Open interest is the crowd still in their seats. Look back about 90 days; the biggest crowds become levels.

Model 3Convexity

Stepping stones across a river. Positive convexity pockets are the stones, and price likes to travel from one to the next.

When to leave it alone

Any one of these and it isn't a trade yet.

One big print

A huge call trade on a flow scanner. It could be a hedge, one leg of a spread, or a bet on volatility.

One trade without context tells you almost nothing.

Just a wall

Price is at the level, but the footprint shows nobody knocking. Or a fast move is still stretching the net.

The wall is where, not when.

A broken dam

The wall broke and price accepted beyond it.

Don't fade it with a bucket. Reassess.

Moving day, or angry

OPEX, triple witching or VIX expiration. Or the trade right after a loss.

Recheck the map, and ask if it's still the plan.

The three setups

The rules: entry, stop and exit

Each setup from start to finish. The put wall and call wall prices are the video's two examples. Neither the video nor his guide gives prices for the third play, so its prices come from our drawing.

Put wall · long

Slow approach, react at the level

Setup
Price drifts down into the put wall in small, steady candles. No momentum.
Entry
Wait for the bar that tags the wall to show absorption: heavy selling at the bid and no new low. Go long on the next bar once it prints stacked buy imbalances (3 or more rows, buyers 3× sellers) and delta flips positive. Enter within a few points of the wall.Video: wall 25,150, delta −3,076 then +3,460, long 25,156.
Stop
Just beyond the failed reaction: under the low the sellers couldn't break. That's a price, not a GEX level. Usually 30 to 50 ticks on NQ.Video: about 44 ticks (11 points), 25,145.
Exit
Half off at the first reaction. The rest at the next level up, zero gamma.Video: half at 25,200 (+44), the rest at 25,250 (+94, about 8.5R).
No trade if
Price slices through the wall with no absorption, or it has already bounced far enough that the stop is bigger than the room left to the target. Out if the wall moves lower during the day or price makes a decisive close below it.

Call wall · short

Fast approach, wait for the overshoot

Setup
Price rips up into the call wall in big, fast candles. Don't short the first touch: let it overshoot.
Entry
Short when price gets back below the wall and the footprint shows the buyers trapped: heavy buying above the wall that went nowhere, then sellers stacking the bid and delta flipping hard negative.Video: wall 25,450, 6,000+ bought above it (delta +5,560), then −1,340 and −5,030. Short 25,447.
Stop
Just beyond the failed reaction: above the overshoot high. That's a price, not a GEX level.Video and guide: overshoot high 25,458, stop 25,459, 48 ticks (12 points) above the entry.
Exit
Half off at the first reaction. The rest at the next level down, zero gamma.Video: half at 25,380 (+67), the rest at 25,320 (+127, about 10R).
No trade if
Price holds above the wall and accepts there, or the wall moves higher. That's a broken dam: stop shorting it and reassess, because the #2 call wall may now be the level that matters.

Zero gamma · short

The regime breaks, go with it

Setup
Price drifts down toward zero gamma, often late morning. The net GEX is shrinking and gamma is building at lower strikes.
Entry
Short only after price breaks below zero gamma and holds there, with the ranges getting bigger. Go with the momentum; don't fade anything.Our drawing: zero gamma 25,300, short 25,288 after a retest holds under it.
Stop
Back above zero gamma, wider than the wall trades. You're wrong if price reclaims zero gamma and holds above it on the retest.Our drawing: 25,318 (30 points).
Exit
The put wall below is the target.Our drawing: 25,180 (+108).
No trade if
Price only pokes under zero gamma and snaps back. No hold, no rapids. Out if it reclaims zero gamma and holds above.

Read the footprint

A footprint splits every candle into price rows: contracts sold aggressively at the bid on the left, bought aggressively at the ask on the right. Ask minus bid is delta. Pick a sign to see it.

A gamma wall alone is information. A big futures trade alone is information. Price action alone is information. Stack them at the same price and you have a trade.The video's closing point, in our words.

How you trade it

Start to finish, for one morning.

  1. Before the session: is it OPEX, triple witching or VIX expiration? Levels mapped and marked? Above or below zero gamma? Max risk per trade and per day written down, a limit of one or two A+ setups, and calm enough to trade.
  2. Map: before the open, mark the put wall, the call wall and zero gamma (and the second biggest walls). Open interest and convexity pockets can add levels.
  3. Regime: ask if you're on the lake (above zero gamma, moves get absorbed) or in the rapids (below it, moves stretch). If price breaks below zero gamma and holds, switch to the third play and go with it.
  4. Approach: watch how price arrives at the level. Slow drift: look for the reaction at the level. Fast push: let it overshoot and wait for the reclaim.
  5. Confirm: on the footprint, at least one of absorption, stacked imbalances, a delta flip or trapped traders (big prints add confidence), right at the level.
  6. Risk: stop where the wall idea is proven wrong (often 30 to 50 ticks on NQ), target the next level. Only take it if you're close to the level.
  7. Manage: half off at the first reaction, the rest to the next level. A wall that breaks and holds is a reason to reassess, not to add.
  8. Stop: one or two quality setups in the first hours, then done. Recheck the map on expiration days, and check yourself after a loss.

Questions

What are the call wall, put wall and zero gamma?

The call wall is the strike with the most positive gamma, where dealers tend to sell rallies. The put wall is the strike with the most negative gamma, where dealers tend to buy dips. Zero gamma is the border: above it dealer hedging absorbs moves, below it the hedging adds fuel and moves stretch.

Why not just buy or sell at the wall?

Because the wall tells you where, not when. Price can run straight through it, especially on a fast approach. You wait for the footprint to show someone defending the level, or for a fast move to overshoot and come back (the reclaim).

Where does the stop go?

Where the wall idea is proven wrong: past the failed low or the overshoot high, often 30 to 50 ticks on NQ. The target is the next level. Entering close to the level is what makes the reward big: in the video's first example, entering at the wall was about 8.5R to the next level; the video shows the same trade chased late at 0.5R.

When should you not trade the levels?

On a single big options print without context, when a wall has broken and price accepted beyond it, on OPEX, triple witching and VIX expiration days when the levels move, and right after a loss if the next trade isn't from the plan.

Does the gamma wall footprint work?

Not in our test. On a year of NQ with real gamma walls and real footprints, the full entry recipe gave only one wall trade. With any one footprint sign as the trigger, 70 trades won 17% of the time and lost 0.40R a trade on average, worse than random entries with the same stops and targets (−0.16R) and worse than the same rules on fake levels.
