What is a look above and fail?

A look above and fail is a value area fade for balanced days on NQ and ES. Let the day's volume profile build for 30 minutes, mark the value area high and low, and wait. When aggressive buyers push over the high (or sellers under the low) and price comes right back inside, they're trapped. You trade the reclaim to the point of control, then the other side of value.

1

Mark the extreme points

  • A developing volume profile of New York's regular hours, 9:30 to 4:00, where the bulk of NQ and ES volume trades.
  • Give it 30 minutes. Then mark the value area high and low (70% of the volume) and the point of control.
  • Before 10:00, use the previous day's regular-hours profile instead.

Little volume has traded past the value area edges. That's where buyers who get aggressive either get paid or quickly get trapped.

2

Wait for price to test one

  • Inside value is noise. Trading the middle gets you chopped up and burns mental capital.
  • Sit on the sideline until price trades at the value area high or low.
  • A setup taken outside your system that loses puts you in a make-it-back mindset.

The hardest step, and a psychological one: you've defined the locations, now just wait.

3

See who gets aggressive past it

  • Under the value area low, sellers push: big trades at the bid, a sell-side imbalance on the delta profile. Over the high, the mirror.
  • He uses big trades and the delta profile. The footprint or time and sales work too.
  • A print at the bid isn't proof of a new seller. You never know intent: read the orders and the reaction together.

Market orders consume limit orders. Whoever is aggressive creates the momentum you trade.

4

Trapped: enter on the way back in

  • If the breakdown were real, price would never return to value. Back inside means those sellers are trapped.
  • Aggressive: a market order as it reclaims the edge. Don't wait for the candle to close.
  • Conservative: let it reclaim, then buy a retest that holds as a higher low while big bids get absorbed.

Their stops are buy orders. Covering adds fuel the other way, which is why good ones go fast.

5

Stop past the big trades

  • Just past the big prints that made the move: if those traders get back to profit, the idea is likely wrong.
  • If the poke ran too far for the risk-reward, risk about 50% of it instead of the whole swing.
  • Plan it before the trade: target, value area range, stop, and at least 2R.

A tighter stop than the swing high saves points on the losers and gets better R on the winners.

6

POC, then the other side

  • The point of control, about 1 to 1.4R, is the first place to take some off and move the stop to break even.
  • The final target is the other side of balance: the opposite value area edge.
  • Past it, trail the stop as it makes new highs (lows). On choppy days, take it all at the other side.

Beginners: make the POC partial a rule, not an option, until your own data says otherwise.

How long it takes

On 2-minute bars, which filter some of the one-minute noise. It's his preference.

Morning

The New York morning has the momentum. A clean one usually works in about five minutes.

Lunch and afternoon

Slower and smaller prints. The setup still counts, but he might hold 30 minutes or more and trail it.

NQ vs ES

ES is naturally slower: 500 stocks against 100.

Win rate

About 65% this year at 2R, he says. It moves with the market environment.

When it's off

It's made for balanced markets.

A trending day

Thin volume pockets on the profile instead of thick clusters building in a range. Value keeps moving.

Not a balance: no trade.

News coming

Within 5 to 10 minutes of a scheduled release, liquidity gets pulled off the book and you get weird spikes.

Even if it looks perfect: stay out.

Three times wrong

Stopped out three times in the same range (two, when it's volatile). A stop alone isn't a reason to skip the next valid one.

The market says you're wrong today. Done with that range.

In the middle

Anywhere between the value area edges, and anything before 10:00 off today's still-forming profile.

Not a location: sit on your hands.

The first 30 minutes

He uses a developing profile because he scalps and wants information as close to real time as possible. But a fresh profile's edges shift a lot early on: it's chaos, the flea market. Drag through the same session and watch them settle.

If you trade before 10:00

  • Use the previous day's regular-hours profile: its value area high and low are the extreme points.
  • Note where New York opens against it. Inside yesterday's value is range compression, like an inside day: expect chop.
  • Then watch how price reacts at yesterday's edges, with the same steps three to six.

New to this? Sit out the first 30 minutes

That's his advice for anyone starting out. The walkthrough's day had a false breakout and a false breakdown before 10:00. They could have been trades, but the framework wasn't there yet.

No special anchor: a plain developing RTH profile.

Aggressive or conservative?

Same location, same push under the value area low. The difference is velocity: how hard it went out and how fast it came back. There's no indicator for it. You learn it from screen time.

AGGRESSIVE

Fast flip: market order on the reclaim

A strong push out, then right back in. Enter as it crosses back over the edge. Waiting for the candle to close can cost you the whole risk-reward. He takes this one far more often.

CONSERVATIVE

Slow creep: wait for the retest to hold

It reclaims, comes back to the edge, and holds as a higher low while more sell orders hit the bid and price won't go down. That's absorption: a passive buyer sitting there.

THE TRADE-OFF

Speed costs you a false start or two

The aggressive entry can be a retest rejection that rolls over. The conservative one waits for proof and can miss the fast ones. He often starts aggressive with part of the size and adds when the edge holds.

In his engulfing analogy for beginners: the down candle had huge sell orders. If it were a true breakdown, it would never have been engulfed back into value.

Plan the trade before you take it

Target, value area range, stop, at least 2R. Type the levels; the defaults are the walkthrough's short. The stop buffer, the dollar risk and the 1.4R line are only examples.

Size for the volatility

You can't size the same in every market. Use the range (ATR) to set the stop, then the stop to set the size.

15–17

NQ points of stop on a normal day at all-time highs, for 30, 40, 50-point targets.

30+

Points of stop when one-minute candles run 100 points. Bigger targets too, so 2 NQ becomes micros.

VIX 18–20

A soft rule: above it, trade micros in a prop account and expect bigger swings.

At least 2R. Below that, he doesn't think it lasts.

Starting out, don't force 2R: throw on a few micros, target POC (about 1 to 1.4R), go to break even, and collect your own data. Then aim for the other side.

Accumulation, distribution and the trap

Balance is buyers and sellers agreeing on a fair price for an hour or two (months, in a stock). Then one side wins. Up out of it is accumulation, like a bull flag; down out of it is distribution. The look above and fail bets that the side that just tried, lost.

01

Aggressive orders go out

Sellers hit the bid under the value area low. Big trades show it; the delta profile shows it, including orders split into small pieces or iceberg-style limits that never trip a big-trade filter.

02

Price can't stay there

A true breakdown wouldn't come back to the point of balance. Back inside, buyers couldn't (or sellers couldn't) sustain value beyond the edge.

03

Their stops become fuel

A short's stop is a buy. Forced covering adds momentum toward the other side, like a fund covering a short squeeze, only in miniature.

How you trade it

The whole setup, in order.

  1. Check the day: balance, not trend (thick volume clusters, not thin pockets). No scheduled news in the next 5 to 10 minutes.
  2. Let the profile develop for 30 minutes on a regular-hours developing profile. Before then, use yesterday's.
  3. Mark the value area high, low and POC. Only the edges are trades. Wait for price to get there.
  4. Watch who gets aggressive past the edge: big trades, a delta imbalance. Then whether they get trapped.
  5. Enter on the way back in: a market order on a fast reclaim, or the retest that holds on a slow one.
  6. Stop just past the big trades (or half the poke). Plan for at least 2R to the other side, or pass.
  7. Take some at POC, move to break even, and target the other side of value. Trail past it on a trend; take it all on a choppy day.
  8. Three stops in the same range: done with it. Wait for a new range.

The indicator

One TradingView script for the setup (Pine Script v6), for intraday charts. It builds the developing regular-hours profile, draws the value area high, low and POC, and marks the look above and fail (and look below and fail) with the stop and both targets. It marks and alerts. It does not place trades.

  • Look Above and Fail

    alerts

    The profile is built from the chart's own bars from 9:30 New York time: each bar's volume is spread evenly over its range in rows, and the value area is the usual 70% grown out from the POC. Before 10:00 it uses yesterday's finished value area. A look above counts when a bar trades a few ticks over the value area high with an aggression check (signed volume from lower time frame bars, or a volume spike). The fail is the first trade back under it within a few bars: that's the aggressive entry, marked at the edge. The conservative entry is a later retest of the edge that closes back inside with a lower high. Stop past the extreme (or half the poke), partial at POC, target the other side. It skips trades under 2R to the other side, after three stops in a day, near the news times you type in, and when the POC has run more than half the value area in the last 30 minutes.

    Pine can't see big trades or a true delta profile, so the aggression check is a stand-in: judge the prints yourself. A profile from bars is coarser than your platform's tick profile; the levels can differ by a row or two. Row size, the poke, the windows and every filter are settings. The thresholds are ours, not the video's.

//
// A balance-day scalp at the value area edges (look above and fail /
// look below and fail), for intraday futures charts (NQ, ES; 1 to 5
// minutes, 2-minute in the video):
//
//   1. Location    A developing volume profile of New York's regular
//                  hours (9:30-16:00). The value area high and low (70%
//                  of the volume) are the extreme points. For the first
//                  30 minutes the previous day's value area is used.
//   2. Wait        Nothing happens in the middle.
//   3. Aggression  Price trades past an edge with an aggression check:
//                  positive (negative) signed volume of lower time
//                  frame bars, or a volume spike. Pine can't see big
//                  trades or a delta profile, so this is a stand-in.
//   4. The fail    The first trade back inside within N bars is the
//                  aggressive entry, marked at the edge. A later retest
//                  of the edge that closes back inside without a new
//                  extreme is the conservative entry.
//   5. The stop    Past the extreme of the poke, or half the poke.
//   6. Targets     POC (partial, then break even), then the other side
//                  of value. Trades under the minimum R to the other
//                  side are skipped.
//
//   Filters: start after the first 30 minutes, a last-entry time, a
//   daily stop count (three strikes), news blackout times, and a trend
//   check (POC migration).
//
//   The profile is built from the chart's bars: each bar's volume is
//   spread evenly over its range in rows. That's coarser than a tick
//   profile, so levels can differ from your platform's by a row or two.
//   It marks and alerts. It does not place trades.
// =====================================================================

    // POC and value area
    keys = prof.keys()
    float tot = 0.0
    int pk = na
    float pv = -1.0
    int kmin = na
    int kmax = na
    for k in keys
        v = prof.get(k)
        tot += v
        if v > pv
            pv := v
            pk := k
        kmin := na(kmin) ? k : math.min(kmin, k)
        kmax := na(kmax) ? k : math.max(kmax, k)
    int a = pk
    int b = pk
    float acc = pv
    while acc < vaPct / 100 * tot and (a > kmin or b < kmax)
        float up = b < kmax ? nz(prof.get(b + 1)) : -1.0
        float dn = a > kmin ? nz(prof.get(a - 1)) : -1.0
        if up >= dn
            b += 1
            acc += math.max(up, 0)
        else
            a -= 1
            acc += math.max(dn, 0)
    poc := pk * rowSize + rowSize / 2
    vah := (b + 1) * rowSize
    val := a * rowSize

float closedR = na
if tDir != 0
    bool hitStop = tDir == 1 ? low <= tStop : high >= tStop
    bool hitPoc = not tHalf and (tDir == 1 ? high >= tPoc : low <= tPoc)
    bool hitOther = tDir == 1 ? high >= tOther : low <= tOther
    float rPoc = math.abs(tPoc - tEntry) / tRisk
    float rOther = math.abs(tOther - tEntry) / tRisk
    float part = partPct / 100
    if hitStop
        float rStop = (tStop - tEntry) * tDir / tRisk
        closedR := tHalf ? part * rPoc + (1 - part) * rStop : rStop

planTrade(int dir, float entry, float ext, float edge) =>
    // dir = -1 short (stop above), +1 long (stop below)
    float half = edge + (ext - edge) / 2
    float base = stopMode == "Past the extreme" ? ext : half
    float stop = dir == -1 ? base + bufTk * tick : base - bufTk * tick
    float risk = math.abs(entry - stop)
    float other = dir == -1 ? eVal : eVah
    float rr = risk > 0 ? (other - entry) * dir / risk : 0.0
    [risk > 0 and rr >= minR, stop, risk, other, rr]

Questions

Why wait 30 minutes?

A fresh profile's edges shift a lot early on. Before 10:00, use the previous day's regular-hours value area high and low instead.

Where does the stop go?

Just past the big trades that made the push. If those traders get back to profit, the idea is likely wrong. Every trade is planned for at least 2R.

When should you not trade it?

On a trending day, within 5 to 10 minutes of scheduled news, after three stop-outs in the same range, or anywhere between the value area edges.

Does the look above and fail work?

Not in our test. On 5-minute bars it looked profitable, but fake value area edges moved by a quarter of the range made as much. On 1-minute bars it lost on SPY and QQQ (-0.07R).
