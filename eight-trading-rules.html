Eight Trading Rules

You used to be profitable and now you're in a slump. What do you do? Mike Bellafiore of SMB Capital was asked that by one of the firm's own traders, and his answer was eight rules that all do the same thing: make the game easier.

Make the game easier. Struggling is normal. Not changing isn't.

Jeff Holden, SMB's head of trader development, asked Mike Bellafiore to join a call with a firm trader who had been profitable and was now in a slump. They listened, asked questions, and found what was causing the underperformance. Then Mike gave the eight rules on this page. The firm's message to struggling traders is two-sided, and both sides matter.

0

What a slump is, and isn't

  • It's inevitable. There's nothing wrong with you. Every trader goes through it, and will go through it again. It feels terrible at the time.
  • It isn't okay to stay the same. SMB doesn't just tell a struggling trader everything is fine. It won't be, unless they change.
  • The fix is a plan. Solutions with effective best practices, and a plan to execute them.

Nobody is owed a profitable month. The video plays Nick Saban's "you're entitled to nothing" speech to make the point: talent without discipline, execution and focus gets you nothing.

Two things can be true

okayStruggling

It's part of the process for everyone, at every level, including the firm's best traders.

not okayStruggling and not changing

Underperforming and doing the same things tomorrow. That's the part you control.

"Nothing is acceptable but your best." — Nick Saban, played in the video

How Shark trades out of a slump

Before the rules, the video hands over to Shark, one of SMB's most successful traders over the last ten years. He's had to deal with large losses and slumps many times, and says it's also what builds a trader's resilience. Here's his routine, on a made-up month.

The slump, and the way out

S

Shark's routine

  • When in doubt, get out. If you're frozen in a position, get out and reassess. You can always get back in.
  • Step away. A one-minute walk around the office or outside. Not being attached to the trade makes for a much better decision.
  • Take a personal inventory. Keep a journal and ask why it's happening. A good setup you sized up on that didn't work? Or you're not seeing things well, or the market doesn't suit your style?
  • Trade smaller. The urge is to trade bigger and make it back. He admits it can still take him two or three large losses before he trades smaller.
  • Book a green day. Then a couple more. Things turn around faster than you'd think, and then a setup comes along that puts the account back in the green.

Social media makes it look like everyone makes money every day. It isn't the truth. Everyone goes through tough times.

The trading coach's version

The video also quotes Dr. Brett Steenbarger: slumps are common even for seasoned professionals. His three steps line up with Shark's.

1. Diagnose it early

Notice the slump before it becomes serious. The journal is how you notice.

2. Reduce risk exposure

Cut size before the losses get big, not after.

3. Put corrective measures in place

A specific change, not just waiting for it to pass.

First, rule out the market

At SMB, when a trader slips into a period of underperformance, the first conversation is often about adjusting to a new market. That wasn't the problem here, and it's worth checking before you change anything else.

when it is the market2022: the Fed raises rates

Buying pullbacks didn't work as well as it had in 2020, 2021 and early 2022. The fix there is adjusting to the new market, like getting on board the AI trend.

when it isn'tThis trader

Not the market and not the strategy. After listening and asking questions, it came down to entries and sizing.

?

Find the real issue

  • Did the market change, so your setup works less often than it used to?
  • Is the strategy itself broken?
  • Or is it you: late entries, too much size, the wrong trades, a tired head?

Changing strategies when the problem is your entries throws away the part that was working. Name the issue first.

Rule 1: lower your size and risk

The first rule, and the one that makes the other seven possible. Same trades, smaller size: the mistakes cost less while you fix them.

Same trades, half the hole

1

Size down until it's fixed

  • Cut the size per trade and the total you're willing to lose in a day.
  • Keep it there until the issue you found is fixed, not until you've made the money back.
  • Go back up in steps, after a run of green days.

This trader's issues were entries and sizing. Lower size is half of the fix by itself.

The trap it stops

the urgeSize up to get it back

Natural, and the fastest way to turn a slump into a hole. Shark says even he still does it sometimes.

the ruleSize down, book green

Smaller losses, a calmer head, and green days that rebuild confidence.

Rule 2: be more selective

Focus on the trades that fall into your lap: the obvious ones you don't have to talk yourself into.

Forced trades vs trades that fall into your lap

2

Only the obvious ones

  • If you have to argue yourself into it, it isn't one.
  • Your best setup, in the right conditions, at a clear level. Everything else waits until you're back.
  • Fewer trades also means fewer decisions under pressure, which helps rule 7.

Studying your past trades usually shows the same thing: the lesser trades cost you, and the really good ones carry you.

Be more selective: focus on trades that fall into your lap. — rule 2, as Mike gives it in the video

Rule 3: shorten your time frame

The longer your time frame, the more intervening factors and randomness can come in. For most active traders, and for this one, a shorter time frame generally leads to more consistent results.

The longer the hold, the wider the outcomes

3

Less time, less noise

  • Your setup's edge mostly shows up early, right after the entry.
  • Every extra hour lets in things your setup didn't plan for: a headline, the market turning, chop, a sector move.
  • While you're rebuilding, trade the part of the move your edge explains, and let the rest go.

This is for active, skilled traders working their way back. A swing trader with a working process doesn't need to become a scalper.

What "shorter" can mean

  • Hold minutes instead of hours.
  • Take the first target, not the runner, for a while.
  • Trade the morning session and stop.
  • Drop a lower time frame for the entry if you've been entering off the higher one.

Rule 4: don't do this alone

Tell a teammate. Ask them to help you be accountable, and share the experience with people who understand it.

4

Only traders understand

  • Nobody understands what it feels like to be a losing trader, an underperforming trader, a trader in drawdown, the way other traders do.
  • Who else works really hard all day and goes home having lost money? Just traders.
  • Support from a teammate helps you feel better, and you feel like you belong with others. Leaning on someone, feeling their empathy and just sharing how you feel helps you get through a period.

At SMB, Jeff Holden, Mike, team leaders and senior traders all mentor. Outside a firm, find one or two traders you trust.

accountability

Someone checks

A teammate who knows your rules and asks if you followed them.

support

Someone gets it

Sharing how you feel takes the weight off a red week.

Rule 5: one good trade

The mantra you want to be whispering to yourself. The self-talk you want in your head.

"One good trade, one good trade, one good trade."
Just make a good trade.
5

Shrink the goal to one trade

  • Not "make back the week". Not even "have a green day". Make the next trade a good one.
  • A good trade is about the process: the right setup, the right entry, the right size. Not whether it made money.
  • String enough of them together and the green days take care of themselves.

It also answers Mark Douglas's advice from the list above: focus on your process.

Rule 6: a daily report card on good entries

Once they found the issue, they targeted it. This trader's entries were sloppy: getting in a little late, chasing. So the work was one daily report card on one thing, done intensively, until it got better.

On time, a bit late, chased

6

One issue, every day

  • Pick the one thing that's hurting you most. Here, entries.
  • Grade it on every trade, every day. Review the trades and check you got in at the right times.
  • Get that one thing better before you move on to anything else.

Jeff Holden said he'd follow up with a video on exactly how SMB's desk does a daily report card, with a template many of their traders use.

Try it: today's report card

Grade each entry you took today. On time scores 2, a bit late 1, chased 0.

Rule 7: keep track of your heart rate, and keep it low

This trader was feeling some anxiety, wasn't seeing the screens well, and was having judgment issues. Dr. Brett Steenbarger has written about using technology to measure your heart rate in real time. When it's low, you make good decisions. When it's up, you don't.

Your heart rate and your decisions

7

Measure it in real time

  • With fast data going by, you have to keep your heart rate down to make good decisions.
  • Wear a device that shows it live, and know your calm range.
  • Above it? Treat it like a stop: no new trades until you're back down. Walk, breathe, then look again.

They suggested it to the trader, who was excited to use it in real time. Mike gives Dr. Steenbarger the credit for the idea.

A simple heart rate rule

in rangeTrade your plan

Seeing the screen well. Take the A trades.

over rangeHands off

No new entries, no moving stops. Step away until it comes down.

Your calm range is yours. Measure it on a quiet morning first. The numbers in the walkthrough are made up.

Rule 8: put your solution together and send it

The last rule: they challenged the trader to write up the solution, with effective best practices, and send it to Jeff and to Mike.

8

From talk to a plan

  • Writing it down forces you to get all your thoughts on paper.
  • You commit to the best practices and a plan to execute them, where others can see it.
  • A lot of the time people just talk about solutions, or just listen. This is the step that actually puts the best practices into motion.

Send it to a mentor or a teammate from rule 4. Their seeing it is what makes it real.

A plan you could send tonight

An example built from the eight rules. Change it to fit your own issue.

The issue: sloppy entries (late, chasing).
1. Half size until 5 green days in a row.
2. Only my A setup. No trade I have to argue for.
3. Hold for the first target only.
4. Check in with ___ at 4:00 every day.
5. One good trade at a time.
6. Report card on entries after every session.
7. Heart rate over my calm range = no new trades.
Review this plan in two weeks.

The way back, step by step

How the eight rules, Shark's routine and the famous traders' advice fit together when you're in a slump. Click through them.

1

Stop the bleeding

  • Frozen in a position? Get out. You can always get back in.
  • Take a break, even a one-minute walk, and reassess away from the screen.

Shark's "when in doubt, get out", and Paul Tudor Jones's step back and cut your losses.

2

Find the real issue

  • Journal it. Is it the market, the strategy, your entries, your size, your head?
  • Be brutally honest. Ask someone who'll tell you.

For the trader in the video: not the market, not the strategy. Entries and sizing.

3

Make the game easier

  • Lower your size and risk (rule 1).
  • Only the trades that fall into your lap (rule 2).
  • A shorter time frame (rule 3).

Smaller, fewer, shorter. Each one takes something hard out of the day.

4

Work on one thing

  • One good trade at a time (rule 5).
  • A daily report card on the one issue (rule 6).
  • Watch your heart rate and keep it in range (rule 7).

Get that one thing better before moving on to anything else.

5

Write it, send it, book green

  • Write the plan and send it to a mentor or teammate (rules 4 and 8).
  • Book a green day, then a couple more. Raise size in steps after that.

A plan other people can see, executed one good trade at a time.

Questions

Is a trading slump normal?

Yes. Every trader goes through one. What isn't okay is struggling and not changing anything.

Why lower size in a slump?

Smaller size and risk take pressure off, so you can get back to executing what used to work without each loss doing more damage.

What is the one good trade rule?

Stop trying to make it all back today. Aim for one good trade, executed well.

Why write the plan down?

Talking about fixes isn't the same as making them. Writing the solution down and sending it to two people makes it a commitment someone can hold you to.

Make the game easier.

A slump isn't a sign that something is wrong with you. Staying in it without changing anything is the only real mistake. Find the one issue, make the game smaller while you fix it, write the plan down, and let someone else see it.
