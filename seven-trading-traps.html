"It's just not my market"

Mike Bellafiore calls it the single most dangerous excuse he hears from experienced traders in a slump. It sounds reasonable. "I'm a breakout trader and breakouts aren't working." "I just need to wait until the market gets better for me."

?

Put the excuse to the test

  • Your team's $40 million quarterback has a long, documented history of folding against one defensive scheme, and did nothing in the off season to fix it.
  • The Super Bowl comes, he sees that exact defense, throws four picks and loses by 50.
  • Then he tells the press conference: "We came up against one of those defenses I'm just historically not very good at."

No fan would accept that. So why is it an acceptable answer from a professional trader, and why is it acceptable to you?

Swap the excuse for a search

The moment you start losing more than usual, don't panic and don't wonder what hit you.

the excuse"It's not my market"

Wait it out, change nothing, and hope your setups come back.

the search"Which trap am I in?"

Go through the historical reasons experienced traders lose money and scrutinize your own trading against each one.

Every desk goes through it

SMB's own traders were underperforming when the firm held this reset meeting: senior partners, principals, top producers and traders, with one stake in the ground. Trading improves starting right after this meeting. Scrub the trading, size down a little, put some points on the board.

Trap 1 · Style drift

You abandon your proven setups and take trades outside your core playbook. A principal called out a textbook case from one of the firm's stellar teams, a group that's flat-out outstanding at short-term momentum.

Same stocks, a different game

1

What happened on the desk

  • Short-term momentum was the team's bread and butter.
  • Then they started buying calls much further out in time and expanding their holding periods.
  • They bled out option premium on longer-term trades that never materialized.

They stepped outside an impressive, proven playbook into a time horizon where they had zero statistical edge.

Ask it of every trade this month

Is this a setup in my playbook?yes / no
Same time frame and holding period I have stats on?yes / no
Same instrument I built the edge with?yes / no
Any "no": it's a new setup, so test it smalldrift

Trap 2 · Missing a regime change

You keep running strategies built for market conditions that are no longer there. Trading coach Dr. Brett Steenberger reviewed a top portfolio manager at a major institution who'd been flat to down for six straight months. His diagnosis: the process was fine, but there was no system to adapt from trending momentum to mean-reverting chop.

Three checks before you size

1

Brandon's regime filter for QQQ

  • Is QQQ within a defined percentage of its all-time high?
  • Is it above the 20-day moving average?
  • Is it above the 50-day moving average?

If all three aren't met, he turns defensive. That filter kept him green while others drew down.

2

What "defensive" means

  • Cut risk on upside momentum trades.
  • Shift to a scalp-minded, move-to-move posture: take what the tape gives and don't hold for the trend.

Two regimes, two playbooks

all three greenTrending momentum

Breakouts follow through. Hold for the move and press the A+ trades.

any one redMean-reverting chop

Breakouts fail. Smaller size, quicker exits, scalp move to move.

Pick the percentage before you need it

The video doesn't give Brandon's exact percentage. The walkthrough uses 5% as a made-up example. Whatever number you choose, write it down while things are calm and don't move it in the middle of a drawdown.

Trap 3 · Holding out for grand slams

You get into good trades at good prices, you're well in the money, and you refuse to take profits because you haven't made enough yet.

The round trip vs a give-back rule

1

Ring the register

  • A firm partner brought up CCXI, a name where traders failed to take open profits and let a massive winner round trip into a red trade on the month.
  • His fix was simple: more traders need hard give-back rules, so a great trade never turns into a loser.
  • When you get a windfall gain, lock it in.

The rule in the walkthrough (past 3R, give back no more than half) is one example. The point is that it's written down before the trade.

A give-back rule, written out

Entry 24.00, stop 23.00, 1,000 shares1R = $1,000
Rule switches on at 3R open27.00
Best close31.50 · +7.5R
Stop = entry + half of the best gain27.75
Locked in, instead of −$800+$3,750

Not the same as letting winners run

You still hold for the bigger move. A give-back rule only decides how much of an open windfall you're willing to hand back. For trailing styles, see Trailing Stop Exits and Let Winners Run.

Trap 4 · Not selective enough

You force B-minus and C-minus trades when the market demands perfection, or you refuse to fold when a promising setup fails to confirm. To stop the bleeding you need ruthless selectivity. Two SMB leaders gave the desk a rule each.

Grade it harsh, fold it early

1

Nano's rule: grade it down three notches

  • In this kind of tape, if a relative strength or weakness setup is missing even one nuance, don't mark it down one notch. Mark it down three.
  • He also named the hidden tax of starting the day in a hole: clawing back $100k of overnight losses drains the mental capital you need to push size on the high-conviction intraday trades.
2

Lance's poker rule: fold the hand

  • Unlike poker, there are no blinds forcing you to play a hand.
  • If a setup shows positive signs at entry but the flop comes up suboptimal, just fold.
  • If your variables aren't present, dump the trade.

A grade ladder for a tough tape

How the walkthrough applies Nano's rule. Where you draw the cutoff is your call; the habit is grading harder when the regime is against you.

All five pieces thereA+ · trade
One missing, normal tape (−1 notch)A · trade
One missing, tough tape (−3 notches)B+ · pass
Good entry, bad flopfold at −0.3R

Grading feeds sizing

A downgrade isn't only a pass or a take. It changes how much you risk. See Dynamic Risk Sizing for A+/A/B/C risk tiers.

Trap 5 · Not enough easy money trades

Bellafiore calls this the single most important lesson in the video. At the reset meeting Graham, a mid-seven-figure producer, called out the developing traders: too many of them were walking the floor complaining the market wasn't any good because overnight momentum had dried up.

Waiting vs slicing out cash flow

1

Graham's challenge

  • While others said there was nothing to do, Graham was quietly slicing out $50,000 to $75,000 a day in intraday scalps.
  • It kept the cash flowing and his mind razor sharp while he waited for the larger opportunities.
  • His message: you need to be adding those trades to your playbook.

Instead of carping and complaining that there aren't enough opportunities, expand your playbook.

Trap 6 · Sizing poorly

You fail to methodically scale risk up when a strategy is hot and down when it goes cold. Or worse, you bet biggest when your edge is lowest.

Cycle the size with the setup

1

Cut size when you're cold

  • Senior trader Kenny: the biggest issue is an inability or unwillingness to cut size when you get cold.
  • It's a delicate dance, because you never want to strip away a trader's aggressiveness.
  • Graham, on the operational side: strategy performance is cyclical. When a setup works, methodically size up. When it underperforms, methodically size down.

Oversizing on a cold strategy in an unfavorable tape will crater your account every time.

What struggling traders do instead

They haven't seen their favorite setup in weeks. The moment something vaguely resembles it, they deploy monster A+ size out of desperation. That's the biggest bet placed exactly where the edge is lowest.

A sizing rule you can follow

Setup printingsize up, step by step
Setup hits a drawdowncut 50%+ at once
Back to full sizeafter it works again

Linda Raschke draws the same line for discretionary traders, and the opposite one for mechanical systems: her drawdown rule.

Trap 7 · Overusing options

Options become a default lottery ticket instead of a tactical weapon or a better way to express a trade. Desk partner Tommy shared the firm's internal data at the reset: traders were heavily positive trading the underlying stocks, and down significantly over the same stretch trading options.

The stop you'd never skip on stock

1

Why options lose when stock wins

  • Capped downside gives the illusion of safety. Traders risk far more capital because they know the absolute loss is defined.
  • In a choppy tape with no follow-through, option market makers will paper-cut you to death with time decay, every single trade.
  • Traders who ruthlessly honor a stop on stock freeze on an option: "I paid a dollar and it's only worth 30 cents, so I'll just let it ride."

That's not risk management. That's paralysis.

Same idea, two structures

Stock: 1,000 shares, stop 49.50−$500
Calls: honor the same stock level−$1,350
Calls: "let it ride" to expiry week−$2,850

Use options on purpose

Options aren't the problem. A defined structure chosen for a reason can be a better way to express a trade. See Covered Calls and the Expected Move dashboard.

The five-point trading reset

Whether you trade on SMB's floor or manage your own book at home, this is the blueprint from their desk reset. Click through the five steps.

1

Audit your regime before sizing

  • Set objective market parameters, like Brandon's QQQ filter: near the high, above the 20-day, above the 50-day.
  • If the tape isn't confirming, stop forcing aggressive momentum into a choppy market.

Fixes trap 2, and keeps trap 1 in check.

2

Cycle your size methodically

  • Strategy performance is cyclical. When a setup is printing, size up.
  • When it hits a drawdown, immediately cut size by 50% or more.

Fixes trap 6. Never bring A+ size to a cold setup.

3

Audit your options exposure

  • Stop letting capped-risk premiums blind you to poor trade structure.
  • If you wouldn't hold the underlying stock through that level, don't hold the option.

Fixes trap 7.

4

Demand ruthless selectivity

  • Lance's poker rule: fold when the flop doesn't fit.
  • Nano's rule: if a setup is missing even one nuance in a choppy regime, downgrade it three grades and pass.

Fixes trap 4, and a give-back rule handles trap 3.

5

Accept Graham's challenge

  • Stop complaining that overnight momentum, or your favorite strategy, has dried up.
  • Slice out daily cash flow in turn trades, tape scalps, low floats, breaking news and other short-term move-to-move trades.

Fixes trap 5: the P&L keeps moving and your mind stays sharp for the bigger trades.

Find the trap. Put a stake in the ground.

When the P&L bleeds, skip the press conference. Search your trading for these seven traps, fix the one you find, and start trading better today, not when the market comes back to you.

  • Still trading your own playbook, on your own time frame?
  • Regime checked before you size: near the high, above the 20 and 50-day?
  • A hard give-back rule on every windfall.
  • One nuance missing in a tough tape: three notches down. Fold bad flops.
  • Short-term trades in the playbook to keep cash flowing.
  • Cold setup: cut size 50% or more. Never desperation size.
  • Wouldn't hold the stock through the level? Don't hold the option.
