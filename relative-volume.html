Relative Volume

Price tells you what happened. RVOL tells you how much participation is behind it. The question isn't whether a stock has volume. It's whether it's doing abnormal volume compared to itself, and whether that participation is increasing, holding, fading or never showing up.

What is relative volume (RVOL)?

Relative volume (RVOL) is a stock's volume so far today divided by its normal volume by the same time of day. 1.0x is a normal day. If a stock usually trades 1 million shares by now and has traded 5 million, it's at 5x. It tells you whether participation is abnormal compared to the stock itself, which total volume alone can't.

What RVOL actually tells you

Current volume compared to normal volume. That's all it is, and it's why total volume on its own can mislead you.

Abnormal compared to itself

1

RVOL = volume now ÷ normal volume by now

  • If a stock usually trades 1 million shares by this point in the day and today it has traded 5 million, it's doing five times normal volume.
  • 10 million shares sounds active. If the stock normally trades 50 million, it isn't special.
  • 2 million sounds quiet. If the stock normally trades 200,000, that's a massive change.

You don't just want to know if a stock has volume. You want to know if it has unusual volume, because unusual volume usually means something changed.

2

No RVOL, no trust

  • You don't need to know every detail right away. You need to know the stock is not trading like a normal day.
  • That's the first filter. If a stock is moving with no relative volume, be suspicious already.
  • If nobody is really participating, why should you trust the move?

Price can break a level on weak participation and mean nothing. The same break with RVOL expanding has attention, liquidity and other traders watching it.

What unusual volume usually means

You don't need the reason yet. RVOL tells you there is one.

A catalyst

News, or an earnings reaction the market actually cares about.

A theme

A sector in play, pulling every name in it into focus.

A crowd

A technical breakout getting attention, institutions involved, day traders piling in.

Why RVOL makes breakouts cleaner

RVOL isn't just for finding active stocks. It changes how well technical analysis works on the stock you're watching.

3

Levels only matter if people are watching them

  • If nobody is watching a stock, a beautiful breakout line, a clean VWAP reclaim or a prior day high can mean nothing. No attention, no liquidity, no crowd reacting to the level.
  • When RVOL is high, the stock becomes a focused name. A lot of traders draw the same premarket high, prior day high, VWAP, opening range and breakout level.
  • So when price gets there, the reaction is more predictable, because more people are involved.

It doesn't mean every breakout works. It means the setup has a much better chance of behaving like a setup.

4

Stocks in play

  • A stock in play isn't just a stock that moved. It has attention, range, volume and a reason people are involved. RVOL helps confirm that.
  • Opening drive, VWAP continuation, day three breakout, fashionably late: each needs enough participation for the trade to matter.

Without it, you're probably just trading noise.

The cumulative RVOL histogram

Not one number for the day. At every bar, today's volume so far against what's normal by that same time of day.

How the histogram is built

5

Same time of day, every bar

  • If it's 10:15, compare today's volume up to 10:15 with what's normal for that stock up to 10:15.
  • That's what makes it cumulative: a running total against a running average, bar by bar, so the open's heavy volume never flatters the rest of the day.
  • The top histogram compares today to the 20-day average. The bottom compares today to the 5-day average.

It isn't used to predict the future. It's used to ask a better question: is this move being supported by abnormal participation?

Is the stock doing abnormal volume compared to itself, and is that participation increasing, holding, fading or non-existent?

Four readings

The histogram answers one question at a glance. Dashed line = 1.0, normal for the time of day.

Increasing

Participation building into the move. What you want on a breakout.

Holding

Steady above 1 after the break, still there on the pullback.

Fading

Price moving while participation drains away. Trust it less.

Never shows up

Under 1 all along. A move in an empty room.

Reading the colors

The same colors as the indicator below.

Grey · under 1.0

Less volume than normal for this time of day.

Blue · 1.0 to 2.0

Above normal. Something is drawing attention.

Green · 2.0 and up

Strong participation. Faded columns mean it's falling.

How to use it in your process

RVOL doesn't replace price action. It confirms or questions it.

7

Before you take a breakout, ask

  • Is this stock in play? Is today's volume abnormal?
  • Is participation increasing into the move?
  • Is RVOL holding after the move? Is the stock still trading actively on the pullback?
  • Or is price moving while participation fades?

Those questions decide how much you trust the setup.

8

High RVOL is not a buy signal

  • It means pay attention: the stock has range, liquidity and attention. You still need your actual trade.
  • That might be an opening drive through the premarket high, a VWAP continuation, a fashionably late or an opening range break.

RVOL tells you whether the stock deserves your attention in the first place. The setup tells you where to trade it.

Four ways to misread volume

Each one looks like reading volume. Each one gets you into the wrong trades.

1  Trusting total volume

10 million shares on a stock that normally does 50 million is a quiet day. Measure the stock against itself.

Kills: your sense of which names are actually in play.

2  Buying because RVOL is high

Monster RVOL says pay attention, not buy here. Without a setup it's just a busy chart.

Kills: entries with no level and no stop.

3  Chasing a break under 1.0

Price clears the level while RVOL sits below normal. Nobody showed up, and the breakout fails.

Kills: the fake breakout you didn't need to take.

4  Reading one number

2x at the open that's draining to 1.1x isn't the same as 1.8x holding. Read the shape: increasing, holding, fading.

Kills: moves you trusted after the crowd left.

We tested it RVOL filter: no help

An RVOL filter, tested on a setup

We required cumulative RVOL of at least 1.0 at the signal on the Zero Lag Pullback (on top of a market-regime filter). It made the result worse, on a small sample.

Questions

Why not just look at total volume?

10 million shares isn't special for a stock that normally trades 50 million (0.2x), while 2 million is a massive change for one that normally trades 200,000 (10x). Most shares traded isn't the same as most unusual.

Why does RVOL make breakouts cleaner?

On a high-RVOL name, many traders are watching the same premarket high, prior day high, VWAP and opening range, so the reaction at those levels is more predictable. It doesn't mean every breakout works.

What is cumulative RVOL?

At every bar, today's volume so far compared with what's normal by that same time of day, using a 20-day and a 5-day baseline, rather than one number for the whole day.

Does an RVOL filter improve a setup?

Not in our one test. Requiring RVOL of at least 1.0 on the Zero Lag Pullback made it worse (-0.18R against -0.02R), but on only 44 trades, and the test couldn't pick stocks in play the way RVOL is meant to.

Price tells you direction. RVOL tells you participation.

Participation is what makes a move trustworthy. The best setups don't happen in dead stocks: they happen when volume, range, catalyst and attention all come together. That's where to be looking.

  • A breakout with increasing RVOL is very different from a breakout with no volume behind it.
  • A pullback with RVOL holding is very different from one where participation disappears.
  • A catalyst with monster RVOL is very different from a headline nobody cares about.
  • It won't make every trade work. It will keep you on better stocks and out of weak breakouts.
